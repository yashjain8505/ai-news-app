**Hadrian raised $40 million in a Series B round**, announced October 6, 2026 and led by **Forgepoint Capital International and Smartfin**. The Amsterdam-based company uses autonomous AI agents to simulate cyberattacks against its customers' own systems, finding vulnerabilities before real attackers do.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why offensive security is becoming an AI-native category of its own.

## What is Hadrian?

Hadrian is an **AI-powered offensive security platform** based in Amsterdam. It uses two autonomous agents, named **Atlas and Nova**, to simulate real-world attacks against customer systems and surface vulnerabilities before malicious actors can exploit them. Founded in **2021** by **Rogier Fischer, Olivier Beg and Maurice Clin**, the company sells to large enterprises with complex, high-stakes infrastructure.

Its customer base already includes **McKesson, NBCUniversal, TotalEnergies, Amadeus, Leroy Merlin and Damen Shipyards** — a mix of healthcare, media, energy, travel, retail and industrial companies.

## The raise: $40M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $40 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** October 6, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Forgepoint Capital International, Smartfin
- **Co-leads:** HV Capital, Motive Partners, Picus Capital, Oetker Ventures

Hadrian's funding history shows a steady step-up: a **€2.5 million pre-seed in 2021**, followed by a **€10.5 million seed in 2022**, and now a $40 million Series B — roughly a 4x jump from its seed round as the company moved from early product validation to enterprise-scale deployment.

## Who invested in Hadrian?

The round was led by **Forgepoint Capital International** and **Smartfin**, with **HV Capital**, **Motive Partners**, **Picus Capital** and **Oetker Ventures** co-leading — a broad syndicate of European and cybersecurity-focused investors backing the company's international expansion.

## What Hadrian will do with the money

Hadrian plans to use the funding to **expand into Europe, the Middle East, Africa and the United States**, while hiring additional engineers and researchers to build out its agent platform.

That expansion is backed by strong customer results: Hadrian says its customers discover **10x more critical risks** and fix problems **80% faster** compared with manual security testing — the kind of efficiency gain that makes a compelling case for enterprise security budgets shifting toward autonomous tools.

## Why it matters

1. **AI agents are reshaping both sides of cybersecurity.** As enterprises deploy more AI agents into critical systems, the attack surface grows — and Hadrian is positioning itself as the defensive counterpart, using agents to find weaknesses before attackers do. That dual dynamic is a recurring theme across [AI defense tech funding in 2026](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026).
2. **Manual penetration testing is losing ground to continuous AI testing.** An 80% speed improvement over manual testing isn't a marginal gain — it suggests traditional, periodic security audits are being displaced by always-on autonomous scanning as the default enterprise approach.
3. **European AI security startups are attracting serious international capital.** A Series B with investors spanning the Netherlands, Germany, the US and beyond shows that offensive security AI isn't a niche regional bet — it's drawing the same global capital pools chasing [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
