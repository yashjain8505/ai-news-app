**Adobe Photoshop with Firefly AI is the best AI photo editor overall for professionals, but it's the wrong pick if you just need to clean up a product shot or a phone photo — Photoroom, Canva, and Google Magic Editor each do a narrower job faster and cheaper.** Here's how the field breaks down by what you're actually trying to do.

## The short answer

If you need layered, professional-grade editing — generative fill, object removal, full creative control — **Adobe Photoshop** with Firefly AI is still the reference point, backed by a 4.6/5 rating across more than 13,000 G2 reviews, though it's subscription-only. If your work is mostly landscapes and portraits, **Luminar Neo** gets you most of the way there for less money and less of a learning curve, thanks to its SkyAI replacement and GenErase object removal. **Canva** is the easiest entry point for anyone without editing experience, with a usable free plan and Magic Edit/Magic Grab tools built for social content. If your photos are blurry, noisy, or low-resolution, **Topaz Photo AI** is a dedicated fix — it bundles denoising and sharpening into one pass rather than asking you to fake it in a general editor. Selling products online points you to **Photoroom**, which is built specifically around background removal, realistic shadows, and ghost mannequin mode. And if you just want free, no-install touch-ups on a phone photo, **Google Magic Editor** covers the basics — erasing objects and swapping skies or backgrounds — at no cost.

## Adobe Photoshop (with Firefly AI)

Photoshop remains the professional standard, and the numbers back it up: a 4.6/5 rating across more than 13,000 reviews on G2. Firefly AI adds generative fill and object removal on top of Photoshop's existing layered editing toolkit, so you can extend backgrounds, remove distracting elements, or generate new content directly inside a file you're already building in layers. It's subscription-only, with no meaningful free tier for real work.

**Pros:** unmatched depth for complex, multi-layer edits; generative fill and object removal are genuinely fast compared to manual retouching; the industry-standard skillset if you ever need to hand a file to another editor.
**Cons:** subscription-only pricing; steep learning curve compared to every other tool on this list.
**Who it's for:** professional photographers, retouchers, and designers who need full creative control, not just quick fixes.

## Luminar Neo

Luminar Neo is aimed squarely at landscape and portrait work, with two AI-driven tools doing most of the heavy lifting: SkyAI, which replaces and relights skies convincingly, and GenErase, which removes unwanted objects from a scene. It's positioned as cheaper and easier to learn than Photoshop, trading some of Photoshop's flexibility for a more guided, single-purpose workflow.

**Pros:** purpose-built sky and portrait tools that are faster to use than Photoshop's general-purpose equivalents; gentler learning curve.
**Cons:** narrower scope than Photoshop — not built for complex compositing or multi-layer commercial work.
**Who it's for:** landscape and portrait photographers who want AI-assisted results without Photoshop's complexity or cost.

## Canva

Canva is the most approachable tool here, and it's the only one on this list with a genuinely usable free plan. Magic Edit and Magic Grab let you make targeted changes — swap an object, pull an element out of a photo — without touching layers or masks, which is exactly the level most social media creators need.

**Pros:** free plan available; fastest tool to learn on this list; built for the kind of quick, single-image edits social posting requires.
**Cons:** not designed for deep, professional-grade retouching or complex composites.
**Who it's for:** social media creators and beginners who want results in minutes, not hours.

## Topaz Photo AI

Topaz Photo AI solves a specific problem: photos that are blurry, noisy, or otherwise degraded. It bundles what used to be two separate products — Denoise AI and Sharpen AI — into one pass, so a single run can clean up noise and recover sharpness in the same photo rather than requiring two separate tools or manual masking in a general editor.

**Pros:** genuinely strong at rescuing photos other editors can't fix cleanly; combines denoising and sharpening in one step.
**Cons:** a specialist tool, not a general editor — it won't replace Photoshop or Canva for compositing or creative edits.
**Who it's for:** anyone restoring old, low-light, or otherwise low-quality photos where clarity is the main issue.

## Photoroom

Photoroom is built around one job: making product photos look professional for e-commerce. Background removal is the core feature, but the AI shadow tool and ghost mannequin mode — which removes a mannequin from an apparel shot while keeping the garment looking naturally worn — are what separate it from a generic background eraser.

**Pros:** purpose-built for product photography; ghost mannequin mode is a real time-saver for apparel sellers; realistic AI-generated shadows.
**Cons:** narrow use case — not built for portraits, landscapes, or general creative editing.
**Who it's for:** online sellers and small e-commerce operations that need consistent, professional product shots without hiring a photo studio.

## Google Magic Editor

Magic Editor is Google's free, mobile-first answer to quick photo fixes. Magic Eraser removes unwanted objects or people from a shot, and it can also reposition subjects, adjust lighting, and swap out skies or backgrounds — all without leaving the Google Photos app, and at no cost.

**Pros:** completely free; works directly on mobile without a separate app or account; covers the most common phone-photo annoyances.
**Cons:** less precise and less powerful than dedicated desktop tools; limited to the edits Google has built in.
**Who it's for:** casual phone photographers who want fast, free corrections without any editing skill required.

## How to choose

Start with what's actually wrong with your photo, not what tool sounds most powerful. If you're building complex, multi-layer creative work professionally, Photoshop's cost is justified by its depth. If your subject is nature or people and you want AI shortcuts without Photoshop's complexity, Luminar Neo is the better fit. If you're a beginner or posting to social media, Canva's free plan will cover you. If the problem is technical — blur or noise — Topaz Photo AI fixes that specifically, and if you're selling products online, Photoroom is worth paying for on its own. For anyone who just needs a quick, free mobile fix, Google Magic Editor is hard to beat at zero cost.
