**Fractile raised $720 million in a Series C round at a $7 billion valuation**, announced November 11, 2026 and led by **Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint**. The London-based chip startup hasn't shipped a single chip yet — that's expected in 2027 — but investors are already pricing it as a multi-billion-dollar bet on where AI's next hardware bottleneck will be.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why memory bandwidth has become such a hot target for venture capital.

## What is Fractile?

Fractile designs **specialized AI inference chips** that use in-memory compute and stacked DRAM to get around the memory-bandwidth bottleneck that slows down running large language models. Founded in **2022** by **Walter Goodwin and Yuhang Song**, the company hasn't disclosed customers yet — its first chips aren't expected to ship until 2027.

In plain terms: running a large AI model fast isn't just about how much raw compute a chip has, it's about how quickly data can move between memory and the processor. Fractile's architecture is built specifically to attack that bottleneck, rather than trying to out-compute incumbents on raw FLOPs.

## The raise: $720M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $720 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $7 billion
- **Announced:** November 11, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Redpoint

For context, Fractile's prior rounds were a **$15 million seed in July 2024** and a **$220 million Series B in May 2026** at roughly a **$1 billion valuation**. Going from a $1 billion to a $7 billion valuation in about six months — before shipping a product — is one of the steepest valuation climbs for a pre-revenue hardware startup this year.

## Who invested in Fractile?

The Series C was led by **Thrive Capital**, **Founders Fund**, **Lightspeed Venture Partners** and **Redpoint**. Additional participants included:

- **Dragoneer Investment Group**
- **DST Global**
- **BlackRock**
- **T. Rowe Price**
- **CapitalG**
- **UK Sovereign AI Fund**

The UK Sovereign AI Fund's involvement stands out: it's a government-linked investor backing a British chip startup directly, rather than funding AI research through grants or policy programs.

## What Fractile will do with the money

Fractile says the capital will go toward **shipping its first chips in 2027**, accelerating chip design, securing foundry partnerships with **TSMC and Samsung**, and hiring silicon and software talent across teams in the **UK, US and Taiwan**. Chip design and foundry access are both capital-intensive and slow, which is likely why Fractile is raising at this scale well before it has a commercial product in market.

## Why it matters

Fractile's raise is a clear marker of where chip investors are placing their bets in 2026:

1. **Memory bandwidth, not raw compute, is now the constraint.** As models get larger and inference volume grows, the bottleneck has shifted from "how many FLOPs can a chip do" to "how fast can it move data" — and Fractile is one of several startups, alongside [Etched](/blog/etched-funding), betting specifically on that shift rather than competing head-on with general-purpose GPUs.
2. **Government capital is now backing chip challengers directly.** The UK Sovereign AI Fund's participation echoes a broader pattern of national governments treating AI chip supply as strategic infrastructure, not just a venture bet — a theme also visible across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **Pre-revenue valuations are compressing the usual timeline.** A $7 billion valuation before a single chip has shipped shows how much capital is willing to front-load risk in hardware right now, betting that whoever solves the memory bottleneck first captures outsized share of AI inference spending.
