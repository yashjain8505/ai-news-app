**Gimlet Labs raised $300 million in a Series B round at a $3 billion valuation**, announced September 4, 2026 and led by **Andreessen Horowitz**. The round comes less than a year after the company emerged from stealth, and it lands squarely on one of AI infrastructure's sharpest emerging pain points: the cost of running inference at scale.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's backing it, and what it says about the inference hardware market.

## What is Gimlet Labs?

Gimlet Labs builds a **multi-silicon inference cloud** — software that intelligently distributes AI model inference across GPUs, custom accelerators, and CPUs from multiple vendors, so companies aren't locked into a single hardware provider. Founded in **2025** by **Zain Asgar**, the company emerged from stealth in October 2025 and counts customers among the **top three frontier labs and top three hyperscalers**.

In plain terms: most AI companies run inference on whatever chips they can get, often from a single vendor. Gimlet's pitch is to sit above the hardware layer and route workloads across whatever silicon is cheapest or most available at a given moment.

## The raise: $300M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $300 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $3 billion
- **Announced:** September 4, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Andreessen Horowitz
- **Co-leads:** Sapphire Ventures, Arm, Menlo Ventures, Factory

For context, Gimlet Labs raised an **$80 million Series A in March 2026**, led by Menlo Ventures — just six months before this round. Total funding now stands at **$392 million**, and the company reached a $3 billion valuation faster than most infrastructure startups manage in twice the time.

The pace is notable given how young the company is: Gimlet only emerged from stealth in October 2025. Going from stealth to an $80 million Series A to a $3 billion Series B valuation inside roughly eleven months puts it among the fastest-moving infrastructure startups of the current cycle, and reflects how urgently frontier labs and hyperscalers are looking for ways to reduce their dependence on any single chip supplier.

## Who invested in Gimlet Labs?

The Series B was led by **Andreessen Horowitz**, with **Sapphire Ventures, Arm, Menlo Ventures and Factory** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **M12**

Arm's participation stands out: as a chip architecture licensor, its interest in a company built around avoiding single-vendor hardware lock-in suggests chip players outside Nvidia are actively backing the infrastructure layer that could reduce Nvidia's grip on inference workloads. Menlo Ventures returning from the Series A also signals continued conviction from an early backer.

## What Gimlet Labs will do with the money

Gimlet Labs plans to use the funding for **expanding multi-silicon data center operations, building out orchestration software, and hiring engineering talent**.

That reflects the nature of the problem the company is solving: routing inference workloads reliably across heterogeneous hardware requires both physical capacity and sophisticated software, and scaling one without the other doesn't work. Serving customers as large as top-three frontier labs and top-three hyperscalers also means the orchestration layer has to work at a scale and reliability bar few startups are asked to meet this early.

## Why it matters

Gimlet Labs' raise reflects a few notable shifts in AI infrastructure:

1. **Inference costs now rival or exceed training costs.** As companies run millions of queries per day, the economics of inference — not just training — are driving major infrastructure decisions, a dynamic also visible in the broader [AI data center funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026) boom.
2. **Vendor diversification is becoming a real strategy, not just a hedge.** A $3 billion valuation just six months after stealth suggests investors believe companies will actively want to mix GPUs, custom accelerators, and CPUs rather than commit to one vendor, echoing the vendor-diversification bets also playing out at compute providers like [Fluidstack](/blog/fluidstack-funding).
3. **Chip vendors are hedging against Nvidia's dominance.** Arm's participation suggests hardware players see value in backing an orchestration layer that doesn't assume any single chipmaker wins the AI inference market outright.
