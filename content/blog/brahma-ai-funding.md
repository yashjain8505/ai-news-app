**Brahma AI raised $150 million at a $2 billion valuation**, announced September 24, 2026 and led by **Multiples Alternate Asset Management**. The round is a striking example of old media and new media converging in 2026 — a company built on the backbone of VFX giant Prime Focus is now selling an AI operating system for enterprise content creation to studios, brands, and sports leagues.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals.

## What is Brahma AI?

Brahma AI describes itself as an **AI-native operating system for enterprise audiovisual content creation**, offering digital humans, voice synthesis, video and audio generation, and content management tools. It's backed by the entertainment assets of **Prime Focus**, giving it a production and creative pedigree that most generative-media startups don't have.

The company was founded in **2024** by **Prabhu Narasimhan (CEO)**, **Namit Malhotra** (founder of Prime Focus), and **Thomas Graham** (former CEO of Metaphysic). Its customers already include major names: **Warner Bros**, the **NBA**, and the **Mayo Clinic**.

## The raise: $150M at a $2B valuation

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $150 million
- **Round:** Latest equity round
- **Valuation:** $2 billion
- **Announced:** September 24, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Multiples Alternate Asset Management

No prior funding details were disclosed for Brahma AI, so this round stands as the company's first publicly reported raise at scale — and a $2 billion valuation for a company founded only in 2024 reflects how much weight investors are putting on its existing entertainment-industry relationships and technology base.

## Who invested in Brahma AI?

The round was led by **Multiples Alternate Asset Management**. No co-leads or additional participants were disclosed.

## What Brahma AI will do with the money

Brahma AI plans to use the funding to **expand its product capabilities in digital humans and generative media, scale sales and customer success, and build out IP libraries**. That last point is notable — rather than just selling tools, Brahma appears to be building owned content libraries it can license or repurpose, a different model from most generative-media startups that sell software and stop there.

## Why it matters

1. **Hollywood is buying, not just watching.** With clients like Warner Bros and the NBA already on board, and a 2026 Technology Emmy recognizing its work, Brahma is evidence that major entertainment and sports organizations are moving past pilot projects and into production use of generative media.
2. **Legacy media infrastructure is becoming an AI moat.** By combining Prime Focus's production backbone with Metaphysic's synthetic media technology, Brahma is positioned differently than pure-software competitors — it owns both the technology and the creative talent needed to deliver finished content, not just a model API.
3. **Generative media funding is part of a broader 2026 surge.** Brahma's raise sits alongside a wave of large checks flowing into AI video and audiovisual startups, a trend covered in [AI video startup funding in 2026](/blog/ai-video-startup-funding-2026) and across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
