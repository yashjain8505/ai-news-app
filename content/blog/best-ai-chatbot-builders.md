**For most SMBs, Tidio ($29/month) is the safest default — it has native WhatsApp, the best WordPress rating (4.8/5), and a free tier to test with. But if WhatsApp and social channels are the whole point, ManyChat ($15-16/month) is cheaper and built for exactly that, while Boei ($19/month) bundles 50+ channels into one inbox for teams tired of juggling tools.**

The chatbot-builder market has split into two camps: general-purpose live-chat platforms that bolted on AI (Tidio, Intercom, Crisp) and AI-native tools built around a knowledge base or a specific channel (Chatbase, ManyChat, Botpress). Which camp you should buy from depends on whether you're trying to answer support questions from your own docs, or trying to sell through messaging apps.

## The short answer

If you want one tool that covers chat widget, WhatsApp, and WordPress without much setup, **Tidio** at $29/month is the default pick — it has the highest WordPress rating in the category (4.8/5) and native WhatsApp support, priced on a billable-conversation model. If your business runs on messaging apps rather than a website — Instagram DMs, SMS, Messenger — **ManyChat** at $15-16/month is purpose-built for social commerce and is the cheapest way in, with a free tier up to 1,000 contacts. If you have technical resources and want a bot trained tightly on your own documents and PDFs, **Chatbase** starts free (50 message credits) and scales to $19-150/month for knowledge-base-driven support bots. Developer teams that want full control over bot logic should look at **Botpress**, free with $5 in AI credits and $89/month at the Plus tier. For SMBs juggling five inboxes across channels, **Boei** at $19/month bundles WhatsApp, SMS, email, and web forms into one place, explicitly positioned to replace 3-5 separate tools. **Intercom** ($39-139/month per seat, plus $0.99 per resolution) is the enterprise option if you need it, but it's priced well above what most SMBs need for basic AI chat.

## Tidio

Tidio is the closest thing to a default choice for SMBs: $29/month gets native WhatsApp integration and the category's best WordPress rating, on a billable-conversation pricing model that scales with actual usage rather than seats.

**Pros:** highest-rated WordPress integration in the category; native WhatsApp without an add-on fee; straightforward 2-click install for WooCommerce stores.
**Cons:** billable-conversation pricing means costs can climb with volume in ways a flat monthly fee wouldn't; not built for social-commerce channels the way ManyChat is.
**Who it's for:** SMBs running a WordPress or WooCommerce storefront that want one chatbot covering web chat and WhatsApp without much configuration.

## ManyChat

ManyChat starts at $15-16/month — the cheapest entry point in this comparison — and is built specifically for SMS, Messenger, and Instagram, which makes it the strongest option for businesses that sell through social and messaging rather than a traditional website. Its free tier covers up to 1,000 contacts, enough to validate the approach before paying.

**Pros:** lowest starting price with native WhatsApp; purpose-built for social commerce flows (broadcasts, drip sequences, product catalogs); generous free tier.
**Cons:** less suited to knowledge-base-style support bots or WordPress-embedded chat widgets; SMB users report migrating away once they exceed ManyChat's broadcast limits (a driver cited in 42% of migrations in this category).
**Who it's for:** ecommerce and social-first businesses selling through Instagram, Messenger, or SMS rather than a website contact form.

## Chatbase

Chatbase's whole pitch is training a chatbot on your own content — upload docs and PDFs and it answers from them. The free tier includes 50 message credits to test the concept, with paid tiers from $19 to $150/month depending on volume.

**Pros:** genuinely free to start; strong fit for support-deflection use cases where answers live in existing documentation; scales cleanly from hobby to higher-volume use.
**Cons:** not designed for channel-specific commerce flows like ManyChat or Boei; the $150/month ceiling on published tiers means high-volume users need to budget for growth.
**Who it's for:** teams that already have support docs, FAQs, or PDFs and want a bot that answers from that content specifically, rather than a general sales/marketing bot.

## Botpress

Botpress is the developer-focused option: free to start with $5 in AI credits, $89/month for the Plus tier, and $10 per additional 5,000 messages beyond that. It gives the most control over bot logic of anything in this list, at the cost of needing someone comfortable building flows rather than clicking through templates.

**Pros:** most flexible logic and integration options; open-source roots mean less lock-in than fully closed platforms; pay-per-message scaling keeps costs predictable at volume.
**Cons:** steeper setup curve than Tidio or ManyChat; overkill for an SMB that just wants a WhatsApp widget live by end of day.
**Who it's for:** teams with development resources who want a chatbot that does something more custom than off-the-shelf templates support.

## Boei and Intercom

Boei, at $19/month, bundles more than 50 channels — WhatsApp, SMS, email, web forms — into a single inbox, explicitly pitched at SMBs who've been stitching together three to five separate tools to cover the same ground. It's worth a look if channel sprawl, not AI sophistication, is the actual problem. Intercom sits at the other end: $39-139/month per seat plus $0.99 per resolution, with WhatsApp as a $29 add-on rather than included. That pricing only makes sense once support volume and headcount justify an enterprise-grade platform — most SMBs will outgrow the other options on this list before they outgrow Tidio or Boei.

## How to choose

Start with the channel your customers actually use. If it's WhatsApp and your website, Tidio's $29/month covers both with the best WordPress fit. If it's Instagram, SMS, or Messenger-driven commerce, ManyChat's $15-16/month is purpose-built and cheaper. If the real problem is answering repetitive questions from your own docs, Chatbase's free tier costs nothing to test. If you have engineering resources and want full control, Botpress scales from free to $89/month without locking you into someone else's templates. And if you're already running three or more separate tools across channels, Boei's $19/month bundle may replace all of them at a lower combined cost — with WhatsApp's 90%+ open rates being the main reason SMBs are consolidating onto these platforms in the first place.
