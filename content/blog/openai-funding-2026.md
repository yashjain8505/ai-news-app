**OpenAI reportedly raised an additional $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation**, announced November 6, 2026, in a round led by **MGX** with **SoftBank** co-leading and existing investors also participating. It's a top-up rather than a full new round, arriving just eight months after OpenAI's $852 billion valuation in March — and it pushes the company into rarefied territory as one of the most valuable private companies in history.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and what it signals about the limits of AI funding at this scale.

## What is OpenAI?

OpenAI builds **frontier large language models and AI systems** for research, commercial, and consumer use. Founded in **2015** by **Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Greg Brockman**, the company's models and products are now embedded deep into enterprise software — its customers include **Microsoft, Apple, and a wide base of enterprise customers**.

## The raise: $11B growth round

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $11 billion
- **Round:** Growth funding (a top-up, not a full new priced round)
- **Valuation:** $1.25 trillion
- **Announced:** November 6, 2026
- **Lead investor:** MGX
- **Co-lead:** SoftBank
- **Participants:** Existing investors

OpenAI's previous round was $122 billion at an $852 billion valuation in March 2026, itself built on a string of earlier rounds from Sequoia Capital, Founders Fund, Andreessen Horowitz and others. Going from $852 billion to $1.25 trillion in about eight months is roughly a 47% increase — a smaller jump in percentage terms than some of OpenAI's earlier rounds, but a much larger one in absolute dollars than almost any private company has ever raised.

## Who invested in OpenAI?

The round was led by **MGX**, with **SoftBank** co-leading, and existing investors also participating alongside them.

## What OpenAI will do with the money

OpenAI says the funding goes toward **compute infrastructure, talent acquisition, model training, and data center buildout**. The company's total compute commitments now exceed **$100 billion over the coming years** — a scale of forward spending that dwarfs the funding round itself and underscores just how capital-intensive frontier model development has become.

## Why it matters

1. **OpenAI's spend is still far outpacing its revenue**, and an $11 billion top-up at a $1.25 trillion valuation tests whether investors will keep funding that gap even as enthusiasm cools elsewhere in AI. The same compute-buildout logic is driving infrastructure raises across the industry, including at companies tracked in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
2. **It sharpens the contrast with rivals pursuing public markets.** While OpenAI keeps raising enormous private rounds, competitor [Anthropic has moved toward an IPO](/blog/anthropic-ipo) — two different paths to the capital frontier labs need to keep training ever-larger models.
3. **The scale of OpenAI's compute commitments is reshaping demand for AI infrastructure broadly**, with independent providers like [Together AI](/blog/together-ai-funding) also raising at multi-billion-dollar valuations to serve the overflow demand that hyperscalers and labs like OpenAI can't fully absorb themselves.
