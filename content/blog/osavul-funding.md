**Osavul raised €8.5 million in a Series A round**, led by **33N Ventures**. No valuation was disclosed, but the raise is a notable marker of where European defense-tech capital is flowing: toward a Ukrainian team building AI to detect state-sponsored hybrid warfare.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and what the company plans to do next.

## What is Osavul?

Founded in **2022** by **Dmytro Plieshakov and Dmytro Bilash**, Osavul builds AI to **detect state-backed hybrid attacks** — campaigns that combine cyberattacks, physical sabotage, spying, and online manipulation rather than relying on any single attack vector. The platform scans public and private data for early warning signs, maps identified threats to a client's own staff and suppliers, and uses human analysts to verify what the AI flags before it's treated as a confirmed threat.

Its customer base is almost entirely institutional: **state, defense, and security bodies in more than 10 countries**, including the UK, Germany, Poland, and the US, as well as **NATO**.

## The raise: €8.5M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** €8.5 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Lead investor:** 33N Ventures

Osavul's prior raise was a **seed round of roughly €3.5 million**, and this Series A brings its **total funding to approximately €12 million**. That's a modest check size by AI-funding standards, but a meaningful jump for a company whose customer base is governments rather than enterprises — a sales cycle that rewards patience over hype.

## Who invested in Osavul?

The round was led by **33N Ventures**, a specialist cybersecurity fund. Other participants included:

- Balnord
- G+D Ventures
- 42CAP

33N Ventures' focus specifically on cybersecurity, rather than generalist AI investing, is itself a signal of how this round was underwritten — the lead investor is betting on Osavul as a security company that happens to use AI, not an AI company bolting on a security use case.

## What Osavul will do with the money

Osavul plans to use the funding to **improve its AI capabilities, grow its sales team, and enter new markets** — specifically energy, transport, ports, and banking. That expansion beyond government and defense customers suggests Osavul sees its hybrid-threat detection model applying anywhere critical infrastructure can be targeted by coordinated state-backed campaigns, not just military and intelligence settings.

## Why it matters

1. **Hybrid threats are pulling defensive capital upstream.** Election interference and coordinated disinformation have become standing government concerns, and a European cybersecurity fund backing a Ukrainian team to detect state-sponsored hybrid campaigns shows capital moving toward early detection rather than after-the-fact content moderation — part of the broader rise in [AI defense tech funding](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026).
2. **Critical infrastructure is the next expansion target.** By pushing into energy, transport, ports, and banking, Osavul is betting that hybrid-threat detection isn't just a defense-sector tool — it's infrastructure-sector insurance against the same kind of sabotage and manipulation campaigns that have already targeted government bodies.
3. **A Ukrainian-founded security company raising European capital is itself a data point.** Osavul's team and customer base reflect how directly exposed experience with state-backed hybrid warfare is translating into product — and investor appetite — in a way few other markets can replicate.
