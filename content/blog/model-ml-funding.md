**Model ML raised $250 million in a Series B round**, announced November 17, 2026 and led by **General Atlantic**, with **QED Investors** co-leading. The raise comes roughly a year after the company's $75 million Series A, as it pushes AI agents deeper into the grunt work of investment banking and consulting.

Here's the full breakdown of the round and what the company plans to do with it.

## What is Model ML?

Model ML builds **AI agents that automate repetitive tasks in investment banking and consulting**, integrating directly with the tools bankers and consultants already use — Excel, PowerPoint, and deal systems. Founded in **2023** by **Chaz Englander** and **Arnie Englander**, the company targets the kind of manual, time-intensive work traditionally handled by junior analysts: building models, formatting decks, and assembling deal materials.

## The raise: $250M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $250 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** Not disclosed
- **Announced:** November 17, 2026
- **Lead investor:** General Atlantic
- **Co-lead:** QED Investors

Model ML's **Series A was $75 million**, raised about a year earlier. A $250 million Series B roughly one year after a $75 million Series A — more than tripling the round size in the space of twelve months — reflects accelerating investor appetite for vertical AI agents aimed at high-value professional workflows, rather than a slow, incremental climb between rounds.

## Who invested in Model ML?

**General Atlantic** led the Series B, with **QED Investors** co-leading. No additional participants have been disclosed.

## What Model ML will do with the money

The company says the funding will go toward **scaling its product, expanding its sales team, and pushing its agents further into the financial workflows of investment banks and consulting firms**. That's a go-to-market-heavy use of funds — less about new R&D and more about getting the existing product in front of more banks and consulting firms, and building out the commercial team needed to close and support those deals.

## Why it matters

1. **Agentic AI is moving from infrastructure into specific business operations.** Rather than selling general-purpose model access, Model ML is targeting a specific, well-understood pain point — the junior-analyst grind in investment banking — which fits the broader pattern tracked in [AI agents funding in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
2. **Capital velocity for vertical agents is accelerating.** Tripling the round size in about a year, without yet disclosing a valuation, suggests investors are willing to move fast on vertical AI agent bets once early traction shows up in a specific high-value workflow.
3. **Professional services is becoming a real battleground for AI agents.** Model ML's focus on investment banking and consulting places it alongside the broader wave of large AI raises cataloged in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), as capital increasingly chases agents built for specific, expensive, white-collar workflows rather than general-purpose assistants.
