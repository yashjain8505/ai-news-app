**Ema raised $77 million in a Series B round**, announced September 23, 2026 and led by **Creaegis**. The raise brings Ema's total funding to $140 million and, the company says, more than quadrupled its valuation compared to its 2024 Series A — a sign that enterprises are putting real budget behind autonomous AI agents for back-office work.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the enterprise AI agents market.

## What is Ema?

Ema builds an **"AI Employee" platform** that deploys autonomous agents to handle HR, IT and Finance workflows across enterprise organizations. Founded in **2023** by **Surojit Chatterjee and Souvik Sen**, the company sells the idea that instead of bolting AI features onto existing software, enterprises can deploy agents that act more like digital employees handling entire workflows. Its customer list includes major names like **NTT DATA, Hitachi, ADP, PwC, Google, KPMG, Wipro and Microsoft**.

## The raise: $77M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $77 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** not disclosed, but more than quadrupled from the Series A
- **Announced:** September 23, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Creaegis
- **Co-leads:** Accel, Section 32 (S32), Prosus

Ema raised its Series A in 2024, and this Series B brings its total funding to **$140 million**. While the company hasn't disclosed a specific dollar valuation, the more-than-4x jump in two years tracks with the broader run-up in valuations across enterprise AI agent startups over that period.

## Who invested in Ema?

The round was led by **Creaegis**, with **Accel, Section 32 (S32) and Prosus** co-leading. No additional participants beyond the lead and co-lead group were disclosed.

## What Ema will do with the money

Ema said the funding will go toward **scaling its go-to-market organization**, with multiple senior leadership hires already completed, alongside continued **platform development**. That go-to-market push follows real commercial traction: the company points to roughly **50-fold revenue growth in two years** and **more than $150 million in bookings**.

## Why it matters

Ema's raise adds to a growing body of evidence about how enterprise AI agents are performing in the market:

1. **AI agents are starting to displace, not just augment, enterprise software and services spend.** Ema's growth in HR, IT and Finance workflows suggests companies are willing to hand off entire processes to autonomous agents rather than using AI as a feature layered on top of existing tools.
2. **Retention metrics are backing up the growth numbers.** A reported **180% net dollar retention** and roughly **80% gross margins** indicate customers aren't just trialing Ema — they're expanding usage, which is the harder signal to fake in enterprise software.
3. **The enterprise agents category is attracting the same investor enthusiasm as AI coding.** Ema's quadrupled valuation in two years mirrors the rapid markups seen elsewhere in [AI agents funding](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026), suggesting investors see a similar opportunity in automating white-collar back-office work as in automating software engineering.
