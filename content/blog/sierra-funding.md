**Sierra raised $1.75 billion in a Series F round at a $30 billion valuation**, announced November 24, 2026 and led by **Greenoaks Capital**, with **Lightspeed Venture Partners** co-leading. The valuation roughly doubles the company's worth from just six months earlier, making it one of the largest and fastest-climbing bets yet on AI agents replacing traditional customer service.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who's behind it, and why investors keep pushing Sierra's price tag higher.

## What is Sierra?

Sierra builds **AI agents that handle customer service**, answering calls and messages on behalf of enterprise companies. Rather than offering a general-purpose chatbot, Sierra sells agents designed specifically to resolve customer issues — the kind of work that used to require large human support teams.

The company was founded in **2018** by **Bret Taylor** and **Clay Bavor**. Taylor is a high-profile figure in Silicon Valley, having previously served as co-CEO of Salesforce and chairman of OpenAI's board, which has helped Sierra attract attention and capital at a scale unusual for an enterprise AI startup.

## The raise: $1.75B Series F

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $1.75 billion
- **Round:** Series F
- **Valuation:** $30 billion
- **Announced:** November 24, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Greenoaks Capital
- **Co-lead:** Lightspeed Venture Partners

Sierra's previous round was a **Series E at a $15.8 billion valuation**, closed just six months earlier in May 2026. Going from $15.8 billion to $30 billion in half a year is roughly a **90% increase** — a markup that puts Sierra among the most richly valued AI agent startups, alongside the broader wave of mega-rounds tracked across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).

## Who invested in Sierra?

The Series F was led by **Greenoaks Capital**, with **Lightspeed Venture Partners** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Benchmark**
- **Sequoia Capital**

Benchmark and Sequoia are both established venture firms with long track records in enterprise software, suggesting Sierra's existing institutional backers are continuing to build their positions alongside the new lead.

## What Sierra will do with the money

Sierra says the financing will go toward **scaling customer acquisition and product development**. For a company selling AI agents into large enterprises, that typically means expanding the sales and go-to-market team to land new accounts, while continuing to build out the agent platform itself to handle a growing range of customer service scenarios.

## Why it matters

1. **Agent applications are commanding premium valuations.** Sierra's jump to $30 billion signals that investors are willing to price specialized AI agent companies well above typical enterprise software multiples, betting on agents displacing large swaths of human support labor — a dynamic also visible in [LangChain's rise to a $3 billion valuation](/blog/langchain-funding) for the infrastructure that underpins these agents.
2. **Revenue, not just hype, is driving the markup.** Sierra reportedly handles more than $400 million in annualized revenue, giving the valuation at least some grounding in real usage rather than pure speculation — though a 90% valuation increase in six months still raises the question of whether AI valuations are outrunning revenue growth.
3. **The agent funding wave keeps compounding.** Sierra's raise follows a string of large agent-focused rounds in 2026, reinforcing that investors see customer-facing AI agents as one of the clearest near-term paths to AI monetization, alongside infrastructure plays like [Baseten's inference platform](/blog/baseten-funding).
