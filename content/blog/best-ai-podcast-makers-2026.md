**If you just want to hear a document summarized as a conversation, Google's NotebookLM Audio Overview is free and the fastest way to test the category. If you're building a branded show with multiple voices and music, Wondercraft ($25–$60/month) is purpose-built for that. If narration realism or editing precision matters more than production features, ElevenLabs and Descript are the stronger picks.** These five tools don't compete head-to-head so much as split the category by what "making a podcast" means to you — summarizing content, producing a branded show, or editing recorded audio.

Pricing spans free to $60/month, and several tools offer usable free tiers, so testing before committing costs little beyond time.

## The short answer

For zero-cost experimentation, **NotebookLM** is the obvious starting point — it's free, ties to your existing Google account, and turns uploaded documents into a dual-host conversational summary with no setup. If you're producing an actual branded podcast with consistent hosts, sound effects, and music, **Wondercraft** is built specifically for that at $25–$60/month. If narration quality is the priority — the voice needs to sound genuinely human, not just competent — **ElevenLabs** leads on realism, with a free tier plus $5–$99/month paid options. If your workflow is more about editing existing recordings than generating new ones, **Descript**'s text-based editing and Overdub voice cloning ($16–$50/month) is the strongest fit. And if you want to turn existing content — URLs, PDFs, YouTube videos — into podcast episodes cheaply, **Monica AI** at $8.30–$16.60/month (or free, limited) covers that specific use case.

## NotebookLM

NotebookLM's Audio Overview feature takes whatever documents you upload and generates a conversational, dual-host summary — no editing, no voice selection, no setup beyond having a Google account. It's free, which makes it the lowest-friction way to see what AI-generated podcast audio actually sounds like before spending money elsewhere.

**Pros:** completely free; zero setup; genuinely useful for turning dense documents into a listenable summary.
**Cons:** no customization of hosts, voices, or format; not designed for publishing a branded, ongoing show.
**Who it's for:** anyone who wants to consume their own documents as audio, or test the category before paying for anything.

## Wondercraft

Wondercraft is built around producing an actual podcast, not just generating audio from text. It supports multi-voice narration, voice cloning, and music and sound effects, at $25–$60/month depending on tier — the kind of feature set you need if you're publishing a recurring branded show rather than a one-off summary.

**Pros:** most complete production toolkit here — voices, cloning, and music in one platform; built for ongoing branded content, not single episodes.
**Cons:** priced meaningfully higher than Monica AI or Descript's entry tiers; overkill if you just need occasional audio summaries.
**Who it's for:** creators or companies producing a recurring, branded podcast who need more than a single AI voice reading a script.

## ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs' focus is narration quality — its voice cloning and text-to-speech are widely regarded as some of the most realistic available, with a free tier to test it and paid tiers from $5 to $99/month depending on usage volume.

**Pros:** best-in-class narration realism; free tier available for testing; wide price range means it can scale from hobbyist to heavy production use.
**Cons:** it's a voice/narration engine more than a full podcast production tool — no built-in music or multi-voice show structure like Wondercraft.
**Who it's for:** anyone where voice quality is the deciding factor, regardless of whether the rest of the production happens elsewhere.

## Descript

Descript's Overdub feature adds voice cloning to what's fundamentally a text-based audio and video editor — you edit the transcript, and the audio updates to match, at $16–$50/month. It's less about generating a podcast from scratch and more about editing one efficiently once you have raw recordings.

**Pros:** editing workflow (edit text, audio follows) is faster than traditional waveform editing; Overdub handles corrections or re-recording without a new take.
**Cons:** not primarily a generation tool — you need source recordings to start with, unlike NotebookLM or Monica AI which generate from documents.
**Who it's for:** podcasters who record real conversations and want to edit and clean them up faster, not generate audio from text alone.

## Monica AI

Monica AI's angle is conversion: feed it a URL, PDF, note, or YouTube video, and it turns that content into a podcast episode. It has a limited free tier and paid plans from $8.30 to $16.60/month, making it one of the cheaper paid options in this category.

**Pros:** cheapest paid tier here; flexible input formats (URLs, PDFs, notes, video); free tier available to test.
**Cons:** narrower feature set than Wondercraft's full production suite; not built for editing existing recorded audio.
**Who it's for:** anyone who wants to repurpose existing written or video content into audio cheaply and repeatedly.

## How to choose

Start with what you're actually trying to produce. If you just want to listen to your own documents, NotebookLM's free Audio Overview solves that completely and costs nothing. If you're launching a recurring branded show, Wondercraft's $25–$60/month gets you the multi-voice, music, and consistency tools a real production needs — Monica AI's cheaper $8.30–$16.60/month tier can do a version of this but with less production polish. If the deciding factor is how human the narration sounds, test ElevenLabs' free tier before paying for anything else. And if your actual bottleneck is editing recorded conversations rather than generating new audio, Descript is the only tool on this list built for that job specifically.
