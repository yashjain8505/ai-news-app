**Vera Health raised $28.5 million in a Series A round**, announced October 27, 2026 and led by **Threshold Ventures**, with **Mantis VC and SV Angel** co-leading. The raise comes after the company quietly became one of the most-used clinical tools in the world — with a team of just 12 people.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and how Vera Health built that kind of reach with so few employees.

## What is Vera Health?

Vera Health is a **clinical search engine** for healthcare professionals. It searches more than **60 million peer-reviewed publications** and medical evidence sources to deliver **evidence-graded answers in seconds** — letting a clinician ask a medical question and get back a sourced, graded answer instead of digging through journals manually. Founded in **2024** by **Maxime Allouch and Taieb Bennani**, the platform is now used by more than **800,000 clinicians across 190 countries**, including roughly **25% of physicians in Spain and Italy** and **14% in France**.

## The raise: $28.5M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $28.5 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Announced:** October 27, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Threshold Ventures
- **Co-leads:** Mantis VC, SV Angel

The round follows a **$4 million seed** raised via Y Combinator — meaning Vera Health built its reach to 800,000+ clinicians on a relatively small amount of capital before this raise. Y Combinator "tripled down" as a participant in the Series A, alongside Gradient, a sign that early backers saw enough traction to significantly increase their stake.

## Who invested in Vera Health?

The round was led by **Threshold Ventures**, with **Mantis VC** and **SV Angel** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- Y Combinator (tripled down)
- Gradient

## What Vera Health will do with the money

Vera Health plans to use the funding to build out its **clinical evidence infrastructure**, expand into additional healthcare markets, and deepen partnerships with **medical societies and health systems**.

That roadmap reflects the nature of the product: a clinical search tool is only as valuable as the breadth and freshness of the evidence base behind it, and expanding partnerships with medical societies is how it gets access to more of that evidence and more institutional trust.

## Why it matters

1. **Vera Health is proof that healthcare AI adoption doesn't have to be slow.** Medical AI has a reputation for crawling through adoption cycles, but reaching 800,000 clinicians with a 12-person team suggests the "evidence-graded" framing — showing sources and confidence rather than just an answer — directly addresses the trust problem that's held back other clinical AI tools.
2. **Small teams can now build real infrastructure, not just demos.** A 12-person company serving a quarter of physicians in some European countries is a reminder of how far AI tooling has compressed the team size needed to build and scale serious software.
3. **This raise sits alongside a broader pattern of AI capital flowing into vertical, trust-sensitive domains**, a trend visible across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026) — medicine, like law and finance, rewards AI products that can show their work rather than just assert an answer.
