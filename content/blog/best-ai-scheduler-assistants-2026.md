**If you need to protect focus time from getting eaten by meetings, Reclaim AI ($8/user/month) is the cheapest and most targeted fix. If your team juggles a heavy task list alongside meetings, Motion ($19/user/month) automatically reprioritizes both. And if you're booking with clients or external contacts rather than managing internal time, Calendly ($10/seat/month) is still the category standard.** None of these tools are quite substitutes for each other — they optimize for different calendars and different problems, so the right pick depends on whose time you're actually managing.

Pricing across the category runs $5–$23/month, and most tools integrate with Google Calendar and Outlook, so switching costs are relatively low if your first pick doesn't fit.

## The short answer

If the problem is that meetings keep eating into deep work, **Reclaim AI** is built specifically to auto-schedule around your tasks, meetings, and breaks to protect that time, at $8/user/month. If you're managing a team with a genuinely heavy task load — not just meetings, but a real project queue — **Motion**'s $19/user/month buys automatic task prioritization and real-time rescheduling as things shift. For booking meetings with people outside your organization — clients, candidates, prospects — **Calendly** at $10/seat/month remains the default, with a free tier that covers basic 1:1 scheduling. If you're a solopreneur and $8–$19/month feels like overkill, **Trevor AI** at $5/month covers AI-estimated task duration and focus mode for a fraction of the cost. And **Akiflow** ($9.50/month) is worth a look if you want tasks, calendar, and workflows unified in one place rather than juggling separate tools.

## Reclaim AI

Reclaim AI's whole premise is that your calendar shouldn't just track meetings — it should actively defend time for tasks, habits, and breaks by auto-scheduling around your existing commitments. At $8/user/month, it's one of the cheaper options here while addressing a problem the others (aside from Clockwise) don't focus on directly.

**Pros:** cheapest option purpose-built for focus-time protection; auto-scheduling adapts as your calendar changes rather than requiring manual blocking.
**Cons:** less useful if your actual bottleneck is external client booking (that's Calendly's job) or team-wide task prioritization (Motion's).
**Who it's for:** individuals or teams whose main problem is meetings crowding out the time they need for actual work.

## Motion

Motion goes further than focus-time protection — it automatically prioritizes an entire task list alongside your calendar and reshuffles both in real time as new items come in. At $19/user/month, it's the priciest option here, reflecting that it's solving a more complex problem than simple calendar defense.

**Pros:** handles task prioritization and calendar scheduling together rather than treating them separately; real-time rescheduling adapts to a changing workload.
**Cons:** more than double Reclaim AI's price; likely more than you need if your problem is just protecting focus blocks, not managing a full task queue.
**Who it's for:** teams with genuinely heavy, shifting task loads who need scheduling and prioritization handled as one system.

## Calendly

Calendly remains the category standard for a specific job: letting external people — clients, candidates, prospects — book time with you without a back-and-forth email chain. At $10/seat/month (with a capable free tier for basic 1:1 scheduling), its AI-powered meeting routing adds smarter distribution when multiple people can take a given meeting.

**Pros:** best-known, most battle-tested tool for external booking; strong free tier covers basic 1:1 use cases at no cost.
**Cons:** not built for internal focus-time protection or task prioritization — it's a booking tool, not a productivity scheduler like Reclaim or Motion.
**Who it's for:** anyone whose scheduling problem is primarily "people outside my org need to book time with me," not internal calendar optimization.

## Trevor AI

Trevor AI is the budget option, at $5/month, aimed squarely at solopreneurs. It uses AI to estimate how long tasks will actually take and includes a focus mode, giving you a lighter version of what Reclaim AI does at meaningfully lower cost.

**Pros:** cheapest tool on this list; AI task-duration estimates help with realistic planning; low cost makes it easy to try.
**Cons:** built for individuals, not teams — no stated multi-user or team-coordination features; fewer integrations and features than the pricier tools.
**Who it's for:** solo workers who want AI-assisted scheduling without paying for team features they won't use.

## Akiflow

Akiflow's pitch is consolidation: tasks, calendar, and workflows in a single interface, at $9.50/month, for people producing high output who don't want to juggle separate apps for planning and scheduling.

**Pros:** combines multiple productivity surfaces (tasks, calendar, workflows) into one tool; priced competitively with Reclaim AI.
**Cons:** positioned for high-output individuals rather than teams; overlaps in purpose with Reclaim AI and Trevor AI, so the choice among the three comes down to which specific workflow fits best.
**Who it's for:** individuals who want one tool managing tasks and calendar together rather than switching between separate apps.

## How to choose

Identify whose time problem you're actually solving. If it's your own focus time getting fragmented by meetings, start with Reclaim AI at $8/user/month — it's the cheapest tool purpose-built for that job. If you're coordinating a team with a real task backlog that needs to interact with the calendar dynamically, Motion's $19/user/month is doing meaningfully more work and the price reflects that. If your scheduling headache is external — clients or candidates booking time with you — none of the focus-time tools solve that; Calendly is still the right call, and its free tier means you can start there at no cost. Solo users on a budget should look at Trevor AI ($5/month) or Akiflow ($9.50/month) before the pricier team-oriented tools.
