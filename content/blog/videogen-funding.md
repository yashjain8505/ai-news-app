**VideoGen raised $3.3 million in a seed round announced on September 8, 2026, led by Y Combinator with Rebel Fund co-leading — no valuation was disclosed.** Here's the full breakdown of the round, the backers, and what the AI video platform plans to do next.

## What is VideoGen?

VideoGen is an AI video creation platform that uses agents to walk users through structured workflows, producing fully editable, copyright-free videos with AI voiceovers available in more than 200 voices across more than 50 languages. Rather than a single prompt-to-video generator, the workflow-based approach gives users more control over the output at each step.

Founded in 2023 by Anton Koenig and David Grossman, VideoGen has grown to more than 5 million users across more than 190 countries — a customer base spanning individual creators up to Fortune 500 company teams.

## The raise: $3.3M Seed

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $3.3 million
- **Round:** Seed
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** September 8, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Y Combinator
- **Co-lead:** Rebel Fund

What stands out about this round isn't its size — $3.3 million is a small seed check by 2026 standards — but the timing. VideoGen bootstrapped its first year on roughly $30,000 of the founders' own internship savings before going through Y Combinator's Summer 2024 batch, and only raised outside seed capital after already reaching 5 million users. That's an unusually large amount of organic traction to build before taking on institutional money.

## Who invested in VideoGen?

Y Combinator led the round, with Rebel Fund co-leading. Additional participants included:

- Lobster Capital
- Stretford End Capital
- Mento VC
- Pioneer Fund
- Decacorn VC

The participant list is heavy on smaller, YC-adjacent funds, consistent with a founder-friendly seed round built around the company's existing YC relationship rather than a competitive, valuation-driven process.

## What VideoGen will do with the money

VideoGen says the funding will go toward team scaling and brand growth. For a company that has already reached 5 million users largely on word-of-mouth and organic growth from a two-person founding team, hiring is the more pressing constraint — turning a lean, bootstrapped operation into one that can support that user base and keep growing it.

## Why it matters

1. **Massive user traction before meaningful capital is rare.** Getting to 5 million users across 190+ countries on $30,000 of personal savings, before raising any real outside seed funding, is a strong signal of product-market fit in AI video tools — the kind of traction that usually only shows up after a company has already raised millions.
2. **AI video tools are proving demand across very different user types.** VideoGen's customer base spans individual creators to Fortune 500 teams, echoing a pattern across the sector tracked in [the 2026 AI video startup funding roundup](/blog/ai-video-startup-funding-2026), where tools built for casual creators are increasingly finding enterprise use cases too.
3. **Small seed rounds are still getting done in a mega-round year.** Amid headline-grabbing billion-dollar AI raises covered in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), VideoGen's $3.3 million round is a reminder that early-stage AI application startups with real usage can still raise on their own terms, without chasing outsized valuations.
