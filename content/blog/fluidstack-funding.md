**Fluidstack raised $1.5 billion in a Series C round at an $18 billion valuation**, announced September 11, 2026 and led by **Jane Street Capital**. The round caps a valuation run that saw the company nearly triple in just two months, as demand for GPU and data center capacity continues to outpace supply.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals about the AI infrastructure market.

## What is Fluidstack?

Fluidstack provides **large-scale GPU and data center infrastructure** for demanding AI workloads, supplying compute capacity to frontier labs and enterprises. Founded in **2017** by **Gary Wu, Jamie Cox, Peixian Wu, Cesar Maklary, Peter Francis, Reaal Khalil and Daniel Fedder**, the company is one of the older independent players in a compute market that's exploded with new entrants since the AI boom began.

In plain terms: as AI companies need more GPUs than the major cloud providers can supply on demand, Fluidstack builds and operates the data centers that fill that gap.

## The raise: $1.5B Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $1.5 billion
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $18 billion
- **Announced:** September 11, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Jane Street Capital

For context, Fluidstack was valued at **$7.5 billion in its Series B in July 2026**, itself up from roughly **$4.5 billion** earlier in the year. Going from $7.5 billion to $18 billion in two months is a jump of well over 100%, and total funding raised now exceeds **$2.6 billion**.

That means Fluidstack's valuation has roughly quadrupled since the start of 2026 alone. Founded back in 2017, well before the current AI boom, the company spent years as a comparatively quiet GPU cloud provider before its valuation started compounding at this pace — a sign of how much the market for independent compute capacity has re-rated as demand for frontier-lab-scale training and inference has surged.

## Who invested in Fluidstack?

The Series C was led by **Jane Street Capital**.

- **Jane Street Capital**

Jane Street's involvement is notable in itself: it's a quantitative trading firm, not a traditional venture investor, and its decision to lead a $1.5 billion infrastructure round suggests sophisticated capital allocators now view AI compute as a core asset class rather than a speculative bet.

## What Fluidstack will do with the money

Fluidstack says the funding will go toward **expanding compute capacity and data center operations**, and **accelerating infrastructure deployment** to keep up with customer demand.

That's consistent with the broader story here: the money isn't funding a new product or a pivot, it's funding more of the same core business — physical GPU capacity — at a pace that matches how fast demand is growing. For an infrastructure company, "accelerating deployment" typically means locking in more land, power, and hardware commitments ahead of when customer demand actually materializes, since data centers take months to build and GPUs take time to source and install.

## Why it matters

Fluidstack's round says a lot about where AI capital is flowing in 2026:

1. **Valuations for compute providers are compounding extremely fast.** Nearly tripling from $7.5 billion to $18 billion in two months outpaces almost every other segment of AI funding, and it tracks with the broader surge documented in [AI data center funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026).
2. **Non-traditional investors are entering AI infrastructure.** A quant trading firm leading a $1.5 billion infrastructure round, rather than a growth-equity fund or a hyperscaler, suggests the investor base for AI compute is broadening beyond the usual names.
3. **The compute land grab is intensifying alongside rivals.** Fluidstack's raise lands in the same window as competitors like [Nscale's $3.5 billion pre-IPO round](/blog/nscale-funding) and [Crusoe's reported $3 billion raise](/blog/crusoe-funding), underscoring how much capital is chasing the physical infrastructure underneath AI rather than the models themselves.
