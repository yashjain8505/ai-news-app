**Kindo raised $30 million in a Series B round**, announced on November 16, 2026 and led by **Crosspoint Capital and Forgepoint**. The raise targets a problem that barely existed a year ago: once a company has dozens of autonomous AI agents running against its systems, someone has to manage, secure and monitor them.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what it means for the emerging agent-governance category.

## What is Kindo?

Kindo is an **AI agent management platform** that helps companies wrangle, secure, and monitor the autonomous AI agents now deployed across their operations. As agents move from pilot projects to production systems with real access to sensitive data and workflows, Kindo positions itself as the layer that keeps that access in check.

## The raise: $30M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $30 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** November 16, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Crosspoint Capital, Forgepoint

Kindo's Series A and earlier rounds had brought its total funding to $57.6 million before this Series B, putting its cumulative raise at roughly $87.6 million. No valuation was disclosed for this round.

## Who invested in Kindo?

**Crosspoint Capital** and **Forgepoint** led the round. No co-leads or other participants were disclosed.

## What Kindo will do with the money

Kindo says the funding will go toward scaling its agent management and security capabilities, along with expanding its sales and engineering teams. The company points to 800% year-over-year revenue growth as evidence that demand for agent oversight is accelerating alongside agent adoption itself.

## Why it matters

1. **Agent governance is becoming its own enterprise security category.** Backing from security-focused investors like Crosspoint Capital and Forgepoint signals that managing autonomous agents is being treated as a distinct discipline, alongside companies like [Pluto Security](/blog/pluto-security-funding), which also raised a Series A this year to monitor AI agents inside enterprises.
2. **800% revenue growth suggests this isn't speculative demand.** That kind of growth rate points to companies already running into real problems with ungoverned agents, not hypothetical risk.
3. **As agentic AI moves from demos to production, tools sitting between agents and sensitive systems could become as standard as firewalls were for an earlier generation of software** — a dynamic also visible across the broader [AI agent funding](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) wave in 2026.
