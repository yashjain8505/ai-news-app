**Trajectory raised $45 million in a Series A round at a $300 million valuation**, announced in November 2026 and led by **Sequoia Capital**. The round comes just months after the company's seed, with the valuation nearly tripling as the startup bets that fine-tuning AI models shouldn't require an ML engineer.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why model customization is attracting this much capital this fast.

## What is Trajectory?

Trajectory is a **continual learning platform** that makes AI model post-training accessible to non-technical experts, letting organizations start with open-source models and **automatically retrain them on proprietary data without needing ML engineers**. Founded in **2026** by **Ronak Malde, Michael Elabd and Arjun Karanam**, the company is going after a specific bottleneck: there aren't enough ML engineers to go around, and most companies that want a model specialized to their own data and workflows can't afford or hire for that expertise.

Its customers include **Harvey, Rogo, Decagon, Mercor and Nvidia** — a notable list given Nvidia is also an investor.

## The raise: $45M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $45 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** $300 million
- **Announced:** November 2026
- **Lead investor:** Sequoia Capital

Trajectory's seed round closed in May or June 2026 at $15 million, led by Conviction, with Bessemer, Radical VC, BoxGroup and a notable group of angels — including Jeff Dean, Fei-Fei Li, and founders of Notion, Dropbox, Braintrust and Hugging Face — also participating. That round valued the company at $115 million post-money. Reaching a $300 million valuation roughly six months later is nearly a 3x jump for a company that's barely out of its first year.

## Who invested in Trajectory?

The Series A was led by **Sequoia Capital**. Additional participants included:

- **Nvidia**
- **Bessemer Venture Partners**
- **Bain Capital Ventures**

Bessemer carried over from the seed round, while Nvidia's participation is notable both as an investor and as a customer of the product.

## What Trajectory will do with the money

Trajectory said the funding will support efforts to **make advanced model post-training accessible to non-technical experts** and let organizations customize AI systems without relying on costly external engineering teams. In practice, that means continuing to build the tooling that lets a legal, finance or domain team — not a research scientist — take an open-source model and specialize it for their own workflows.

## Why it matters

1. **Fine-tuning is becoming a self-serve product, not a specialized service.** The scarcity of ML engineers has been a real deployment bottleneck for enterprises wanting custom models, and Trajectory's bet is that democratizing post-training opens up a much larger buyer base — similar to how [AI inference infrastructure](/blog/baseten-funding) became its own category once enough companies needed to run models in production.
2. **The valuation trajectory itself is the story.** Doubling from $115 million to $300 million in about six months, with both Nvidia and Sequoia in the cap table, signals that major strategic and venture investors view model customization as core infrastructure rather than a nice-to-have feature.
3. **It fits a broader 2026 pattern of infrastructure companies attracting outsized early valuations** on the strength of marquee customers and investor names alone — a dynamic also visible across [other AI agent and infrastructure funding](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) this year.
