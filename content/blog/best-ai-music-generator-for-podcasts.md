**Soundraw is the safest all-around pick for podcast background music — it's trained only on owned/licensed data, holds a 4.77-star rating, and tested well for long-form coherence — but Beatoven.ai is cheaper, Suno is more creative for short intros, and Soundverse AI is purpose-built for podcast workflows if you can get enterprise pricing.** None of them fully solve music for a 20-60 minute episode without artifacts, so pick based on where the track actually plays in your show.

## The short answer

For a **background bed under a full episode**, **Soundraw** is the strongest tested option — it scored 8.5/10 for a 3-minute podcast bed and is trained on licensed data only, so it carries zero copyright risk on platforms like Spotify or Apple Music. For a **short, distinctive intro or outro**, **Suno** generates more creative and unique-sounding music, though it's known to lose coherence past 90 seconds, which is exactly why it's better suited to short segments than full-episode beds. If **budget is the priority**, **Beatoven.ai** at $2.50-6/month is explicitly licensed for commercial podcast use and is the cheapest option here by a wide margin. If your show is produced at a level where you can access **Soundverse AI**, it's ranked #1 for podcast-specific workflows (intro/outro music and episode beds) — but pricing isn't public, and it reads as an enterprise-only tool rather than something a solo podcaster can just sign up for.

## Soundraw

Soundraw's premium tier runs **$16.99/month** and tested at **8.5/10 for a 3-minute podcast background bed** — the best long-form coherence result among the tools compared here. It's trained exclusively on **owned or licensed data**, which is why it carries a track record of zero copyright strikes on Spotify or Apple Music, and holds a **4.77-star rating on AppSumo**.

**Pros:** best-tested coherence for longer tracks; genuinely safe from a copyright standpoint for commercial distribution.
**Cons:** priced above Beatoven.ai and Suno's entry tier, and even Soundraw's best result was for a 3-minute clip, not a full episode.
**Who it's for:** podcasters who want a legally clean background bed and are willing to pay a bit more for tested reliability.

## Suno

Suno's **$10/month Pro tier** produces music that's more creative and distinctive than the alternatives here, which makes it a strong choice for a memorable intro or outro. The catch: it **struggles with coherence past 90 seconds**, so it's not built for a bed that has to run under a 30-minute episode. It's also worth knowing that Suno faces **active copyright lawsuits** — a July 2026 GEMA ruling and pending Sony/UMG cases — even though it retains 2 million paid subscribers and roughly $200M in ARR.

**Pros:** the most creative, unique-sounding output of the tools compared; cheap at $10/month.
**Cons:** loses coherence beyond 90 seconds, so it's a poor fit for full-episode beds; ongoing lawsuits make its commercial terms murkier for a monetized show.
**Who it's for:** podcasters who want a standout intro/outro and are comfortable with the commercial licensing being less settled than Soundraw's or Beatoven's.

## Beatoven.ai

At **$2.50-6/month**, Beatoven.ai is the cheapest tool on this list by a wide margin, and it comes with an explicit **non-exclusive perpetual license for commercial podcast use** — meaning the licensing question is answered upfront rather than something you have to dig for.

**Pros:** lowest cost here, with commercial rights spelled out clearly.
**Cons:** not benchmarked for long-form coherence the way Soundraw was — budget pricing likely trades off against production polish.
**Who it's for:** indie and budget-conscious podcasters who need clear commercial rights without paying premium prices.

## Soundverse AI

Soundverse AI is ranked **#1 for podcast workflows specifically**, built around the actual jobs podcasters need — intro and outro music, and background beds sized to an episode — rather than general-purpose music generation adapted to the use case. Pricing isn't publicly listed, which points to an **enterprise-only** model.

**Pros:** the only tool here purpose-built around podcast production workflows rather than generic music generation.
**Cons:** no public pricing makes it hard to evaluate against the others without contacting sales; likely out of reach for solo or small podcasters.
**Who it's for:** studios or larger podcast operations that can justify an enterprise sales conversation.

## How to choose

Match the tool to where the music actually sits in your episode. A **background bed under narration** needs coherence over minutes, not seconds — that's Soundraw's tested strength, and it's the safer legal bet for a monetized show. A **short intro or outro** can use something punchier and more creative, where Suno's output stands out, as long as you're comfortable with its unresolved copyright litigation. If cost is the binding constraint, Beatoven.ai gives you commercial rights without the premium price. And if you're producing at a scale that justifies it, Soundverse AI is worth an enterprise inquiry given it's the only tool built around the podcast workflow end to end. Worth noting: **none of the current leaders fully solve coherence for a 20+ minute track** without repetition or key-change artifacts — if your show needs a full-length original score rather than a short loopable bed, expect to still do manual editing.
