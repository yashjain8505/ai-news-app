**Choose Suno if you're writing vocal-driven songs and want the most natural-sounding AI voice in the category. Choose Freebeat if you need background or production music with commercial licensing built for business use.** They're not really competing for the same job — Suno is the creative songwriting tool, Freebeat is the commercial production tool.

## The short answer

Suno's whole reputation is built on vocal quality: v5.5 produces the most natural-sounding AI vocals available, which makes it the default choice for anyone writing full songs — lyrics, melody, and a voice that sounds like it's actually singing rather than a synthesized approximation. Freebeat takes a different angle, optimizing for background and production music with licensing terms built for commercial use, which matters more to teams making content (ads, videos, product demos) than to someone trying to write a song. If your goal is "make a song," Suno is the tool. If your goal is "get clean, licensable music behind a video," Freebeat's focus fits better.

## Suno

Suno v5.5 is priced on a consumption model: **free tier** gives you 10 songs a day, **Pro** is $10/month, and **Premier** is $30/month with 2,000+ credits monthly. The free tier alone is generous enough to write and iterate on songs regularly without paying anything, though — as with most AI music tools — commercial use requires a paid tier.

**Pros:** the most natural, expressive AI vocals available in this category; generous free tier for iteration; credit-based pricing scales with how much you actually use it.
**Cons:** consumption-based pricing means heavy users need to watch their credit usage rather than paying one flat rate; optimized for songs with vocals, not instrumental background tracks.
**Who it's for:** songwriters, musicians, and creators making full vocal tracks who want the highest vocal quality on the market.

## Freebeat

Freebeat is a newer, more specialized entrant focused on background music and production rather than vocal songwriting, with subscription pricing built around different use cases. Its differentiator against Suno isn't audio quality in the abstract — it's licensing clarity for commercial background use, which is exactly what teams making marketing or video content need and don't always get cleanly from tools built primarily for creative songwriting.

**Pros:** built specifically for commercial background-music licensing, which reduces ambiguity for business use; competitively priced against Suno for regular use.
**Cons:** as an emerging tool, it doesn't have Suno's track record or the vocal quality that makes Suno the pick for actual songs; less documented feature depth than more established competitors.
**Who it's for:** businesses and content teams that need production/background music with clean commercial rights, not vocal-forward songs.

## Pricing models: consumption vs. subscription

The two tools also differ in how they charge, which matters as much as the headline price. Suno runs on a **consumption-based credit model** — you pay for a tier that includes a set number of credits per month (or per day on the free tier), and heavy usage within a billing period can bump you toward the next tier faster than a flat subscription would. That scales naturally with how much music you're actually generating, but it also means a burst month of heavy songwriting can feel more expensive than the sticker price suggests. Freebeat, by contrast, runs **subscription-based plans** tied to different use cases rather than a shared credit pool — a structure that's easier to budget against if your usage is fairly steady month to month, since you're not tracking a depleting credit balance.

Neither model is objectively better; it depends on how predictable your usage is. A songwriter who works in bursts — heavy writing sessions followed by quiet weeks — may find Suno's credit model matches actual usage more efficiently than a flat subscription would. A content team generating a consistent volume of background tracks every month is better served by Freebeat's steadier subscription structure, since it doesn't require monitoring a credit balance mid-project.

## Where AIVA and ElevenLabs Music fit

Neither Suno nor Freebeat is the right fit for every use case. If you need **orchestral or cinematic scoring** — think film, game, or trailer music — AIVA (€11-49/month) specializes in that register and its Pro tier includes full copyright ownership, which matters if you need to prove you own the rights outright rather than just a license to use the track. If budget is the deciding factor and you want one flat-rate plan without thinking about credits, **ElevenLabs Music** at $9.99/month is the cheapest paid all-in-one option in the category, though it doesn't specialize the way Suno (vocals) or AIVA (orchestral) do.

## How to choose

Start with what you're actually making. If it's a song with lyrics and a voice — a track you'd release, share, or use as an actual piece of music — Suno's vocal quality makes it the clear pick, and its free tier is generous enough to test before you pay anything. If you're making content that needs music behind it — a video, an ad, a product demo — and commercial licensing clarity matters more than vocal artistry, Freebeat's focus on clean commercial rights for background music is the better fit. If your project leans cinematic or you need documented copyright ownership, look at AIVA instead of either. And if you just want one affordable subscription without managing credits, ElevenLabs Music's flat $9.99/month is worth a look before committing to either Suno or Freebeat.
