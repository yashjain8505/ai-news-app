**PixVerse raised $439 million in a Series C extension round at a valuation of more than $2 billion**, announced on July 13, 2026 and led by **Alibaba**, with **Ivy Capital** and **Mirae Asset** co-leading. It's one of the largest checks written into generative video this year, and it comes as the category shifts from novelty text-to-video clips toward something closer to interactive, navigable worlds.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI video.

## What is PixVerse?

PixVerse is an **AI-powered video generation startup** founded in **2023** by **Wang Changhu** and **Jaden Xie**. Rather than stopping at generating a single video clip from a text prompt, the company is building **interactive world models** — AI-generated scenes that users can navigate, not just watch.

The company has already built a large consumer base: **more than 150 million registered users across 177 countries**, according to the company. That kind of reach for a generative video tool is a signal that the technology has moved well past early-adopter novelty, and having users spread across 177 countries suggests demand isn't concentrated in one market or language but is genuinely global.

## The raise: $439M Series C Extension

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $439 million
- **Round:** Series C Extension
- **Valuation:** $2 billion+
- **Announced:** July 13, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Alibaba
- **Co-leads:** Ivy Capital, Mirae Asset

This round was structured as an extension of PixVerse's existing Series C rather than a fresh priced round, following earlier rounds the company had already raised. Alibaba leading the extension is notable in itself — it puts one of China's largest tech companies directly behind a consumer-facing generative video startup at a moment when competition in AI video is intensifying globally, with well-funded rivals racing to ship better models and grab consumer mindshare at the same time.

Extensions like this one typically happen when existing investors, or new strategic backers, want in on a round before the company goes back to market for a fresh priced round — a sign that demand for the deal outpaced what the original round size allowed.

## Who invested in PixVerse?

The round was led by **Alibaba**, with **Ivy Capital** and **Mirae Asset** co-leading the extension.

- **Alibaba** (lead)
- **Ivy Capital** (co-lead)
- **Mirae Asset** (co-lead)

## What PixVerse will do with the money

PixVerse says the funding will go toward launching its **new V-Series video model**, developing its **next-generation world model**, and expanding into **interactive entertainment** more broadly — pushing the product beyond single-clip generation and toward persistent, explorable environments.

That roadmap tracks with where the company's user base already sits: a platform with 150 million registered users and, per the company, 15 million monthly actives has the scale to justify investment in a more ambitious, interactive product rather than incremental improvements to clip generation. Moving from generating a video to generating a navigable world is a much harder technical problem — it requires consistency across frames and viewpoints, not just a convincing single shot — which is likely why the round size needed to grow via an extension rather than staying at the original target.

## Why it matters

PixVerse's raise is a useful marker for where AI video funding is heading in 2026:

1. **Generative video is consolidating around scaled consumer platforms.** Deep-pocketed backers like Alibaba are placing large bets on teams that have already turned viral traction into a durable, global user base — not just promising technology.
2. **The product bar is shifting from clips to worlds.** PixVerse's stated focus on "interactive world models" rather than single video outputs suggests the next competitive battleground in AI video is navigability and persistence, not just generation quality.
3. **This is part of a broader wave of AI video capital.** PixVerse's round sits alongside a stack of large 2026 raises tracked in Wortins' [AI video startup funding roundup](/blog/ai-video-startup-funding-2026), underscoring how much capital is chasing the category this year — and it's one of the larger single checks written into any AI startup so far in 2026, a point covered in Wortins' [biggest AI funding rounds roundup](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).

For a startup only three years old, a $2 billion-plus valuation backed by one of Asia's largest tech conglomerates is a strong statement about how much value investors are already assigning to consumer AI video, even before the "interactive world model" vision is fully built out.
