**Feathery raised $30 million in a Series A round**, announced on July 17, 2026 and led by **Portage Ventures** and **Index Ventures**. The round lands as a growing share of AI investment shifts toward agents that can operate inside regulated, high-stakes industries rather than just general-purpose consumer tools.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI in financial services.

## What is Feathery?

Feathery is an **AI operating system and decisioning platform for financial services**, built to automate high-stakes workflows in **insurance and wealth management**. Rather than replacing human judgment outright, the platform is designed to keep decisioning automated where possible while preserving oversight for the moments that require it.

The company already has meaningful traction in a notoriously conservative sector: **more than 300 firms** across insurance and wealth management use its platform, according to the company.

## The raise: $30M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $30 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** July 17, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Portage Ventures, Index Ventures

Neither Feathery's founding year nor its prior funding history was disclosed alongside this round, and the company did not share a valuation figure. That's not unusual for a Series A — many companies at this stage keep valuation details private, preferring to let the size of the round and the caliber of investors speak for the business instead.

## Who invested in Feathery?

The Series A was led by **Portage Ventures** and **Index Ventures**. Additional participants included:

- **Allstate Ventures**
- **Clocktower Technology**
- **Bain Capital Ventures**

Allstate Ventures' participation is notable given Feathery's focus on insurance workflows specifically — it's a strategic investor with a direct stake in whether AI decisioning tools actually hold up in that industry. Having an insurer's venture arm in the round, alongside generalist growth investors like Bain Capital Ventures, suggests Feathery's technology has already been validated by at least one potential customer or partner rather than just pitched on a roadmap.

## What Feathery will do with the money

Feathery said the funding will go toward **expanding its AI decisioning system for financial services** and **growing its customer base** beyond the 300-plus firms it already serves. For a platform whose entire value proposition is automating decisions in industries with heavy compliance obligations, that growth almost certainly means building out more workflow coverage — handling a wider range of insurance and wealth management decisions — rather than simply adding more logos on top of the same feature set.

## Why it matters

Feathery's raise is a useful data point on where AI agent funding is concentrating in 2026:

1. **Investors are pricing "trustworthy" agentic AI at a premium.** Money is flowing toward startups that can make AI agents reliable enough for high-stakes operational decisions, not just fast or cheap ones — a harder problem than consumer chat products face, since a wrong decision in insurance underwriting or wealth management carries real financial and legal consequences.
2. **Regulated industries are becoming a real AI agent market, not just an experiment.** Feathery's focus on keeping human oversight in the loop reflects the compliance realities of insurance and wealth management — a template other regulated-industry AI startups are likely to follow, a trend covered further in Wortins' [AI agents funding roundup](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
3. **Strategic investors are showing up earlier in AI agent rounds.** Allstate Ventures joining at the Series A stage, rather than waiting for a later round once the product is proven at scale, suggests incumbents in regulated industries are moving quickly to get exposure to AI vendors before they get too expensive or get snapped up by a competitor.
