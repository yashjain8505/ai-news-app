**Comp AI raised $34 million in a Series A round led by Roo Capital and Grand Ventures**, announced on September 18, 2026. The raise follows a $3.5 million seed round and backs a company betting that compliance — traditionally a periodic, manual scramble before an audit deadline — should instead run continuously in the background, watched over by AI agents.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and what Comp AI is building.

## What is Comp AI?

Comp AI is an **enterprise platform that uses AI agents to monitor compliance continuously**, rather than relying on the periodic audits most regulated companies currently depend on. Instead of checking whether the company still meets a given standard once a quarter or once a year, Comp's agents watch for drift — early signs that a control, policy or process has slipped out of compliance — as it happens.

No founders or named customers were disclosed alongside the round. The underlying idea is a shift from compliance as a point-in-time snapshot — "were we compliant on the day of the audit?" — to compliance as an ongoing state that's monitored the same way a security team monitors for intrusions.

## The raise: $34M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $34 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** September 18, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Roo Capital, Grand Ventures

Comp AI previously raised a **$3.5 million seed round**. Going from a $3.5 million seed to a $34 million Series A is nearly a 10x jump in round size, which typically signals the company demonstrated strong early customer traction before going back out to raise. That kind of jump is well above the more modest step-up most Series A rounds represent over their seed, and it points to two investors — Roo Capital and Grand Ventures — competing to lead rather than one firm setting the terms alone.

## Who invested in Comp AI?

The round was led by **Roo Capital** and **Grand Ventures**. No additional co-leads or participants were disclosed.

## What Comp AI will do with the money

Comp AI says the funding will go toward **product development and expanding its sales organization**. That combination — building the product further while simultaneously scaling the go-to-market team — points to a company that believes it has found product-market fit and now needs to both deepen the offering and get it in front of more regulated enterprise buyers.

Selling into compliance and security teams typically means a longer, more relationship-driven sales cycle than a typical SaaS product, since buyers are trusting the vendor with visibility into sensitive internal controls. Investing specifically in the sales organization, rather than just marketing spend, reflects that reality.

## Why it matters

1. **Compliance is shifting from a periodic chore to continuous infrastructure.** Regulated companies have historically spent enormous amounts of staff time preparing for scheduled audits; Comp AI's pitch is that AI agents can make that process passive and constant instead, catching problems the moment they appear rather than months later.
2. **AI agents are moving into operational, back-office roles, not just chat.** Comp AI is part of a broader wave of companies putting autonomous agents to work on defined, high-stakes enterprise tasks — a trend Wortins tracks in the [AI agents funding roundup](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
3. **As companies deploy more AI agents themselves, they need oversight tools for those agents too.** Comp AI's continuous-monitoring model is well positioned for that dynamic: the same infrastructure built to watch for regulatory drift can extend to watching whether an organization's own AI systems are behaving as intended.
