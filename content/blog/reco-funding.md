**Reco raised $55 million in a Series B round**, announced September 29, 2026 and led by **AT&T Ventures, Forestay, and Quadrille Capital**. No valuation was disclosed for the round, but the raise lands squarely in one of 2026's fastest-growing security niches: keeping track of AI agents enterprises didn't know they had.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and what Reco is building.

## What is Reco?

Reco, led by founder **Ofer Klein**, is an **AI agent security platform**. It uses context graphs to map AI agents to the apps, people, accounts, and permissions they touch across an organization — then uses that map to discover unauthorized agents, inspect the prompts and tool calls those agents make, and control what data and systems they're allowed to access.

Reco already counts **more than 100 customers, including Fortune 100 companies**, with financial services making up roughly **40% of its annual recurring revenue**. AT&T, one of its investors, is also a customer.

## The raise: $55M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $55 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** September 29, 2026
- **Lead investors:** AT&T Ventures, Forestay, Quadrille Capital

This Series B follows a **$30 million Series B round in February 2026** and **$85 million raised in earlier rounds** — Reco says its total funding now stands at **$140 million**. Two Series B tranches within the same calendar year suggests investors wanted more exposure to the company's growth before it moved on to a priced-up Series C.

## Who invested in Reco?

The round was led by **AT&T Ventures**, **Forestay**, and **Quadrille Capital**. No additional participants were disclosed for this round.

## What Reco will do with the money

Reco says the funding goes toward **hiring, sales, partnerships, and expanding customer support** — the standard playbook for a security vendor scaling past its first wave of enterprise customers into broader market coverage.

## Why it matters

1. **Agent sprawl is becoming a real, measurable security problem.** Gartner projects the typical Fortune 500 company will run **more than 150,000 AI agents by 2028**, up from fewer than 15 in 2025. That's not a hypothetical growth curve — it's the kind of explosion that creates exactly the blind spots Reco is built to find, a theme running through the broader surge in [AI agent funding](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) this year.
2. **The threats aren't theoretical — Reco has already found them.** The company says it discovered **21,000 unknown agents** inside a single Fortune 100 customer's environment, including rogue agents tied to former employees. That's a concrete demonstration of the exposure enterprises are sitting on right now, not a future risk.
3. **Agent security is converging with the broader AI defense category.** As AI systems take on more autonomous, permissioned actions inside enterprise environments, tools like Reco's sit alongside the wider wave of [AI defense tech funding](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026) treating AI itself — not just the infrastructure around it — as the new attack surface.
