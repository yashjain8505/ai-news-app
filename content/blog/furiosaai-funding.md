**FuriosaAI raised $620 million in a Series D round at a $2.9 billion valuation**, announced October 28, 2026 and led by **Infinitum Partners and the Korea National Growth Fund**. The raise nearly quadruples the Seoul-based chipmaker's valuation from just a year earlier, as South Korea doubles down on homegrown AI silicon.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and why a national growth fund is betting on a chip startup chasing Nvidia.

## What is FuriosaAI?

FuriosaAI is a **Seoul-based AI chip startup** that designs inference accelerators — chips built specifically to run trained AI models efficiently, rather than train them. Its flagship product, the **RNGD** accelerator, is positioned as a competitor to Nvidia's inference hardware. Founded in **2017**, the company has built a manufacturing partnership with **TSMC** to produce its chips at scale.

## The raise: $620M Series D

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $620 million
- **Round:** Series D
- **Valuation:** $2.9 billion
- **Announced:** October 28, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Infinitum Partners, Korea National Growth Fund

FuriosaAI's valuation trajectory is the real headline here. The company was valued at **$735 million in 2025**, after a **$200 million Series C in 2024**. Going from $735 million to $2.9 billion in roughly a year is nearly a **4x jump** — one of the sharpest valuation climbs of any AI chip startup in 2026, and a sign that investors are pricing in a long, expensive race against Nvidia in inference hardware.

## Who invested in FuriosaAI?

The round was led by **Infinitum Partners** and the **Korea National Growth Fund**. Additional participants included:

- Jericho Capital
- Worldline Capital
- QUDA Capital
- NeoFort Holding
- Naver
- Kakao Investment
- Korea Development Bank

The presence of the Korea National Growth Fund, Korea Development Bank, Naver and Kakao Investment alongside private venture firms points to this round being as much a national industrial-policy bet as a conventional venture raise — South Korea's largest tech and financial institutions are lining up behind a domestic alternative to Nvidia.

## What FuriosaAI will do with the money

FuriosaAI says the capital will go toward **expanding production of its RNGD chip**, strengthening its software ecosystem, and accelerating development of its **third-generation accelerator**, which it's building in partnership with **Broadcom**.

That roadmap tracks the two hardest problems chip startups face: getting enough manufacturing capacity to ship at volume, and building the software stack that makes raw silicon actually usable by AI developers. The TSMC manufacturing relationship addresses the first; the software work and Broadcom partnership address the second.

## Why it matters

1. **South Korea is treating AI silicon as strategic infrastructure.** A National Growth Fund and the state-owned Korea Development Bank backing a single chip startup signals this isn't just venture capital chasing returns — it's a government-adjacent bet on reducing dependence on Nvidia, in line with the broader wave of [AI chip startup funding in 2026](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026).
2. **Inference, not training, is where the challengers are attacking.** FuriosaAI isn't trying to out-build Nvidia on training chips — it's targeting inference, the cheaper, more fragmented market where a startup has a more plausible wedge.
3. **The valuation math shows how fast capital is moving into chip alternatives.** A near-4x jump in roughly a year puts FuriosaAI among [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), even without the household-name recognition of a model maker.
