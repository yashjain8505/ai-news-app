**Gatik raised $200 million in a Series D round**, announced August 25, 2026 and led by **Qatar Investment Authority and Koch Disruptive Technologies**, with **ARK Invest and Millennium Management** also participating. Unlike robotaxi startups chasing complex urban driving, Gatik has stuck to a narrower bet — autonomous trucks running fixed, short-haul routes between warehouses and stores — and that focus is now backed by nearly a decade of paying enterprise customers.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and why Gatik's approach stands out in autonomous vehicles.

## What is Gatik?

Gatik is an **autonomous trucking company** operating self-driving vehicles for short-haul logistics and the "middle mile" — the leg of the supply chain between distribution centers and retail stores. Founded in **2019**, the company counts **Walmart, Kroger, Tyson and PepsiCo** among its customers, giving it a base of established retail and consumer-goods relationships rather than early pilots alone.

## The raise: $200M Series D

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $200 million
- **Round:** Series D
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** August 25, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Qatar Investment Authority, Koch Disruptive Technologies
- **Participants:** ARK Invest, Millennium Management

Gatik's prior funding came through Series A, B and C rounds totaling roughly $300 million, putting its cumulative funding at around $500 million with this Series D added in. No post-money valuation was disclosed for the new round.

## Who invested in Gatik?

**Qatar Investment Authority** and **Koch Disruptive Technologies** led the round, joined by:

- **ARK Invest**
- **Millennium Management**

The mix of a sovereign wealth fund, an industrial conglomerate's venture arm, and two public-markets-focused investment firms reflects the kind of later-stage, growth-capital backing typical of a startup with real commercial contracts rather than pure R&D.

## What Gatik will do with the money

Gatik said the funding will go toward **expanding to more than 100 driverless trucks** across Texas, Arizona, Arkansas and Canada.

## Why it matters

Gatik's round is a useful counterpoint to the more headline-grabbing corners of autonomous vehicles:

1. **Boring routes, real revenue.** Gatik has completed more than 85,000 deliveries at 99% on-time reliability and holds roughly $600 million in contracted revenue — figures that put it closer to operational logistics reality than venture hype, a theme explored further in our [physical AI and robotics funding roundup](/blog/physical-ai-robotics-funding-2026).
2. **Blue-chip customers are a stronger signal than demos.** Walmart, Kroger, Tyson and PepsiCo are paying logistics relationships, not pilot partnerships — evidence that autonomous middle-mile trucking is moving past the proof-of-concept stage.
3. **It's part of a broader wave of large 2026 AI rounds.** At $200 million, Gatik's Series D adds to the pattern tracked in our [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), where autonomous vehicles and physical AI are pulling in growth-stage capital alongside more traditional software bets.
