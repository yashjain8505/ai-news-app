**Pluto Security raised $42 million in a Series A round**, announced on October 13, 2026 and led by **Jump Capital**, with **Skinos Ventures and Ansa Capital** co-leading. The round lands as enterprises scramble to figure out what their employees are actually doing with AI agents — and discover they mostly don't know.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why agent governance is suddenly a funded category.

## What is Pluto Security?

Pluto Security **monitors AI agents and non-developer-built applications inside enterprises**, detects policy violations, and enables real-time intervention to stop unauthorized actions on corporate systems. As employees increasingly build their own AI-powered tools and workflows without IT oversight — so-called "vibe-coded" apps — a visibility gap has opened up that traditional security tools weren't built to cover.

Founded in **2025**, Pluto was started by **Shahar Bahat, Tal Ofer Regev and Gil Maman**. Its customers include **Oscar Health, Workato, Sixt and AlphaSense**.

## The raise: $42M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $42 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Announced:** October 13, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Jump Capital
- **Co-leads:** Skinos Ventures, Ansa Capital

Pluto Security didn't disclose a valuation for this round. For context, the company previously raised an **$8.5 million seed round** led by TLV Partners, with co-investors Ansa Capital, AlphaDrive Ventures and Skinos Ventures — the latter being Check Point co-founder Shlomo Kramer's firm. Both Ansa Capital and Skinos Ventures returned for the Series A.

## Who invested in Pluto Security?

The round was led by **Jump Capital**, with **Skinos Ventures** and **Ansa Capital** co-leading. **AlphaDrive Ventures** also participated, having previously backed the company's seed round.

## What Pluto Security will do with the money

Pluto plans to use the funding to **expand its enterprise customer base, scale its agent governance platform features, and build out threat detection capabilities** for the rapidly growing install base of unauthorized AI tools inside companies.

The growth numbers behind that plan are notable: Pluto says it hit **$2 million in ARR and 30-40+ customers within six months of launch** in April 2026, with **60% of detected risks coming from non-developer-built apps** — exactly the blind spot the company was built to cover.

## Why it matters

1. **AI agent adoption has outpaced enterprise governance.** As companies let employees build and deploy AI agents without centralized oversight, a visibility gap has opened that traditional security tooling — built for code written by developers — doesn't address, a theme that runs through much of the [AI agents funding wave of 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
2. **Founder pedigree signals this is being taken seriously at the top of the security industry.** Pluto's team includes alumni from Microsoft security and Palo Alto Networks R&D leadership, suggesting experienced security operators see agent governance as a durable, not fad-driven, category.
3. **Six-month revenue traction validates urgency, not just hype.** Hitting $2M ARR and dozens of customers within half a year of launch — rather than years — suggests enterprises are actively seeking this kind of tooling right now, not waiting to see if the problem is real, a pattern also visible in adjacent [AI defense tech funding](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026).
