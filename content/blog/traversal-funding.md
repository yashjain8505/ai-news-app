**Traversal raised $120 million in a Series B round at a $1 billion valuation**, announced in November 2026 and led by **Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint Ventures and Khosla Ventures**. The round crosses the unicorn line less than six months after the company's Series A, as enterprises lean harder on AI agents to keep their own AI-built software running.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why a debugging startup just hit a ten-figure valuation.

## What is Traversal?

Traversal builds **AI agents that diagnose software failures and automate incident response**. Founded in **2025** by **Anish Agarwal, Raj Agrawal, Raaz Dwivedi and Ahmed Lone**, the company sells into engineering organizations that are shipping more code than ever — much of it AI-generated — and need something faster than a human on-call engineer to figure out why a system just broke.

Its customers include **Nubank, Harvey, DigitalOcean, PepsiCo, American Express and Blackstone** — a mix of fintech, legal tech and enterprise infrastructure companies, all of which run complex production systems where downtime is expensive.

## The raise: $120M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $120 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $1 billion
- **Announced:** November 2026
- **Lead investors:** Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint Ventures, Khosla Ventures

Traversal's Series A was $48 million, closed in June 2025 and led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins, with Amex Ventures joining in December 2025. Going from that round to a $1 billion valuation in roughly a year and a half is a sharp climb, and it tracks with the company's own claim of growing ARR 9x year-to-date.

## Who invested in Traversal?

The Series B was led by **Kleiner Perkins**, **Redpoint Ventures** and **Khosla Ventures**. Additional participants included:

- **Sequoia Capital**
- **Battery Ventures**
- **Amex Ventures**

Sequoia and Amex Ventures both carried over from the Series A, a sign of continued conviction from existing backers rather than a full investor turnover between rounds.

## What Traversal will do with the money

Traversal said the funding will go toward **hiring across research, engineering and go-to-market teams**. For a company whose core product is an AI agent that has to understand distributed systems well enough to find root causes faster than a human, that hiring is mostly about building out the research bench that keeps the underlying models sharp, alongside the sales motion needed to land more enterprise accounts.

## Why it matters

1. **AI is creating its own debugging problem.** As more production code is written or touched by AI agents, the volume and novelty of failures engineering teams face is growing — and Traversal is positioned squarely at that bottleneck, similar to how [infrastructure plays like Baseten](/blog/baseten-funding) are capturing demand created by the broader shift to AI-native software.
2. **Enterprise validation is real, not theoretical.** Customers like American Express, Nubank and Blackstone are not experimenting with toy workloads — they're running mission-critical systems, and their willingness to hand incident response to an AI agent suggests the category has moved past early pilots.
3. **A unicorn valuation at Series B, from a 2025-founded company, shows how fast AI-native infrastructure startups can scale.** It's a pattern showing up across [AI agent funding broadly](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) in 2026 — strong revenue growth compressing the time between rounds and the size of the valuation jump.
