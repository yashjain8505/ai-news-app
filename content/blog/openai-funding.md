**OpenAI raised an additional $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation**, announced November 6, 2026 and led by **SoftBank and MGX**, with **Coatue Management** co-leading. It's an extension of the Series G that pushes OpenAI's valuation past the trillion-dollar mark for the first time, even as the size of the raise itself came in well below early reports.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and what the numbers say about where investor appetite for frontier AI stands heading into 2027.

## What is OpenAI?

OpenAI is an **AI research lab** developing frontier large language models and AI agent infrastructure. Founded in **2015** by **Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever and others**, the company's technology is embedded in products from **Microsoft and Apple**, and is sold directly to enterprise customers through its API.

## The raise: $11B Series G (additional)

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $11 billion
- **Round:** Series G (additional)
- **Valuation:** $1.25 trillion
- **Announced:** November 6, 2026
- **Lead investors:** SoftBank, MGX
- **Co-leads:** Coatue Management

This latest infusion follows **multiple rounds totaling more than $122 billion**, including a $122 billion raise in March 2026 at an $852 billion valuation. Going from $852 billion to $1.25 trillion in roughly eight months is a huge jump in absolute dollar terms, but notably, the $11 billion raised here is a **markdown from the initial $30 billion target** that had been reported before the round closed — a sign that even OpenAI isn't immune to investors pricing in more caution than the headline valuation suggests.

## Who invested in OpenAI?

The round was led by **SoftBank** and **MGX**, with **Coatue Management** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Sequoia Capital**
- **Founders Fund**
- **Andreessen Horowitz**
- **T. Rowe Price Associates**

The presence of MGX — an Abu Dhabi-backed investment vehicle — alongside SoftBank underscores how much of OpenAI's capital base now flows from sovereign and quasi-sovereign money rather than traditional venture funds alone.

## What OpenAI will do with the money

The financing feeds into OpenAI's enormous compute build-out: the company has signaled an estimated **$856 billion in compute spending through 2030**. Raising capital at this scale is less about a single product push and more about keeping pace with the sheer cost of training and serving frontier models at global scale — data centers, chips, and the power contracts to run them.

## Why it matters

OpenAI's latest raise is a signal worth parsing carefully, not just a bigger number:

1. **The markdown matters more than the valuation.** Closing at $11 billion against an initial $30 billion target, even while the valuation climbs to $1.25 trillion, suggests a market that's normalizing after a period of peak exuberance — investors are still showing up, but on tighter terms.
2. **Capital is going global and sovereign.** SoftBank and MGX leading the round, on top of OpenAI's broader $122 billion-plus raised in 2026, shows frontier AI labs increasingly funded by global capital pools rather than Silicon Valley venture alone — a dynamic also visible in deals tracked on [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **The compute bill keeps growing faster than the raises.** An $856 billion compute spending estimate through 2030 dwarfs even this $11 billion round, meaning more mega-raises are all but guaranteed as long as OpenAI keeps scaling.
