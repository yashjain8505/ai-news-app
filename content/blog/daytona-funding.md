**Daytona hit a $1 billion valuation** in a round led by **FirstMark Capital**, announced on October 23, 2026. The exact size of the check hasn't been disclosed, but the milestone itself is the headline: another agent-infrastructure startup has crossed into unicorn territory as enterprises race to put autonomous AI agents into production.

Here's what's known about the round and why it fits a bigger pattern.

## What is Daytona?

Daytona is an **agentic AI platform** for building and deploying autonomous agents. The company sits in the increasingly crowded "agent infrastructure" category — tooling that helps developers and enterprises run AI agents reliably rather than building and operating that infrastructure themselves.

No founding year, founders, or named customers have been disclosed for the company.

## The raise: $1B valuation

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** not disclosed
- **Round:** not disclosed
- **Valuation:** $1 billion
- **Announced:** October 23, 2026
- **Lead investor:** FirstMark Capital

No prior funding rounds have been disclosed. What is clear is that the round brought in, alongside FirstMark Capital, what's been described as a broad roster of angel investors — a signal that individual operators and founders in the AI space see enough promise in Daytona's approach to write personal checks.

## Who invested in Daytona?

**FirstMark Capital** led the round. Beyond that lead, the round reportedly included a wide group of angel investors, though specific names have not been disclosed.

## What Daytona will do with the money

Daytona says the funding will go toward **product development and market expansion** — the standard playbook for an infrastructure startup trying to capture share while demand for agent tooling is still being defined.

## Why it matters

1. **Agent infrastructure is minting unicorns fast.** Daytona joins a growing cohort of agent-focused startups reaching billion-dollar valuations, part of the broader wave covered in [Wortins' AI agents funding roundup](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
2. **Angel-heavy rounds signal insider conviction.** A "who's who of angels" backing a round, as reported, often means operators who've built or scaled similar infrastructure companies are personally betting on the category — a different kind of validation than institutional capital alone.
3. **The lack of disclosed round size is itself a data point.** Valuation-only announcements are increasingly common in this cycle, letting companies signal momentum without revealing how much capital — or dilution — it took to get there.
