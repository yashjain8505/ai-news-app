**Blue J raised $176 million in a Series E round at a valuation of $700 million or more**, announced on November 10, 2026 and led by **JMI Equity**. The round arrives as one of the clearer examples yet of AI finding a paying, regulated niche rather than chasing a general-purpose audience.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI in professional services.

## What is Blue J?

Blue J is an **AI-powered tax research and advisory platform** that uses generative AI to answer complex tax questions spanning U.S. federal, state and local tax law, as well as Canadian and UK tax law. Founded in **2015** by **Benjamin Alarie, Albert Yoon and Anthony Niblett**, the company has spent a decade building a product aimed squarely at one of the most document-heavy, liability-sensitive corners of professional services. Its customers include **KPMG, Crowe LLP, RSM**, a number of the **top 100 US CPA firms**, and **Fortune 100 companies**.

## The raise: $176M Series E

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $176 million
- **Round:** Series E
- **Valuation:** $700 million or more
- **Announced:** November 10, 2026
- **Lead investor:** JMI Equity

This is Blue J's third large raise in roughly two years. The Series D brought in $122 million at a $600 million-plus valuation in August 2025, which itself followed an $80 million Series C in 2024. Stepping from north of $600 million to north of $700 million in just over a year is a steadier climb than the breakneck valuation jumps seen elsewhere in AI, which fits a company selling into firms like KPMG and Crowe LLP that move carefully and expect staying power from their vendors.

## Who invested in Blue J?

**JMI Equity** led the round. Additional participants included:

- **Oak HC/FT**
- **Sapphire Ventures**
- **Intrepid Growth Partners**

## What Blue J will do with the money

Blue J says the capital will go toward commercializing and scaling **Workflows**, its agentic AI product built for multi-step tax tasks, and toward expanding further into the UK and other international markets. That's a meaningful shift from answering single tax questions to handling entire research-and-advisory workflows end to end — the kind of jump from "assistant" to "agent" that a growing share of enterprise AI funding is now chasing.

## Why it matters

1. **Professional services is proving to be a real AI market, not just a demo market.** Blue J's backers point to 165% ARR growth and more than 45,000 tax professionals on the platform — evidence that domain-specific AI can generate durable revenue in a field where mistakes carry real liability.
2. **Agentic products are becoming the next proof point for AI vendors.** Blue J's push into Workflows mirrors the broader move among [AI agent startups](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) to sell multi-step task automation rather than single-query tools, since that's where enterprises are willing to pay more.
3. **Regulated, high-stakes workflows are turning into a defensible niche.** At a $700 million-plus valuation, Blue J sits among a small group of AI-infrastructure companies with real, recurring customers rather than speculative usage — a pattern visible across the [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
