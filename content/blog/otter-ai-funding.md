**Otter.ai raised $320 million in a growth round** announced October 20, 2026, led by **General Catalyst's growth fund**. The company has been transcribing meetings since 2016 — long before "AI notetaker" was a crowded category — and this raise is a bet that it can turn that early lead into enterprise infrastructure rather than a feature every video-call app now bundles for free.

Here's the full breakdown of the round and what it signals about the meeting-AI market.

## What is Otter.ai?

Otter.ai is an **AI-powered meeting transcription assistant** that automatically records, transcribes, and summarizes conversations for knowledge workers. Founded in **2016**, it was one of the earliest companies to apply AI to the unglamorous but universal problem of "what actually got said and decided in this meeting." Its customer base spans **thousands of knowledge workers**, with a growing enterprise customer segment.

## The raise: $320M growth round

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $320 million
- **Round:** Growth round
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** October 20, 2026
- **Lead investor:** General Catalyst

Otter.ai has raised multiple earlier rounds and, notably, was **already profitable with strong usage metrics** going into this raise — a contrast with much of the AI funding landscape, where growth rounds often fund companies still burning heavily toward product-market fit. That profitability gives this raise a different character: it's expansion capital for a working business, not a runway extension.

## Who invested in Otter.ai?

The round was led by **General Catalyst**, through its growth fund. No co-leads or additional participants have been disclosed.

## What Otter.ai will do with the money

Otter.ai says the funding will go toward **enterprise sales expansion, international growth, and product development** aimed at moving the company beyond transcription into deeper meeting intelligence — the layer above "here's what was said" that gets into "here's what to do about it."

## Why it matters

1. **Transcription alone is no longer a differentiator.** Every major video-call platform now ships built-in transcription and summaries, which means the value has to come from somewhere else — in Otter's case, enterprise-grade meeting intelligence sold per seat.
2. **It's a sign of category maturation.** A $320 million round for a company that's already profitable, rather than one still searching for revenue, suggests meeting AI has moved from consumer novelty to business-critical software, a pattern visible across other large rounds tracked on [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **Higher-margin enterprise contracts are the real prize.** Shifting from "tool that transcribes" to "infrastructure enterprises pay for" is a classic path to defensible revenue, and it's the clearest explanation for why General Catalyst was willing to write a check this size for a decade-old company in a now-commoditized space.
