**Positron AI raised $875 million in a Series C round at a $5 billion valuation**, announced September 10, 2026 and led by **NEA, Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners**. It's one of the largest chip-startup raises of the year, and it lands as inference cost — not model quality — increasingly determines who can afford to run AI at scale.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why memory-first chip design is attracting serious capital.

## What is Positron AI?

Positron AI builds **memory-first AI inference silicon** that uses **commodity LPDDR5X memory** instead of specialized high-bandwidth memory (HBM), an approach aimed at making large-scale AI inference cheaper to run. Founded in **2023** by **Thomas Sohmers, Edward Kmett and Barrett Woodside**, the company has already landed customers including **Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, Parasail/SnapServe, Jump Trading and i3d.net**.

In plain terms: most AI inference chips lean on expensive, supply-constrained HBM to move data fast enough to keep up with large models. Positron's bet is that commodity memory, arranged the right way, can get close enough on performance while being dramatically cheaper and easier to source — a pitch that matters as HBM shortages have become a recurring bottleneck across the AI chip industry.

## The raise: $875M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $875 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $5 billion
- **Announced:** September 10, 2026
- **Lead investors:** NEA, Atreides Management, Valor Equity Partners
- **Co-lead:** Jim Clark

For context, Positron AI raised a **$230 million Series B in February 2026**, which followed a **$51.6 million Series A in July 2025** and a **$23.5 million pre-Series A in 2024**. That puts total capital raised at roughly **$1.18 billion in about 18 months**, with the valuation climbing sharply as the company has moved from early funding rounds toward a production-ready chip.

## Who invested in Positron AI?

The Series C was led by **NEA, Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners**, co-led by **Jim Clark**, with additional participation from:

- **Andra Capital**
- **SemiAnalysis Capital**
- **Qatar Investment Authority**
- **Hudson River Trading**
- **Cisco Investments**

The investor list mixes traditional venture firms (NEA), sovereign capital (Qatar Investment Authority), trading firms (Hudson River Trading) and strategic corporate money (Cisco Investments) — a spread that suggests both financial and strategic interest in Positron's approach to inference economics.

## What Positron AI will do with the money

Positron AI plans to use the funding for the **tapeout of its Asimov chip at TSMC's N3P node**, targeting completion by the **end of 2026** with production expected in the **second half of 2027**. The company also intends to build a **2MW+ engineering data center and emulation platform**, and to **scale production of its Titan system** for models with **16-32+ trillion parameters**.

That roadmap — tapeout, a dedicated engineering data center, and system-level production scaling — reflects a hardware company moving from prototype to manufacturing, which is a capital-intensive phase that explains why the round is sized as large as it is.

## Why it matters

Positron AI's round says a lot about where chip investment is headed in 2026:

1. **Inference cost is now the dominant constraint on AI deployment economics.** As more companies move from experimenting with AI to running it in production, the cost of serving models — not just training them — is becoming the line item that determines profitability, and Positron is betting that commodity-memory silicon can meaningfully lower that cost.
2. **Enterprise adoption is already validating the architecture.** Oracle Cloud Infrastructure's 50+ rack deployment is a concrete signal that memory-first inference chips can work at real scale, not just in benchmarks — a milestone that matters more than valuation alone.
3. **Chip startups are raising at a pace that rivals foundation model labs.** Positron's $1.18 billion in roughly 18 months puts it in the same funding tier as companies like [Etched](/blog/etched-funding), underscoring how much capital is now chasing alternatives to Nvidia's dominant position in AI compute.
