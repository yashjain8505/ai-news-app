**Naïve raised $28.5 million in a Series A round**, announced on August 6, 2026 and led by **Nexus Venture Partners**. The raise backs a bet that AI agents won't just assist with company operations — they'll increasingly run them.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI-run back offices.

## What is Naïve?

Naïve is a **serverless API platform** that lets AI agents automate a company's back-office operations — from setup tasks to the ongoing operational plumbing that keeps a business running. Founded in **2026** by **Sean Dorje and Dennis Zax**, the company has already signed up more than **30,000 developers**.

## The raise: $28.5M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $28.5 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Announced:** August 6, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Nexus Venture Partners
- **Co-leads:** Y Combinator, Zetta

Naïve has not disclosed prior funding rounds, making this Series A its first public capital raise.

## Who invested in Naïve?

The Series A was led by **Nexus Venture Partners**, with **Y Combinator** and **Zetta** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **HubSpot**
- **Amazon**
- **DocuSign**

The presence of HubSpot, Amazon and DocuSign as participants — all companies with their own stakes in business operations software — suggests strategic as well as financial interest in where AI-run back offices are headed.

## What Naïve will do with the money

Naïve says the funding will go toward **engineering, product development, and developer tools and SDKs** — the groundwork needed to let more developers build on top of its agent-driven operations platform.

## Why it matters

Naïve's raise is an early marker of a bigger shift in how companies think about operational headcount:

1. **The "autonomous company" thesis is attracting real capital, not just attention.** Naïve's pitch — that AI agents can handle the grunt work of setting up and running a company — is moving from a science-fiction pitch to something investors are willing to fund at Series A.
2. **Growth numbers back up the thesis.** Tenfold revenue growth to low double-digit millions and 30,000+ developer signups in just months of operation is a fast trajectory for a company founded in 2026, suggesting real developer demand rather than speculative interest.
3. **This fits a broader wave of AI agent infrastructure funding.** Naïve joins a growing set of companies, including [June](/blog/june-ai-funding), building the operational and interaction layers that sit on top of foundation models rather than the models themselves.
