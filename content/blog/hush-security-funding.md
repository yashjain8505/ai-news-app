**Hush Security raised $30 million in a Series A round**, announced on August 20, 2026 and led by **Akamai**. The raise puts fresh capital behind a narrow but fast-growing problem: how enterprises govern AI agents that now have permission to touch real infrastructure.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what it means for the agent security market.

## What is Hush Security?

Hush Security is an **AI agent governance platform**. It enrolls autonomous agents in a registry, manages just-in-time permissions, and controls what those agents can access as they spread across enterprise systems. Founded in **2025**, the company emerged from stealth that September before raising this Series A less than a year later.

## The raise: $30M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $30 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Announced:** August 20, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Akamai

Hush Security emerged from stealth in September 2025 with an undisclosed seed round. This Series A brings its total funding to **$41 million** — a quick follow-on that suggests the company found demand for agent governance tooling faster than a typical enterprise security startup.

## Who invested in Hush Security?

The Series A was led by **Akamai**. No co-leads or additional participants were disclosed for this round. Akamai's involvement is notable given its position as a major infrastructure and security vendor, rather than a traditional venture fund.

## What Hush Security will do with the money

The company says the funding will go toward **agent governance infrastructure, security framework development, and enterprise integrations** — the work of making its registry and permissioning system plug into the identity and access systems large organizations already run.

## Why it matters

Hush Security's raise reflects a security category that's forming quickly around AI agents:

1. **Agent sprawl is becoming a real security problem, not a hypothetical one.** Gartner projects Fortune 500 firms will run more than 150,000 AI agents by 2028, up from fewer than 15 today — a scale of autonomous software identity that most enterprise security stacks were never built to track.
2. **Identity and access control is shifting from humans to software.** As agents gain the ability to act on real infrastructure, the just-in-time permissioning model Hush Security is building addresses a gap that's distinct from traditional human identity management, similar in spirit to how [other agent-focused startups](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) are racing to define the operational layer around autonomous systems.
3. **A major infrastructure player leading the round is a signal of urgency.** Akamai leading rather than co-investing suggests strategic interest from a company that already secures internet-scale infrastructure and sees agent governance as adjacent to its core business.
