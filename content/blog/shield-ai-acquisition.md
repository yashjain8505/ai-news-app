**Shield AI has acquired radar maker Echodyne in a deal valued at $5.3 billion**, announced November 9, 2026, alongside new funding that values Shield AI itself at **$28.8 billion** and was led by **Disruptive** and **Innovation X Capital**, with **Advent** and **Blackstone** co-leading. It's one of the largest defense-tech acquisitions on record, and it comes as autonomous-warfare startups race to own more of the hardware stack rather than just the software layer.

Here's the full breakdown of the deal, who's backing it, and what it signals about where defense-tech money is headed.

## What is Shield AI?

Shield AI develops **autonomous software for military defense applications**, including drone detection, counter-UAS (counter-drone) systems, and battlefield autonomy. Founded in **2015**, the company has built its business around software that lets drones and other systems operate without constant human piloting — a capability increasingly in demand as militaries look to scale up autonomous surveillance and defense without scaling up personnel at the same rate.

## The raise: $5.3B acquisition

The headline numbers:

- **Deal value:** $5.3 billion
- **Type:** Acquisition (of Echodyne) paired with new growth funding
- **New Shield AI valuation:** $28.8 billion
- **Announced:** November 9, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Disruptive, Innovation X Capital
- **Co-leads:** Advent, Blackstone

Shield AI's prior Series H had valued the company at $12.7 billion — meaning this round more than doubles its valuation. The new capital is structured unusually: $1 billion in equity, $1 billion in a structured investment from Blackstone, and $500 million in debt. That mix of equity, structured capital, and debt is more typical of a company financing a large acquisition than a startup raising a conventional venture round, and it reflects just how large the Echodyne deal is relative to Shield AI's balance sheet.

## Who invested in Shield AI?

The round was led by **Disruptive** and **Innovation X Capital**, with **Advent** and **Blackstone** co-leading. Blackstone's piece came in the form of a structured investment rather than straight equity, alongside the $500 million in debt financing that rounded out the package.

## What Shield AI will do with the money

The funding's stated purpose is specific: **to acquire Echodyne**, a radar maker whose counter-drone systems are already deployed across 40 countries, with more than 100 systems in the field. Echodyne gives Shield AI the hardware side of counter-drone detection — radar that spots incoming threats — to pair with the autonomy software Shield AI already builds. Owning both halves of that pipeline lets Shield AI sell an integrated sense-and-respond system instead of software that depends on a third party's sensors.

## Why it matters

1. **Defense-tech startups are moving from software to full-stack hardware ownership.** Shield AI's jump from pure autonomy software to owning radar hardware mirrors a broader shift among AI-native defense companies, also visible in how fast [AI agent companies like Cognition](/blog/cognition-ai-funding) are expanding beyond their original product lines as they scale.
2. **This is one of the largest defense-tech acquisitions on record**, and it shows how aggressively defense budgets and private capital are flowing into companies that can offer end-to-end autonomous-warfare solutions rather than single-point products.
3. **The financing structure — equity, structured capital, and debt together — signals how large these deals have become.** Raising $2.5 billion in fresh capital to help fund a $5.3 billion acquisition is the kind of financing typically reserved for much more mature companies, not an eleven-year-old startup.
