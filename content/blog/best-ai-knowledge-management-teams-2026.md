**There's no single best AI knowledge management tool — Guru ($25/user/month) wins on governance and verified answers, Glean ($25-50/user/month) wins on breadth if your knowledge is scattered across 100+ apps, and Confluence Rovo ($5.42/user/month) is the cheapest way to add AI search to an enterprise tool you likely already own.** The category splits on a real tradeoff: purpose-built knowledge platforms (Guru, Glean) cost more but do more, while AI layers bolted onto existing tools (Notion, Confluence, Slack) cost less if you're already paying for the base product.

At 100 people, the cost spread is enormous — roughly $626/year for Confluence Rovo versus $30k/year for Guru — so the decision isn't just "which tool is best," it's "how much is fixing stale, scattered answers actually worth to your team."

## The short answer

If the core problem is people trusting wrong or outdated answers, **Guru** is built around that specifically: proactive staleness alerts and verification workflows on top of 100+ connectors, backed by a 4.8/5 rating across 2,317 G2 reviews, at $25/user/month with a 10-seat minimum. If your knowledge is scattered across dozens of disconnected systems — Slack, Salesforce, GitHub, Jira, Google Workspace — **Glean** indexes 100+ enterprise apps with semantic search and reportedly processes 270M+ AI knowledge actions annually, priced $25-50/user/month. Smaller teams already living in Notion should look at **Notion AI**'s Business tier ($20/user/month), which bundles custom AI agents, meeting transcription in 16 languages, and database auto-fill — though it's rated for around 200 concurrent users before performance degrades. IT-heavy organizations already on Atlassian get the cheapest path to AI knowledge search through **Confluence Rovo**, at $5.42/user/month on the Standard plan with 20+ pre-built agents and 80+ connectors on a credit-based usage model. And if your team already lives in Slack, **Slack AI**'s unified search ($15/user/month on Business+) searches across 100+ connected SaaS apps with permission-aware retrieval — the company reports 70% of orgs using the feature see a 25%+ productivity gain.

## Guru

Guru's differentiator is trust, not just retrieval: proactive alerts flag content that's gone stale, and verification workflows let designated owners confirm answers are still accurate before they're surfaced to the rest of the team. With 100+ connectors and a 4.8/5 rating across 2,317 G2 reviews, it's positioned as the governance-first option in the category, at $25/user/month with a 10-seat minimum.

**Pros:** highest publicly cited user satisfaction rating in this comparison; staleness detection and verification directly address the "can I trust this answer" problem other tools don't solve as explicitly.
**Cons:** 10-seat minimum makes it a worse fit for very small teams; per-seat cost is on the higher end of the category.
**Who it's for:** teams that have been burned by outdated internal documentation and need built-in processes to keep answers current and verified.

## Glean

Glean's pitch is breadth: it indexes 100+ enterprise apps — Slack, Salesforce, GitHub, Jira, Google Workspace among them — with semantic search that understands intent rather than just matching keywords, and the company reports processing 270M+ AI knowledge actions per year across its customer base. Enterprise pricing runs $25-50/user/month.

**Pros:** widest integration footprint in this comparison; semantic search is a meaningfully better experience than keyword search once your knowledge is spread across many systems.
**Cons:** pricing tops out higher than Guru at the top of its range; the scale of integration that makes Glean valuable also means more setup complexity than a single-purpose tool.
**Who it's for:** larger organizations whose knowledge genuinely lives across many disconnected apps, not concentrated in one or two systems.

## Notion AI

For teams already running their documentation and project work in Notion, the Business tier at $20/user/month adds custom AI agents, meeting notes that transcribe in 16 languages, and automatic database fill-in. It's the cheapest of the purpose-built knowledge options here, but Notion notes it's built for roughly 200 concurrent users before performance starts to degrade.

**Pros:** cheaper than Guru or Glean; no need to adopt a separate knowledge tool if your team already works inside Notion.
**Cons:** the ~200 concurrent user ceiling makes it a weaker fit for larger organizations; less purpose-built for cross-app knowledge governance than Guru or Glean.
**Who it's for:** small-to-mid teams already using Notion as their primary workspace who want AI search and agents without adding a new tool.

## Confluence Rovo

Rovo is Atlassian's AI layer on Confluence, and at $5.42/user/month on the Standard plan, it's by far the cheapest enterprise-grade option in this comparison — for a 100-person team, that's roughly $626/year versus $30k/year for Guru at the same headcount. It includes 20+ pre-built agents and 80+ app connectors, metered through a credit model (25-70 credits per user per month).

**Pros:** lowest cost by a wide margin; already-familiar for any team running Confluence, so adoption friction is low.
**Cons:** the credit-based usage model adds a layer of cost unpredictability that flat per-seat pricing (Guru, Notion) doesn't have; less specialized governance tooling than Guru's staleness alerts and verification workflows.
**Who it's for:** organizations already on Atlassian's Confluence who want the cheapest path to AI-powered knowledge search without switching platforms.

## Slack AI

Slack AI's unified search, available on the Business+ plan at $15/user/month, searches across 100+ connected SaaS apps with permission-aware retrieval — meaning it respects existing access controls rather than surfacing content a searcher shouldn't see. Slack reports that 70% of organizations using the feature see a 25%+ productivity gain.

**Pros:** permission-aware retrieval is a meaningful security advantage; priced in the middle of the category and bundled with a tool most teams already use daily.
**Cons:** search-focused rather than a full knowledge-management platform — it doesn't include the verification workflows or staleness alerts that Guru offers.
**Who it's for:** teams whose daily work already happens in Slack and want search across connected apps without adopting a separate knowledge base.

## How to choose

Start with where the problem actually lives. If it's trust in the answers themselves — people citing outdated docs — Guru's verification workflows and staleness alerts are the most direct fix, even at $25/user/month. If the problem is that knowledge is scattered across a dozen disconnected tools, Glean's 100+ app index solves that more completely than any single-workspace tool can. If you're a smaller team and already pay for Notion, Confluence, or Slack, the cheapest and lowest-friction move is turning on the AI layer you're already halfway paying for — Confluence Rovo at $5.42/user/month is the standout value here. Reserve Guru or Glean for when the cost of bad or missing knowledge — repeated questions, wrong answers, onboarding friction — clearly exceeds the $25-50/user/month premium over the bundled options.
