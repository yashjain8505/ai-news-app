**Rillet AI raised $100 million in a Series C round at a $1 billion valuation**, announced in August 2026 and led by **ICONIQ**. The round makes Rillet a unicorn just two years after it was founded. Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it says about AI's inroads into back-office finance.

## What is Rillet AI?

Rillet AI is an **AI-native accounting and finance platform** that automates routine accounting work — the kind of repetitive, rules-based bookkeeping and reconciliation tasks that traditionally require finance teams to grow headcount as a company scales. Founded in **2024**, Rillet counts **Neuralink and Mercor** among its customers.

## The raise: $100M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $100 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $1 billion
- **Announced:** August 2026
- **Lead investor:** ICONIQ

Rillet's total funding now **exceeds $200 million** across multiple rounds, meaning this single Series C accounts for roughly half of everything the company has raised since its 2024 founding — a rapid trajectory from startup to unicorn in about two years.

## Who invested in Rillet AI?

The round was led by **ICONIQ**, with participation from:

- **Sequoia**
- **Andreessen Horowitz**
- **Oak HC/FT**
- **Bain Capital Ventures**

That's a lineup of some of the most prominent multi-stage venture firms in the industry, spanning both generalist funds (Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz) and fintech-focused investors (Oak HC/FT, Bain Capital Ventures).

## Why it matters

1. **AI is automating structured, rules-heavy back-office work at real scale.** Rillet's pitch isn't a flashy consumer product — it's accounting, one of the most process-driven functions inside any company. One of its customers reportedly manages $2 billion in annual recurring revenue with just a three-person finance team, which is the kind of leverage that makes AI-native software genuinely disruptive rather than just a nice-to-have.
2. **Fast unicorn status signals investor appetite for proven AI leverage, not just AI branding.** Going from founding to a $1 billion valuation in roughly two years, with backing from firms like Sequoia and ICONIQ, suggests investors are rewarding demonstrated efficiency gains over speculative potential — a theme that shows up across the [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **Vertical AI in finance is attracting top-tier, multi-stage capital.** The mix of generalist and fintech-specialist investors in this round shows that AI-native finance software is now viewed as a durable category, not a niche experiment.
