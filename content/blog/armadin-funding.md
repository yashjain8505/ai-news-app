**Armadin raised $255.5 million in a Series B round at a valuation of more than $2.5 billion**, announced October 1, 2026 and led by **Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)**, with **Accel** co-leading. The round lands barely seven months after the company's Series A, and it puts one of cybersecurity's best-known names at the center of AI's latest arms race: securing the agents enterprises are now plugging into their own networks.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why it's happening now.

## What is Armadin?

Armadin builds **AI agent swarms** that hunt for vulnerabilities through continuous offensive security, with the goal of replacing traditional, point-in-time penetration testing. Instead of a security firm running a manual test once or twice a year, Armadin's agents are designed to probe systems continuously, the way an adversary would.

The company was founded in **2025** by **Kevin Mandia**, the former CEO of Mandiant, alongside **Travis Lanham**, **Evan Peña**, and **David Slater**. Mandia's background carries real weight here: he led Mandiant through its $5.4 billion acquisition by Google, and that track record is a big part of why investors are willing to back a company this young at this valuation. Armadin counts **corporate and government customers** among its early clients.

## The raise: $255.5M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $255.5 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $2.5 billion+
- **Announced:** October 1, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)
- **Co-lead:** Accel

This is Armadin's second fundraise since launching. The company closed a **$189.9 million Series A in March 2026** at an undisclosed valuation — meaning that in roughly seven months, it went from a freshly funded startup to a company two of venture capital's most prominent firms are valuing above $2.5 billion. That pace is unusual even by 2026's standards, and it reflects how quickly investors are moving to back credible teams in AI security.

## Who invested in Armadin?

The Series B was led by **Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)**, with **Accel** co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed for this round.

## What Armadin will do with the money

Armadin says the funding will go toward **scaling its agent capabilities, expanding its customer base, and hiring security and AI talent**. In practice, that means growing the engineering bench needed to keep agent swarms effective against evolving attack techniques, and building out the commercial team to serve a customer base that already spans both private companies and government buyers.

## Why it matters

1. **The attack surface is growing faster than defenses.** As enterprises wire autonomous AI agents into production systems, they're creating new entry points for attackers faster than security teams can audit them manually. Armadin's bet is that only another layer of AI — agents watching agents — can keep pace, a theme running through the broader [AI defense tech funding](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026) boom in 2026.
2. **Founder pedigree is buying speed, not just capital.** Mandia selling Mandiant to Google for $5.4 billion gives Armadin a credibility shortcut that most two-round startups don't get — it's a big reason a16z and Accel were comfortable pricing a seven-month-old Series A at a $2.5 billion-plus follow-on.
3. **Offensive-security-as-a-service is becoming a venture category of its own.** Armadin's raise is part of a broader wave of nine- and ten-figure rounds flowing into AI-native security and infrastructure companies this year, a trend visible across the [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
