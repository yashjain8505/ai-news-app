**Cognition raised $2 billion in a Series E round at a $48 billion valuation**, announced September 8, 2026 and led by **Andreessen Horowitz**. The round comes just four months after its previous raise, and it lands as investors keep betting big on autonomous coding agents.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the AI coding market.

## What is Cognition?

Cognition is the company behind **Devin**, an AI coding agent that autonomously handles software development tasks end-to-end rather than just suggesting code inside an editor. Founded in **2024** by **Scott Wu**, the company positions Devin as a system that can take on real engineering work — writing, testing and shipping code — with less hand-holding than typical AI coding assistants. Its customers include **Mercedes-Benz, NASA, Goldman Sachs and Citi**.

## The raise: $2B Series E

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $2 billion
- **Round:** Series E
- **Valuation:** $48 billion
- **Announced:** September 8, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Andreessen Horowitz
- **Co-leads:** Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst, Avenir

For context, Cognition's previous round was a **$1 billion Series D at a $26 billion valuation** in May 2026. Going from $26 billion to $48 billion in four months is nearly a doubling of valuation — one of the fastest markups among AI agent companies this year.

## Who invested in Cognition?

The Series E was led by **Andreessen Horowitz**, with **Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst and Avenir** co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed beyond the lead and co-lead group.

## What Cognition will do with the money

Cognition said the funding will go toward **expanding compute infrastructure and R&D for its own model development**. That second piece is notable: rather than relying solely on third-party foundation models to power Devin, Cognition is investing in building its own models, a move that reduces dependence on outside AI providers and gives it more control over the agent's performance and cost structure.

## Why it matters

Cognition's raise is a signal of where investor conviction is concentrated in 2026:

1. **Autonomous coding agents are proving out commercially, not just technically.** Cognition's run-rate revenue reportedly grew from $492 million to $900 million in the same four-month window as its valuation nearly doubled — a sign that Devin is generating real usage, not just hype.
2. **The AI coding market isn't consolidating around a single winner.** A $48 billion valuation for Cognition arrives alongside continued investment across the AI agents space, echoing the same "picks and shovels don't have to have one winner" dynamic seen in [AI infrastructure funding rounds](/blog/baseten-funding).
3. **Model independence is becoming a competitive differentiator.** By funding its own model development instead of leaning entirely on third-party providers, Cognition is following a path other well-capitalized [AI agents](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) companies are also pursuing as they mature past their earliest product stage.
