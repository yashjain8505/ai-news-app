**The right AI tutor depends on who's learning and why: Khanmigo ($4/month) is built for K-12 Socratic coaching, Quizlet Q-Chat ($7.99/month) is best for flashcard-driven recall, NotebookLM (free) is the strongest choice for learning from your own source material, and Wondering (custom pricing) targets ambitious adult self-learners.** Teachers have their own separate lane — MagicSchool AI and Brisk Teaching automate planning and grading rather than tutoring students directly.

The AI tutoring market was $2.7B in 2026 and is growing at a 30.5% CAGR toward $17.7B by 2033, alongside 70% edtech penetration in developed markets — so this is a genuinely fast-growing category, not a niche experiment.

## The short answer

For K-12 students, **Khanmigo** at $4/month for families (or $44/year) is the cheapest option here and the most deliberate about pedagogy: it's built on the Socratic method, meaning it coaches your thinking rather than handing over answers outright. If the learning style is recall and flashcard drilling rather than open-ended coaching, **Quizlet Plus with Q-Chat** ($7.99/month or $35.99/year) adds Magic Notes and adaptive pacing on top of Quizlet's existing study-set ecosystem. For adult self-learners tackling new topics on their own time, **Wondering** is a premium platform (custom pricing) purpose-built for ambitious 22-45 year olds, pacing lessons through interactive visuals, podcasts, and exercises. If you'd rather learn from material you already have — a textbook, a set of papers, your own notes — **NotebookLM** is free with a Google account and grounds every answer in your source material with citations. For teachers, the tools aren't tutors at all: **MagicSchool AI** (free, or Plus at $12.99/month) automates lesson planning and worksheet generation, and **Brisk Teaching** (free limited tier, or Pro at $9.99/month) integrates into Google Docs to speed up grading.

## Khanmigo

Khanmigo's defining feature isn't its price ($4/month for families, or $44/year) — it's the pedagogy. It's deliberately built around the Socratic method, meaning it won't just hand a struggling student the answer; it asks guiding questions and coaches the thinking process instead. That's a meaningfully different product than a chatbot that answers homework questions directly.

**Pros:** cheapest tool on this list; specifically designed to build understanding rather than just deliver answers; K-12 focused, so content and tone are age-appropriate.
**Cons:** the Socratic approach is a poor fit if a student just needs a quick, direct answer under time pressure; scope is K-12, not useful for adult learners or professional topics.
**Who it's for:** K-12 students and parents who want a tutor that builds understanding over time, not just a homework-answer machine.

## Quizlet Plus with Q-Chat

Quizlet Plus ($7.99/month or $35.99/year) layers Q-Chat and Magic Notes on top of Quizlet's existing flashcard and study-set ecosystem, with adaptive pacing that adjusts based on what a student has and hasn't retained.

**Pros:** builds on Quizlet's large existing library of study sets, so there's less setup than starting from scratch; adaptive pacing targets actual recall gaps.
**Cons:** more expensive than Khanmigo; recall-and-drill is a narrower learning style than Khanmigo's open-ended Socratic coaching.
**Who it's for:** students whose main bottleneck is memorization and recall — vocabulary, definitions, formulas — rather than conceptual reasoning.

## Wondering

Wondering is positioned differently from the rest of this list: a premium platform, custom-priced, aimed specifically at ambitious 22-45 year old self-learners rather than students in a school system. It personalizes lesson pacing through interactive visuals, podcasts, and exercises, treating adult self-directed learning as its own distinct problem rather than a repurposed K-12 product.

**Pros:** purpose-built for adult learners, a segment most of the other tools here don't target; multi-format content (visuals, podcasts, exercises) suits varied learning preferences.
**Cons:** custom pricing means no transparent entry point to evaluate against the others; not a fit for K-12 or classroom use.
**Who it's for:** adults learning ambitiously outside a formal school or work-training context, who want more structure than a free tool like NotebookLM provides.

## NotebookLM

NotebookLM is free with a Google account, and its core value is grounding: instead of generating answers from general training data, it answers from material you upload — your own notes, textbooks, papers — with citations back to the source. That makes it less a "tutor" in the coaching sense and more a study companion for material you already have.

**Pros:** free; answers are grounded in and cited to your actual source material, reducing the risk of confidently wrong answers; works for any subject you can supply materials for.
**Cons:** it doesn't coach or pace learning the way Khanmigo or Wondering do — it's reactive to what you upload and ask, not a structured curriculum.
**Who it's for:** anyone who already has source material (a textbook, lecture notes, research papers) and wants to query and synthesize it, rather than follow a guided curriculum.

## MagicSchool AI and Brisk Teaching

These two aren't tutors — they're built for the teacher's side of the classroom. MagicSchool AI is free for over 80 tools, or $12.99/month (Plus) for expanded lesson planning, worksheets, rubrics, and curriculum generation. Brisk Teaching is free at a limited tier or $9.99/month (Pro), and integrates directly into Google Docs with a Chrome extension, focused specifically on speeding up grading.

**Who they're for:** teachers looking to cut prep and grading time, not students or self-learners looking for a tutor.

## How to choose

Match the tool to who's learning and what "help" should look like. For K-12 students, Khanmigo's Socratic method at $4/month is both the cheapest and most pedagogically deliberate choice, while Quizlet Q-Chat ($7.99/month) is the better fit if the actual bottleneck is memorization rather than conceptual understanding. For adults learning on their own initiative, Wondering is built for that specifically, but NotebookLM (free) is worth trying first if you already have source material to learn from — there's no cost to seeing whether grounded Q&A meets the need before paying for a premium platform. Teachers have a different problem entirely: MagicSchool AI and Brisk Teaching aren't competing with the tutoring tools above, they're solving prep and grading time, and both have usable free tiers to start with.
