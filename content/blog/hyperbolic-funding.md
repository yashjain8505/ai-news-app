**Hyperbolic raised $30 million in a Series A-1 round**, announced October 27, 2026 and led by **Walden Catalyst**, with **Dell Technologies Capital and General Catalyst** co-leading. The round comes as the startup chases $150 million in annual recurring revenue by year-end, built entirely on aggregating scarce GPU capacity for AI developers.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's backing it, and why GPU scarcity keeps attracting investor dollars.

## What is Hyperbolic?

Hyperbolic is an **AI compute marketplace** that aggregates GPU capacity across roughly **30 neocloud providers**, letting developers buy, manage, and run compute from a single interface instead of negotiating with each cloud separately. Founded in **2024** by **Jasper Zhang**, the company counts **Modal, Higgsfield and Daytona** among its customers, alongside more than **250,000 developers** on the platform.

## The raise: $30M Series A-1

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $30 million
- **Round:** Series A-1
- **Announced:** October 27, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Walden Catalyst
- **Co-leads:** Dell Technologies Capital, General Catalyst

This is Hyperbolic's second raise in its young history, following a **$20 million Series A** — bringing total funding to $50 million. Notably, Walden Catalyst is led by Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan, putting one of the chip industry's most prominent executives directly behind a startup whose business model depends on GPU scarcity persisting.

## Who invested in Hyperbolic?

The round was led by **Walden Catalyst**, with **Dell Technologies Capital** and **General Catalyst** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- Vista Equity Partners
- Cambium Capital

## What Hyperbolic will do with the money

Hyperbolic says the funding will go toward building its **Forge unified control plane**, expanding multi-cloud orchestration, and building out its **Global Compute Grid** — all aimed at hitting a stated goal of **$150 million in ARR by the end of the year**.

That's an aggressive revenue target for a company that had raised only $20 million before this round, and it underscores how much demand exists for compute brokering right now: developers don't just want raw GPUs, they want someone to manage the complexity of sourcing them across dozens of providers.

## Why it matters

1. **GPU scarcity is still the defining constraint in AI.** Hyperbolic's entire business exists because individual developers and smaller companies can't get reliable, affordably priced GPU access on their own — a problem reflected across the broader wave of [AI data center funding in 2026](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026).
2. **Chip-industry money is flowing into compute brokers, not just chipmakers.** An Intel CEO's venture fund backing a GPU marketplace — rather than a chip designer — shows that even hardware insiders see value accruing to the layer that allocates scarce compute, not just the layer that manufactures it.
3. **The round size is modest, but the ambition isn't.** $30 million is small next to the mega-rounds dominating [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), but a $150 million ARR target by year-end would make Hyperbolic one of the fastest-scaling infrastructure startups of the year if it hits it.
