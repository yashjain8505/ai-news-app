**Helsing raised $1.8 billion in a Series C round at an $18 billion valuation**, announced July 13, 2026 and led by **JPMorgan Chase, Lightspeed Venture Partners and ICONIQ**. The round makes Helsing the world's most valuable defense-tech company, landing as investor capital pours into military AI amid rising geopolitical tension.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals about the state of defense AI investment.

## What is Helsing?

Helsing builds an **AI software platform** that ingests and makes sense of military sensor, surveillance and weapons data for defense operations. In plain terms: modern militaries generate enormous volumes of raw sensor feeds, and turning that flood of data into usable, real-time situational awareness for commanders is as much a software problem as a military one. Helsing's platform sits in that layer, processing battlefield data to support defense decision-making — placing it squarely inside the fast-growing "battlefield AI" category, alongside a wave of startups trying to modernize how militaries fuse and act on sensor data. The company's founding year, founders and customer base were not disclosed alongside this round, but the scale of the raise alone signals a company already operating well beyond the prototype stage.

## The raise: $1.8B Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $1.8 billion
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $18 billion
- **Announced:** July 13, 2026
- **Lead investors:** JPMorgan Chase, Lightspeed Venture Partners, ICONIQ

No co-leads or additional participants were named alongside the three lead investors. Prior funding history for Helsing wasn't disclosed with this round, so it's not possible to say how much the $18 billion valuation represents a step up from earlier rounds — but on an absolute basis, it's enough to make Helsing the highest-valued company in defense technology today, ahead of most other startups building software or hardware for military use.

## Who invested in Helsing?

The round was led jointly by **JPMorgan Chase**, **Lightspeed Venture Partners** and **ICONIQ**:

- **JPMorgan Chase**
- **Lightspeed Venture Partners**
- **ICONIQ**

The mix is notable in itself — a major bank sitting alongside two venture firms as co-leads on a defense-tech round is not the typical shape of a Series C, and reflects how mainstream capital is now flowing directly into military AI rather than staying confined to specialist defense investors.

## What Helsing will do with the money

Helsing said the funding will go toward **research and development for its defense AI capabilities**. Beyond that stated use, no additional detail was given on headcount plans, geographic expansion, or specific product lines the R&D spend will target.

## Why it matters

Helsing's raise is a striking data point for anyone tracking where AI capital is concentrating in 2026:

1. **Defense AI is now one of the best-funded, most contested corners of the AI market.** An $18 billion valuation for a company processing military sensor and weapons data shows investors are pricing battlefield software as aggressively as any consumer or enterprise AI category — see our broader look at [defense-tech funding in 2026](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026) for how Helsing fits alongside other players in the space.
2. **The round raises real accountability questions.** Software that helps interpret surveillance and weapons data sits close to life-and-death decisions, and the pace of capital moving into this category is outrunning public debate over autonomy and oversight in military AI systems.
3. **It's one of the largest single AI rounds of the year.** At $1.8 billion, Helsing's Series C ranks among the [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), underscoring how much capital is now chasing a small number of very large defense and infrastructure bets.
