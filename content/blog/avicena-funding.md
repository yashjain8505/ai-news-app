**Avicena Tech raised more than $100 million in a Series C round**, announced November 24, 2026, led by **InterVest** and co-led by **Tiger Global** — with a backer list stacked full of the chip and memory industry's biggest strategic investors.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and why moving data between AI chips has become a funding priority of its own.

## What is Avicena Tech?

Avicena Tech develops **microLED-based optical interconnects** — ultra-low-power links for chip-to-chip communication inside AI infrastructure. Instead of the lasers typically used in optical networking, Avicena uses dense arrays of microLEDs to move data between chips with less power draw, under its **LightBundle** platform. The company was founded in **2019** by **Bardia Pezeshki** and **Rob Kalman**.

## The raise: $100M+ Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $100 million or more
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** November 24, 2026
- **Lead investor:** InterVest
- **Co-lead:** Tiger Global

Avicena's funding history before this round was smaller but strategically dense: a **$25 million Series A** in August 2022 led by **Samsung** and **Cerberus**, followed by a **$65 million Series B** in May 2025 led by **Tiger Global** — roughly **$90 million** in total prior funding. This Series C alone is larger than everything the company had raised before it combined, more than doubling its total capital raised.

## Who invested in Avicena Tech?

The round was led by **InterVest**, co-led by **Tiger Global** (which also led the Series B), and joined by:

- **Cerberus Capital Management**
- **SK Hynix Ventures**
- **Samsung Catalyst Fund**
- **AMD Ventures**
- **Prosperity7 Ventures**
- **Diamond Edge Ventures** (Mitsubishi Chemical)
- **Hitachi Ventures**

The presence of **SK Hynix, Samsung, and AMD** investing directly is the standout detail — these are companies that build the memory and chips Avicena's interconnects are meant to link, not generalist financial investors.

## What Avicena Tech will do with the money

Avicena says the funding will go toward expanding its engineering team, accelerating product development, and preparing its LightBundle platform for commercial deployment, with a first commercial product targeted for **2027**.

## Why it matters

1. **Moving data between chips is becoming the bottleneck, not just making the chips.** As AI models scale across thousands of chips working together, how fast and how efficiently those chips talk to each other increasingly determines both performance and power consumption — and strategic investors like SK Hynix and Samsung clearly see that as worth securing a stake in.
2. **Chipmakers are investing directly in the infrastructure around their own products**, a pattern also visible in [Axelera AI's Series D](/blog/axelera-funding) for edge accelerators — strategic corporate capital is flowing into adjacent AI-chip infrastructure, not just the chips themselves.
3. **It's part of a broader week of chip-infrastructure capital**, alongside [Amazon's $7.5 billion acquisition of Decart AI](/blog/decart-ai-acquisition) — both deals point to the same underlying theme: getting more performance out of AI hardware, whether through software optimization or physical interconnects, is now a well-funded category of its own.
