**LetsEnhance is the best all-around AI image upscaler in 2026 at $9/month, but the right pick depends on whether you're printing photos, restoring old ones, working for free, or scaling AI art — Topaz Gigapixel, Upscayl, and VanceAI each win in a narrower lane.** Here's how the six leading options compare on price, workflow, and output quality.

## The short answer

For most people who want a strong online tool without committing to desktop software, **LetsEnhance** (from $9/month) covers the widest range of needs with seven specialized models and batch processing up to 20 images. If you're a working photographer editing inside Lightroom or Photoshop, **Topaz Gigapixel** (from $33/month or $399/year) is the professional standard — desktop, local GPU processing, and plugin integration, though it costs meaningfully more. If budget is the only constraint, **Upscayl** is completely free and open-source, runs fully on your own machine with no uploads, and produces clean output with no watermarks. **VanceAI** is the flexible middle ground with pay-per-credit pricing (from $4.95 per 100 credits) and specialized models for photos, anime, and text. **HitPaw FotorPea** (~$21.99/month or $129.99 lifetime) bundles upscaling with restoration and colorization if you want more than just resolution. And **ON1 Resize AI** (~$50–$120 one-time) is the print specialist, with margin presets and paper-specific output for anyone whose end goal is a physical print rather than a screen.

## LetsEnhance

LetsEnhance (from $9/month) is built as a general-purpose upscaler with real range: seven specialized AI models covering everything from "Gentle" enhancement to a dedicated "Old Photo" mode, batch processing of up to 20 images at once, and output control down to 300+ DPI for print-ready files. That combination of breadth and print-readiness is what makes it the strongest single online option rather than a niche tool.

**Pros:** widest model selection of any tool here; batch processing saves real time; DPI control makes it usable for print, not just web.
**Cons:** subscription required for meaningful volume; not a desktop app, so it depends on uploads.
**Who it's for:** anyone who wants one flexible online tool that handles most upscaling scenarios well, without the cost of professional desktop software.

## Topaz Gigapixel

Topaz Gigapixel (from $33/month or $399/year) is the professional choice, and the pricing reflects that positioning. It's desktop software that processes locally on your own GPU rather than uploading to a server, integrates directly as a plugin into Lightroom and Photoshop, and offers nine distinct AI models with unlimited rendering once you're subscribed.

**Pros:** local GPU processing means no upload wait and better privacy; direct Lightroom/Photoshop integration fits an existing photo workflow; unlimited rendering on subscription.
**Cons:** meaningfully more expensive than the online alternatives; requires a capable GPU to get the benefit of local processing.
**Who it's for:** working photographers already inside the Adobe ecosystem who upscale regularly enough to justify the cost.

## Upscayl

Upscayl is the standout free option: fully open-source, cross-platform across Windows, macOS, and Linux, and it processes entirely on your own machine using Real-ESRGAN models. There's no watermark, no account, and no data leaving your computer, which makes it the obvious choice for anyone upscaling sensitive or personal images.

**Pros:** completely free; fully local processing for privacy; no watermarks; actively maintained open-source models.
**Cons:** lacks the specialized model variety and batch tooling of paid services; output quality depends more on your own hardware.
**Who it's for:** privacy-conscious users and anyone who wants a genuinely free, no-strings tool rather than a freemium trial.

## VanceAI

VanceAI (from $4.95 per 100 credits) sits between the free and premium tiers with a pay-as-you-go model. It offers both a web tool (upscaling up to 8x) and a desktop app that claims up to 40x scaling, plus specialized models tuned for photos, anime, and text, with denoising built in.

**Pros:** credit-based pricing avoids a recurring subscription if your usage is occasional; dedicated anime and text models are a genuine differentiator; both web and desktop options.
**Cons:** credit pricing can add up for high-volume users compared to a flat monthly plan; the higher scaling claims (up to 40x) are desktop-only.
**Who it's for:** occasional users, and anyone specifically upscaling anime art or AI-generated images who wants a model tuned for that content.

## HitPaw FotorPea

HitPaw FotorPea (~$21.99/month or $129.99 lifetime) goes beyond pure upscaling by combining it with restoration, colorization, and denoising in one package, targeting 4K/8K output. The lifetime license option is notable since most competitors here are subscription-only.

**Pros:** bundles multiple restoration features beyond upscaling; lifetime license avoids ongoing subscription cost.
**Cons:** monthly price is comparable to LetsEnhance without clearly exceeding it on pure upscaling quality.
**Who it's for:** anyone restoring old or damaged photos who wants upscaling, colorization, and denoising in a single tool rather than stitching several apps together.

## ON1 Resize AI

ON1 Resize AI (~$50–$120 one-time license) is built specifically for print output, with margin presets and paper-specific presets that online upscalers generally don't offer. It scales up to 10x and comes with a 30-day free trial with no feature restrictions, which is a meaningfully longer evaluation window than most competitors give you.

**Pros:** one-time purchase instead of a subscription; print-specific presets are genuinely useful if that's your end goal; generous unrestricted trial.
**Cons:** narrower focus than the general-purpose tools — less useful if your output is purely digital/web.
**Who it's for:** photographers and print shops whose final output is a physical print, not a screen.

## How to choose

Match the tool to where the image ends up. If you're printing, ON1 Resize AI's margin and paper presets solve a problem the other tools don't address. If you're a professional photographer already working in Lightroom or Photoshop, Topaz Gigapixel's plugin integration and local processing justify the higher cost. If cost is the deciding factor, Upscayl gives you a genuinely free, private option with no watermark. If you need occasional upscaling without a subscription, VanceAI's credit system is more flexible than a monthly plan. And if you want one tool that handles most everyday cases well — including old-photo restoration — LetsEnhance or HitPaw FotorPea are both reasonable general-purpose picks.
