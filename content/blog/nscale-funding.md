**Nscale is raising $3.5 billion in pre-IPO financing at a $14.6 billion valuation**, announced September 4, 2026, led by **Nvidia and Third Point**. It's a striking figure for a UK-based compute provider that only closed its Series B a few months earlier — and it comes as Nscale prepares for a public listing.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's backing it, and why it matters.

## What is Nscale?

Nscale is a **UK-based AI infrastructure provider** that builds and operates compute services and data center capacity for large-scale AI workloads. Founded in **2024** by **Josh Payne and Nathan Townsend**, the company has grown quickly by supplying GPU capacity to major AI labs — its customers include **Anthropic**.

In plain terms: Nscale doesn't build AI models, it builds and rents out the physical compute those models run on. As demand for training and inference capacity has outstripped what the big hyperscalers can supply on their own, independent providers like Nscale have stepped in to fill the gap.

## The raise: $3.5B pre-IPO round

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $3.5 billion
- **Round:** Pre-IPO
- **Valuation:** $14.6 billion
- **Announced:** September 4, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Nvidia, Third Point
- **Co-leads:** Aker ASA, 8090 Industries

For context, Nscale raised a **$155 million Series A in December 2024**, then a **$1.1 billion Series B in March 2026** led by Aker ASA. Going from a $155 million Series A to a $14.6 billion pre-IPO valuation in under two years is an extraordinary trajectory, even by 2026's standards, and it's underpinned by a **$103 billion contracted revenue backlog** — including a reported **$45 billion** tied to the Anthropic relationship alone, with the remainder spread across other hyperscaler and enterprise customers.

That backlog figure is the real story behind the valuation. Most young infrastructure companies are valued on projected demand; Nscale is being valued on contracts it says are already signed. A pre-IPO round of this size, rather than a straightforward IPO, also suggests Nscale wanted to lock in committed capital and validate its valuation with sophisticated private investors before facing public market pricing.

## Who invested in Nscale?

The pre-IPO round is led by **Nvidia** and **Third Point**, with **Aker ASA** and **8090 Industries** co-leading.

- **Nvidia**
- **Third Point**
- **Aker ASA**
- **8090 Industries**

Aker ASA's involvement continues from its role leading Nscale's Series B, giving it a growing stake across multiple rounds. Nvidia's participation is notable for a different reason: it's investing directly in a company whose core business is buying and deploying Nvidia GPUs at scale.

## What Nscale will do with the money

Nscale says the funds will go toward **AI data center expansion and operations** to meet demand from Anthropic and other hyperscalers, as well as **preparation for an IPO**.

That combination — building out physical capacity while readying the company for public markets — reflects the scale Nscale is now operating at. A $103 billion contracted backlog isn't something a company can serve without aggressively expanding its data center footprint first.

## Why it matters

Nscale's pre-IPO round is a signal on several fronts:

1. **AI compute buildout is going global, not just American.** A $14.6 billion valuation for a UK infrastructure company shows investor appetite for non-US, non-hyperscaler capacity is real and growing, mirroring the broader rush into [AI data center funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026).
2. **Nvidia is investing in its own customers.** A direct stake in a company that then spends heavily on Nvidia GPUs is a form of vertical integration that's becoming more common across the AI compute stack, and it echoes the dynamics playing out at competitors like [Fluidstack](/blog/fluidstack-funding).
3. **The IPO will be a stress test for compute valuations.** With $103 billion in contracted revenue behind it, Nscale's public debut will show whether capital-intensive infrastructure providers can sustain hypergrowth multiples once they're subject to public market scrutiny.

For a company that priced its Series A at $155 million just two years ago, a $14.6 billion pre-IPO valuation is a reminder of how quickly capital has moved to secure a position in AI compute — and how much is riding on that bet paying off once Nscale lists.
