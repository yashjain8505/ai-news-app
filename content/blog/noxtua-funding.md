**Noxtua raised more than €100 million in a Series C round**, announced September 23, 2026 and led by **C.H. Beck**, a major German legal publisher that now holds a majority stake in the company. The round is a notable bet on "sovereign" AI — systems built and controlled entirely within Europe — for one of the highest-stakes professional domains: the law.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for legal AI in Europe.

## What is Noxtua?

Noxtua is a **sovereign legal AI platform** offering research, analysis and document drafting capabilities. Founded in **2024** by **Dr. Leif-Nissen Lundbæk** as a CMS-backed legal AI spinout, Noxtua's defining feature is that it's powered exclusively by European legal publishers rather than general-purpose foundation models trained on broader, less domain-specific data. Its customers include major law firms **CMS and Dentons**.

## The raise: €100M+ Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** more than €100 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** September 23, 2026
- **Lead investor:** C.H. Beck
- **Co-lead:** MANZ

The most notable detail isn't the dollar amount — it's who's writing the check. C.H. Beck, one of the largest legal publishers in the German-speaking world, is taking a **majority shareholder** position rather than acting as a typical financial investor. That's a different kind of backing than most AI startups get: it ties Noxtua directly to the publisher that controls much of the legal content the platform is trained on and licensed against.

## Who invested in Noxtua?

The round was led by **C.H. Beck**, with **MANZ**, an Austrian legal publisher, co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed.

## What Noxtua will do with the money

Noxtua said the funding will support **pan-European expansion into additional markets** and continued **platform development**. Having its two lead investors both be legal publishers rather than generalist venture funds gives Noxtua a built-in path to that expansion — publisher relationships in new markets can translate into distribution and content licensing that a pure AI startup would otherwise have to build from scratch.

## Why it matters

Noxtua's raise reflects a few dynamics worth watching in enterprise legal AI:

1. **Legal publishers are becoming AI investors, not just AI targets.** Rather than being disrupted by legal AI startups or simply licensing content to them, C.H. Beck is taking a controlling stake — a sign that established publishers see more value in owning a stake in the AI layer than staying arm's-length from it.
2. **"Sovereign" AI is a real product differentiator in regulated markets, not just marketing language.** As European enterprises weigh GDPR compliance and data-residency requirements, a platform built exclusively on European legal sources — rather than a general-purpose model adapted for legal use, the approach taken by competitors like [Harvey](/blog/harvey-funding) — has a clear pitch for law firms and in-house teams operating under EU rules.
3. **Legal AI funding is concentrating around domain expertise.** High-stakes, precedent-driven work like legal research and drafting rewards specialized data and publisher relationships over general model capability, which is shaping how capital is flowing into the category.
