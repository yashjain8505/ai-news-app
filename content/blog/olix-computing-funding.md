**OLIX Computing raised $312 million in a Series B round at a $3.3 billion valuation**, announced August 3, 2026 and led by the **UK Sovereign AI Venture Fund**. It's the largest semiconductor funding round Europe has produced to date, and it comes with government money attached — a detail that says as much about the current AI chip race as the dollar amount does.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what OLIX is building.

## What is OLIX Computing?

OLIX Computing builds **Optical Tensor Processing Units** — AI inference chips that use **photonic interconnects instead of electrical wires** to move data. The pitch is that light-based interconnects can sidestep the energy consumption and latency limits that electrical wiring runs into as AI chips scale. The company was founded in **2024**, raised a Series A that same year, and expects its first customer chips to ship in the second half of 2027.

Data movement, not raw compute, is one of the biggest bottlenecks in modern AI chip design — shuttling data between memory and processing units over electrical wiring burns significant power and adds latency as chips get denser. Photonic interconnects, which move data as light instead of electrical signal, are a longstanding research idea for getting around that limit; OLIX's bet is that it can be the company that makes the approach commercially viable for AI inference specifically.

## The raise: $312M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $312 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $3.3 billion
- **Announced:** August 3, 2026
- **Lead investor:** UK Sovereign AI Venture Fund

OLIX raised its Series A in 2024, the same year it was founded, and has now reached a $3.3 billion valuation with this Series B — a fast climb for a company that hasn't shipped a customer chip yet. First customer silicon isn't expected until H2 2027, meaning investors are pricing the round almost entirely on the technology's promise and the strength of its backers rather than commercial traction.

## Who invested in OLIX Computing?

The **UK Sovereign AI Venture Fund** led the round, with participation from:

- **Arm**
- **Hudson River Trading**
- **Reed Hastings**
- **Fundomo**

Arm's involvement is notable given its own position in chip architecture, and Reed Hastings' participation as an individual investor adds a high-profile name to a round that's otherwise dominated by strategic and government-linked capital. Hudson River Trading's presence as a quantitative trading firm is a less obvious inclusion, and points to how compute-hungry firms outside the traditional AI industry are also positioning themselves early around next-generation chip architectures.

## What OLIX Computing will do with the money

OLIX says the funding will go toward **chip development and manufacturing** — the capital-intensive work of taking a novel photonic architecture from lab prototype to shippable silicon, with first customer chips targeted for H2 2027.

That roughly three-year gap between founding and first customer silicon is typical for chip startups, where design, fabrication and validation cycles run far longer than software development timelines. It also means OLIX's backers are committing capital years ahead of any revenue, which is part of why the round size and valuation lean so heavily on the credibility of the approach and the strategic weight of investors like Arm and the UK's sovereign fund.

## Why it matters

1. **A government venture fund is now directly underwriting chip startups.** The UK Sovereign AI Venture Fund leading this round is a concrete example of sovereign compute becoming a strategic government priority, not just a private-capital story — a trend likely to keep shaping [AI chip startup funding through 2026](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026).
2. **Photonics is a genuinely different bet than the dominant electrical-chip approach.** Most AI inference chip startups, like [Etched with its Sohu chip](/blog/etched-funding), are still racing on conventional electrical architectures. OLIX is betting on different physics entirely — if photonic interconnects deliver on the energy and latency promise, it could open a path around the ceilings electrical chips are starting to hit as models scale.
3. **Pricing a pre-revenue chip startup at $3.3 billion shows how much capital is chasing alternatives to Nvidia.** With no shipping product until 2027, this valuation reflects investor appetite for any credible attempt to diversify the AI inference chip supply chain.
