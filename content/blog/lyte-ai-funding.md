**Lyte AI raised $165 million in a Series C round at a $1.6 billion valuation**, announced September 2, 2026 and led by **Maverick Silicon**. The round comes as physical AI — robots that operate in the real world — emerges as one of the hottest investment categories of 2026.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's backing it, and what it signals for robot perception technology.

## What is Lyte AI?

Lyte AI designs **multimodal sensors, processors, and software** that let robots perceive and navigate their physical environments accurately. Founded in **2021** by **Alexander Shpunt, Arman Hajati and Yuval Gerson**, the company is shipping to customers in **inspection, logistics, and manufacturing robotics**.

The founding team's background is notable: Shpunt co-founded **PrimeSense**, the company acquired by Apple whose technology underpins both the Kinect and Face ID. Lyte's broader team includes ex-Apple engineers who built that foundational 3D sensing technology, now applied to robot perception rather than consumer devices.

## The raise: $165M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $165 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $1.6 billion
- **Announced:** September 2, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Maverick Silicon

For context, Lyte AI raised a **$107 million Series B in January 2026**. Total funding now stands at **$272 million**, roughly eight months after that prior round.

## Who invested in Lyte AI?

The Series C was led by **Maverick Silicon**, with additional participation from:

- **Fidelity Management & Research**
- **Atreides Management**

The presence of Fidelity and Atreides — both asset managers more commonly associated with public-market and growth-stage investing — alongside a specialist semiconductor investor like Maverick Silicon points to a broadening investor base for physical AI hardware companies.

## What Lyte AI will do with the money

Lyte AI plans to use the funding for **ramping production and expanding customer deployments** in robotics.

That framing matters: this isn't R&D capital for a still-unproven technology, it's production capital for a company already shipping sensors and processors to real robotics customers in inspection, logistics, and manufacturing.

## Why it matters

Lyte AI's raise fits into a broader pattern reshaping robotics investment in 2026:

1. **Physical AI has moved from research to deployment.** A $1.6 billion valuation tied to production shipments, not just prototypes, suggests investors believe robot perception technology is ready for real-world scale — a shift also visible across the wider [physical AI and robotics funding](/blog/physical-ai-robotics-funding-2026) landscape.
2. **Mobile-era hardware talent is migrating into robotics.** The founding team's history building the sensing technology behind Face ID and Kinect suggests robot perception is now attracting the caliber of engineering talent that once flowed into smartphones.
3. **Perception is becoming a chokepoint layer.** As humanoid and mobile robots move into factories and warehouses, reliable 3D sensing and perception is shaping up as essential infrastructure that multiple robotics companies will depend on, rather than something each one builds in-house.
