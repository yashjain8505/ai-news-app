**Blacksmith raised $45 million in a Series B round at a $550 million valuation**, announced on August 15, 2026 and led by **Peak XV Partners**. The raise bets on a specific bottleneck: as AI writes more and more code, someone has to validate it fast enough to keep up.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the CI/CD market.

## What is Blacksmith?

Blacksmith runs a **continuous integration platform on purpose-built hardware**, designed to accelerate testing and validation of AI-generated code. Founded in **2024**, the company sells the idea that as code generation tools produce more commits faster, the CI pipeline that tests and validates that code becomes the new chokepoint. Its customers include **Supabase, Clerk and Ashby**.

## The raise: $45M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $45 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $550 million
- **Announced:** August 15, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Peak XV Partners
- **Co-leads:** Y Combinator, GV

Blacksmith's customer base grew from 800 to 6,000 companies following its earlier funding, a roughly 7.5x increase that underpins the jump to a $550 million valuation at Series B.

## Who invested in Blacksmith?

The Series B was led by **Peak XV Partners**, with **Y Combinator** and **GV** co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed for this round.

## What Blacksmith will do with the money

Blacksmith says the funding will go toward **scaling its hardware, expanding engineering, and deepening its product** for validating AI-generated codebases. Given that the company's edge is running CI on purpose-built hardware rather than generic cloud compute, hardware scaling is likely the most capital-intensive piece of that plan.

## Why it matters

Blacksmith's raise points to a specific infrastructure squeeze building underneath the AI coding boom:

1. **AI code generation is shifting the bottleneck downstream.** As tools like [Devin and Cursor](/blog/best-ai-code-editors-2026) let developers ship code faster, the constraint moves from writing code to verifying it — CI/CD, which used to be a background process, becomes a limiting factor on how fast teams can actually ship.
2. **Weekly CI job growth of 5–10% is a concrete usage signal.** That kind of week-over-week growth in test volume shows this isn't a hypothetical problem — it's already straining infrastructure at companies adopting AI coding tools at scale.
3. **Specialized hardware is becoming a competitive moat in CI.** Rather than compete purely on software features, Blacksmith is betting that purpose-built infrastructure can out-perform generic cloud CI providers on speed, similar to how [Cognition](/blog/cognition-funding) has bet on owning more of the coding-agent stack rather than sitting on top of general-purpose infrastructure.
