**Moonshot AI raised $3.5 billion in a Series D+ round at a $35 billion valuation**, announced July 29, 2026 and led by **China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund**. The round came in above target, and it lands as Moonshot pushes toward what would be the first Hong Kong IPO by a Chinese frontier AI lab.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for China's AI race with the US.

## What is Moonshot AI?

Moonshot AI is a Chinese AI lab that **develops large language models and multimodal AI systems**. Founded in **2023** by **Yang Zhilin, Zhou Xinyu and Wu Yuxin**, the company operates the **Kimi chatbot** and sells API and enterprise AI services built on its own models.

Kimi has become one of the more visible consumer-facing products to come out of China's frontier AI push, competing for attention alongside labs like DeepSeek and Zhipu as Chinese developers race to match — or beat — US model performance. Founders Yang Zhilin, Zhou Xinyu and Wu Yuxin built Moonshot around the idea that a Chinese lab could compete directly at the frontier rather than settling for a fast-follower position, and the company's fundraising pace over the past year suggests investors increasingly agree.

## The raise: $3.5B Series D+

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $3.5 billion
- **Round:** Series D+
- **Valuation:** $35 billion
- **Announced:** July 29, 2026
- **Lead investor:** China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund

Moonshot's valuation has climbed sharply and repeatedly over the past two and a half years. It raised a Series A/B in February 2024 at over $1 billion, led by Alibaba, followed by a Series B extension from Tencent in August 2024. A Series C landed between December 2025 and January 2026 at $500 million, and a Series D in May 2026 brought in $2 billion at a $20 billion valuation. This Series D+ round pushes the valuation to $35 billion — a roughly 75% jump in just two months. Reporting also indicates the round was oversubscribed against an original $1-2 billion target, and that Moonshot is already planning a final pre-IPO round at a reported $50 billion valuation.

## Who invested in Moonshot AI?

The round was led by **China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund**, a state-backed vehicle. No co-leads or additional participants have been publicly disclosed, which is common for late-stage rounds involving Chinese state investment funds where full cap table details aren't released.

## What Moonshot AI will do with the money

Moonshot said the capital will go toward **scaling infrastructure for its Kimi K3 model** and **corporate restructuring ahead of an IPO**. That combination — compute investment paired with pre-IPO housekeeping — signals a company preparing to move from private fundraising to public markets in the near term, with Hong Kong reported as the likely listing venue.

Scaling infrastructure for a specific model generation, rather than a general compute buildout, points to a fairly concrete roadmap: Moonshot needs the training and serving capacity to keep Kimi K3 competitive against both US frontier models and rival Chinese labs, while simultaneously getting its corporate structure into IPO-ready shape. Doing both at once is expensive, which helps explain why the round came in well above its original fundraising target.

## Why it matters

Moonshot's round is a significant marker in the global AI funding landscape:

1. **State capital is backing China's frontier AI push.** A round led by a national investment fund, rather than private venture capital, shows how directly the Chinese government is now funding the country's AI leaders — mirroring the scale of private capital flowing into [the biggest funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
2. **Chinese labs are being priced like frontier competitors.** A $35 billion valuation for Moonshot puts it in the same conversation as other Chinese frontier labs like [DeepSeek](/blog/deepseek-funding), reinforcing that investors see multiple credible Chinese challengers to US model makers, not just one.
3. **An IPO would be a first.** If Moonshot completes a Hong Kong listing, it would be the first Chinese frontier AI lab to go public — a milestone worth watching alongside the broader wave of [AI IPOs expected in 2026](/blog/ai-ipos-2026).
