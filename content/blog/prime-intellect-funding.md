**Prime Intellect raised $130 million in a Series A round at a $1 billion valuation**, announced July 8, 2026 and led by **Radical Ventures**, with **Nvidia Ventures, Intel Capital and Dell** co-leading. The round reached unicorn status at Series A on the back of a reported $100 million annualized revenue run rate — an unusually mature number this early in a company's life.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals about the market for enterprise AI agent infrastructure.

## What is Prime Intellect?

Prime Intellect is a **full-stack platform for enterprises to build, train and deploy their own AI agents**. Founded in **2024**, the company bundles **compute infrastructure, reinforcement-learning frameworks, evaluation tools and a modular marketplace of agent components** into a single platform, letting enterprises own their agent stack rather than depend entirely on a single frontier model provider.

Its customers include **Ramp** and **Zapier** — both companies that have used the platform to build custom agents rather than relying solely on off-the-shelf models from frontier labs. That approach — combining infrastructure, training tooling and a components marketplace in one platform — positions Prime Intellect as a middle layer between raw compute providers and the frontier model APIs enterprises would otherwise default to.

## The raise: $130M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $130 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** $1 billion
- **Announced:** July 8, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Radical Ventures
- **Co-leads:** Nvidia Ventures, Intel Capital, Dell

No prior funding rounds were disclosed, meaning Prime Intellect went straight to a $1 billion valuation at Series A — a sign of how much revenue and customer traction the company had already built before this round closed.

## Who invested in Prime Intellect?

The round was led by **Radical Ventures**, with **Nvidia Ventures**, **Intel Capital** and **Dell** co-leading — a lineup that pairs a specialist AI venture firm with strategic capital from major compute and hardware players. Additional participants included:

- **Iconiq**
- Angel investors from **Perplexity, Box and Cognition**

The presence of Nvidia Ventures, Intel Capital and Dell as co-leads, rather than just participants, points to strategic interest from infrastructure providers in how enterprises will actually deploy AI agents at scale.

## What Prime Intellect will do with the money

Prime Intellect said the funding will go toward **expanding its engineering teams** and **scaling compute infrastructure** — the two things a company selling agent-building infrastructure needs most as customer usage grows. For a platform whose core promise is letting enterprises train and run their own agents, compute scarcity is a direct constraint on growth; more engineers and more compute translate fairly directly into being able to onboard more customers without service degradation.

## Why it matters

Prime Intellect's raise is a useful data point for where enterprise AI spending is heading, and for how quickly infrastructure companies can be priced once they can point to real usage rather than just a roadmap:

1. **Revenue at Series A is becoming a real differentiator.** A reported $100 million annualized revenue run rate at the Series A stage is a striking number, and it's part of why investors were willing to price the company at $1 billion this early — a dynamic increasingly visible across [the biggest funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
2. **Enterprises want an alternative to frontier-model lock-in.** Prime Intellect's key proof point — an agent Ramp built on its platform reportedly beat frontier models on accuracy while costing significantly less — speaks directly to a real customer fear: being fully dependent on one AI lab's pricing and roadmap.
3. **Picks-and-shovels AI infrastructure keeps attracting capital.** Prime Intellect's bet on agent-building infrastructure echoes the thesis behind companies like [Baseten](/blog/baseten-funding) in inference — investors backing the tooling layer that sits underneath AI applications, not just the applications themselves. It's also a clear sign of momentum in the broader [AI agents funding](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) category.
