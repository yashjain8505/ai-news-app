**Wispr Flow raised $280 million in a Series B round at a $2.0 billion valuation**, announced on August 17, 2026 and led by **Menlo Ventures**. The raise comes less than a year after the company's Series A, and it lands as voice becomes one of the more contested interaction layers in enterprise AI.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the voice AI market.

## What is Wispr Flow?

Wispr Flow is a **voice AI platform** built for dictation, meeting transcription and speech model development, with a specific focus on working reliably in noisy, real-world conditions rather than the clean, quiet environments most voice tools are trained for. Founded in **2019**, the company positions itself around the idea that speech is a faster, more natural input method than typing — provided it can hold up outside a quiet office.

## The raise: $280M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $280 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $2.0 billion
- **Announced:** August 17, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Menlo Ventures
- **Co-leads:** Notable, NEA, Neo Ventures

For context, Wispr Flow's Series A raised $25 million in November 2025. With this round, total funding reaches $361 million — a fast progression from seed-stage voice startup to a $2 billion company in under a year, reflecting how quickly capital has moved toward the spoken-interaction layer of AI.

## Who invested in Wispr Flow?

The Series B was led by **Menlo Ventures**, with **Notable**, **NEA** and **Neo Ventures** co-leading. The company has not disclosed additional participants in this round.

## What Wispr Flow will do with the money

Wispr Flow says the funding will go toward expanding its **voice model, called Canto**, along with meeting transcription and speech-first interface development. That points to a company trying to move beyond dictation as a single feature and toward owning more of the underlying voice model stack itself, rather than building on top of third-party speech models.

## Why it matters

Wispr Flow's raise is a useful signal for where voice AI stands in 2026:

1. **Voice is becoming an enterprise interface, not just a consumer convenience.** As [AI agents](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) proliferate across workplace tools, speech is emerging as a faster way to direct them than typing — and Wispr Flow's focus on noisy, real-world conditions targets exactly the gap that keeps voice tools from working outside quiet home offices.
2. **The company is showing real commercial traction, not just funding momentum.** Wispr Flow reports 150%+ quarterly revenue growth, which is the kind of usage curve that typically justifies a valuation jump from a $25 million Series A to a $2 billion Series B in under a year.
3. **Competition in spoken interfaces is still relatively thin.** Compared to the crowded field of text-based AI agent startups, voice remains a frontier with fewer well-funded, dedicated players — which is part of why investors moved quickly here.

Wispr Flow's jump also fits a broader pattern of AI-native productivity tools attracting outsized rounds in 2026, alongside companies like [June](/blog/june-ai-funding) building at the interaction layer rather than the model layer.
