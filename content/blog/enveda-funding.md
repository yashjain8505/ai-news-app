**Enveda raised $311 million in a Series E round at a $2 billion valuation**, announced September 23, 2026 and led by **Catalio Capital Management**. The round is one of the larger biotech raises of the year, and it backs a company betting AI can speed up drug discovery by mining nature instead of relying solely on chemical synthesis.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI-driven drug discovery.

## What is Enveda?

Enveda uses **AI to identify drug molecules in plants and microbes**, then predicts their structure and potential as treatments. Founded in **2019** by **Viswa Colluru**, the company applies machine learning to natural compound libraries — a space that has historically relied on slow, manual chemistry — to find candidates faster and cheaper than traditional discovery methods.

## The raise: $311M Series E

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $311 million
- **Round:** Series E
- **Valuation:** $2 billion
- **Announced:** September 23, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Catalio Capital Management
- **Co-leads:** ICONIQ, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Surveyor Capital, Durable Capital Partners

For context, Enveda's previous Series round, roughly a year earlier, valued the company at approximately $1 billion. Doubling its valuation over that span puts Enveda among the better-funded AI drug discovery startups, at a time when investors are still sorting out which approaches to computational biology will actually produce approved medicines.

## Who invested in Enveda?

The round was led by **Catalio Capital Management**, with **ICONIQ, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Surveyor Capital and Durable Capital Partners** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **T. Rowe Price Investment Management**
- **Digitalis Ventures**
- **Alderline Group**
- **Baillie Gifford**
- **Premji Invest**
- **Lux Capital**

The mix of crossover public-market investors — T. Rowe Price, Baillie Gifford — alongside biotech-focused venture funds suggests investors are treating Enveda less like an early-stage bet and more like a company preparing for eventual public-market scrutiny.

## What Enveda will do with the money

Enveda said the funding will go toward advancing **three clinical-stage AI-discovered medicines into later development phases**, as well as bringing additional nature-derived candidates into human trials. That marks a shift from discovery work into the far more expensive and regulated business of clinical development, where capital needs climb sharply as programs move through trial phases.

## Why it matters

Enveda's raise says something about where AI-driven biotech is heading in 2026:

1. **AI is being trusted with the expensive part of drug discovery, not just the early screening.** Funding clinical-stage programs, rather than only earlier-stage molecule identification, shows investors believe Enveda's AI-discovered candidates are worth carrying through costly human trials.
2. **Nature-derived discovery is carving out its own lane.** Rather than competing head-on with protein-structure-prediction approaches like [Chai Discovery's](/blog/chai-discovery-funding), Enveda is building around plant and microbial compound libraries — a different bet on where the next wave of AI-discovered medicines will come from.
3. **Crossover capital is flowing into AI biotech ahead of likely public offerings.** The presence of public-market investors alongside venture firms, a pattern also seen at [QuantHealth](/blog/quanthealth-funding), points to biotech AI companies positioning for later liquidity events as their pipelines mature.
