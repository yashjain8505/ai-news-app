**ASI raised $325 million in a Series C round at a $3 billion valuation**, announced on October 21, 2026. The company builds AI systems aimed at some of the highest-stakes infrastructure problems around — air traffic control and power grid management — and the size of this round suggests investors are willing to price that kind of mission-critical AI at a premium.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what it signals for AI's expansion into critical infrastructure.

## What is ASI?

ASI builds **AI systems for critical infrastructure optimization**, with applications spanning air traffic control and grid management. Rather than targeting consumer or enterprise software use cases, ASI's work sits in domains where failures have immediate, physical-world consequences — flight safety and power reliability — which also tends to mean longer sales cycles and heavier regulatory scrutiny.

No founding year, founders, or named customers have been disclosed for the company.

## The raise: $325M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $325 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $3 billion
- **Announced:** October 21, 2026

No prior funding rounds have been disclosed, so it's unclear how this $3 billion valuation compares to ASI's earlier rounds. The fact that this is already a Series C, though, suggests the company has been building and raising for some time before reaching this scale.

## What ASI will do with the money

ASI says the funding will go toward **scaling operations and customer deployment** — language that points to a company moving from pilots or early contracts toward broader rollout with government agencies, utilities, or infrastructure operators.

## Why it matters

1. **Governments and utilities are becoming real AI customers, not just pilots.** A $325 million Series C for infrastructure-focused AI suggests investors see a credible path to large, durable contracts with public-sector and utility buyers — markets that move slowly but pay well once trust is established.
2. **Critical infrastructure is an emerging frontier for AI capital.** ASI's raise sits alongside a broader trend of capital flowing into [AI data center and infrastructure funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026), extending the infrastructure thesis beyond compute itself into the physical systems — power, transportation — that AI increasingly needs to help manage.
3. **National security and energy framing raises the stakes — and the scrutiny.** AI systems touching air traffic control and grid management will likely face more regulatory oversight than typical enterprise software, which could slow ASI's path to revenue even as it accelerates its fundraising.
