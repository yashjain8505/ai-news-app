**Biren Technology raised $515 million in a post-IPO share placement, announced October 8, 2026 and led by China International Capital Corporation and UBS**, at a valuation of **$9.41 billion** as of August 2026. It's Biren's second major capital raise in under a year, coming nine months after its Hong Kong IPO, and it lands squarely in the middle of China's push to build domestic alternatives to export-restricted US GPUs.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, the backers, and why it matters for the global AI chip race.

## What is Biren?

Biren Technology is a **Chinese fabless semiconductor company** that designs AI graphics processing units and full-stack computing solutions for AI training, inference, and high-performance computing. Founded in **2019** by **Lingjie Xu (CEO, a former AMD GPU architect), Zhang Wen (formerly of SenseTime), and Jiao Guofang (formerly of Huawei)**, the company ships its BR300 series GPUs to customers including **China Mobile, China Telecom, and SenseTime**.

## The raise: $515M post-IPO placement

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $515 million
- **Round:** Post-IPO share placement
- **Valuation:** $9.41 billion (as of August 2026)
- **Announced:** October 8, 2026
- **Lead investors:** China International Capital Corporation (CICC), UBS

This follows Biren's **Hong Kong IPO on January 2, 2026**, which raised $717 million, plus earlier rounds including a roughly $150 million Series A in June 2024, $280 million in government-backed funding in October 2022, and $207 million in pre-IPO funding in June 2025. Including the IPO and this latest placement, Biren has now raised well over $1.5 billion since founding.

## Who invested in Biren?

The placement was led by **China International Capital Corporation** and **UBS**. No additional participants were disclosed.

The involvement of CICC, one of China's largest state-linked investment banks, alongside UBS reflects the mix of domestic policy support and international capital markets access that's characterized Biren's funding history — including its earlier government-backed round.

## What Biren will do with the money

Biren laid out a specific split for the proceeds: **70% toward supply chain procurement and production**, **20% toward R&D and software ecosystem development**, and **10% toward general corporate and working capital**. That heavy weighting toward procurement and production reflects the realities of chip manufacturing under export controls — securing supply chain capacity is as much a strategic priority as the chip design itself.

The growth backing that spending is real: Biren posted **207% revenue growth in 2025**, reaching **1.03 billion CNY**, with its BR300 series GPUs optimized for DeepSeek and other large language models.

## Why it matters

Biren's raise is a clear marker of how the AI chip race is playing out geopolitically:

1. **China is building domestic GPU alternatives under real constraints.** US export restrictions on advanced chips have made companies like Biren central to China's push for computing self-sufficiency — chips optimized for domestic LLMs like DeepSeek, in the same ecosystem that produced [DeepSeek itself](/blog/deepseek-funding).
2. **Revenue growth is funding the buildout, not just investor enthusiasm.** A 207% year-over-year revenue jump gives Biren's raise a different character than many AI chip startups burning cash on pure R&D — it's scaling production to meet demand it already has.
3. **Public markets and state-linked capital are now intertwined with chip strategy.** Going public in January and returning to raise more via a share placement nine months later shows Biren treating capital markets as an ongoing tool for funding production scale-up, not a one-time liquidity event.
