**Velaura AI raised $110 million in a Series A round at a valuation of $1 billion or more**, announced in August 2026 and led by **Seligman Ventures**. That's a striking outcome for a Series A — most companies don't reach unicorn status until several rounds later — and it points to how hungry investors are for anything that addresses the power problem in AI infrastructure. Here's the full breakdown.

## What is Velaura AI?

Velaura AI develops **power-efficient semiconductor technology for AI data center chips**. Rather than chasing raw compute performance alone, the company is focused on the energy side of the equation — building silicon designed to do more AI processing per watt, which matters as data centers increasingly run into power constraints rather than pure compute limits.

## The raise: $110M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $110 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** $1 billion or more
- **Announced:** August 2026
- **Lead investor:** Seligman Ventures

A $1 billion-plus valuation on a Series A is unusual — it signals that investors are pricing in significant conviction about Velaura's technology and team well before the company has had multiple rounds to prove itself in the market. No prior funding rounds were disclosed, which is consistent with this being an early, if unusually large, raise.

## Who invested in Velaura AI?

The round was led by **Seligman Ventures**, with participation from:

- **Capricorn Investment Group**
- **Samsung Catalyst Fund**
- **StepStone**
- **Maverick Silicon**

The presence of **Samsung Catalyst Fund** and **Maverick Silicon** — both investors with direct ties to the semiconductor industry — is notable. It suggests Velaura's backers include not just financial investors but players with a strategic interest in chip manufacturing and design.

## Why it matters

1. **Power efficiency is now the binding constraint on AI infrastructure scale.** As data centers hit limits on available electricity rather than available chips, silicon-level power efficiency determines what can actually be deployed — not just raw compute capacity. That's the same dynamic driving investment across the broader [AI data center funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026) landscape.
2. **A $1B+ Series A is a sign of how fast conviction can build in AI chips.** Investors are willing to price in massive upside at an early stage when they believe a technology addresses a structural bottleneck — here, the energy cost of running AI at scale — rather than waiting for multiple rounds of proof points.
3. **Strategic semiconductor investors are showing up earlier.** Samsung Catalyst Fund's participation alongside more traditional venture money reflects how chipmakers are increasingly investing directly in the startups shaping next-generation AI silicon, a pattern also visible across other deals in [AI chip startup funding](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026).
