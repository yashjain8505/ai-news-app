**Thinking Machines Lab raised $5 billion in a Series A round at a $40 billion valuation**, announced November 6, 2026 and led by **Accel**, with existing investors also participating. The valuation has more than tripled in about 16 months for a company that's barely a year and a half old — one of the steepest climbs of any AI lab in 2026.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why a $40 billion price tag for such a young company is drawing scrutiny as well as capital.

## What is Thinking Machines Lab?

Thinking Machines Lab builds **frontier AI models** along with **tools for model fine-tuning and adaptation**. The company was **founded in 2025**, making this Series A one of the fastest valuation run-ups for any AI startup on record relative to its age.

## The raise: $5B Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $5 billion
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** $40 billion
- **Announced:** November 6, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Accel
- **Participants:** Existing investors

For context, Thinking Machines Lab's seed round was **$2 billion at a $12 billion valuation**, led by Andreessen Horowitz in July 2025. Going from $12 billion to $40 billion in roughly 16 months — more than tripling — puts this round among the fastest valuation climbs of any AI lab in 2026, seed round included.

## Who invested in Thinking Machines Lab?

The Series A was led by **Accel**, with **existing investors** — including Andreessen Horowitz, which led the company's seed round — also participating.

## What Thinking Machines Lab will do with the money

Thinking Machines Lab hasn't disclosed a specific breakdown of how it will spend the new capital. Given that its business is building frontier models and fine-tuning tooling, most of that spend would typically go toward compute, training runs, and research talent — but that's inference from the company's focus, not a stated plan.

## Why it matters

1. **The valuation is tripling faster than the company's track record can justify on paper.** A $40 billion price tag for a company founded in 2025 with minimal disclosed revenue raises real questions about whether private AI funding has become detached from fundamentals, rather than tracking proven commercial traction.
2. **It reflects investor conviction that frontier-model builders capture the most value in AI**, even as other parts of the stack see cooler appetite — this round lands amid reports that AI infrastructure startups are having a harder time raising, a split in sentiment also visible in how differently investors are pricing companies like [Together AI](/blog/together-ai-funding) versus pure frontier-model labs.
3. **It adds another data point to how large private AI rounds have become in 2026** — alongside the other mega-rounds tracked in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), Thinking Machines Lab's raise shows that even very young labs can command tens of billions in valuation if investors believe they're building the next frontier model.
