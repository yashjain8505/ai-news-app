**Scarlet raised $50 million in a Series B round**, announced on October 17, 2026 and led by **Headline Growth**, with **Notion Capital** co-leading. The round funds a company sitting at a chokepoint that most AI-in-healthcare conversations skip past entirely: getting regulatory approval fast enough to actually ship.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why regulatory speed has become its own competitive category.

## What is Scarlet?

Scarlet is an **AI-native medical device certification body** that accelerates regulatory approval for AI and software used in healthcare devices, cutting typical certification timelines by **12-18 months or more**. Rather than building AI products itself, Scarlet certifies other companies' software-as-a-medical-device products against regulatory standards, faster than traditional notified bodies.

Founded in **2021** by **James Dewar and Jamie Cox**, Scarlet's customers include **Flo Health, DeepHealth, NHS England, Skin Analytics, Heidi Health, Tandem Health, Flok Health, Augmedit, Fraiya and Lucida Medical**.

## The raise: $50M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $50 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** October 17, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Headline Growth
- **Co-lead:** Notion Capital

Scarlet didn't disclose a valuation for this round. For context, the company previously raised a **$17.5 million Series A in June 2024**, led by Atomico, which followed a **£6 million seed 2 round in August 2022**, led by Creandum, and an initial **£1.5 million seed round in January 2021** from Kindred Capital and Entrepreneurs First. Atomico, Creandum, Kindred Capital and Entrepreneurs First all returned as participants in the Series B.

## Who invested in Scarlet?

The round was led by **Headline Growth**, with **Notion Capital** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Atomico**
- **Creandum**
- **Kindred Capital**
- **Entrepreneurs First**

Every prior-round investor returned for this Series B, a sign of continued conviction as the company has scaled its regulatory footprint.

## What Scarlet will do with the money

Scarlet plans to use the funding to **expand its regulatory certifications, deepen its market presence in the U.S. and Asia-Pacific regions, and accelerate product update cycles** for its software-as-a-medical-device customers.

That expansion matters because healthcare AI companies increasingly want to ship product updates on a weekly cadence, while traditional certification bodies can take 6-8 months per review cycle — a mismatch Scarlet is positioned to close as it adds regions and designations.

## Why it matters

1. **Scarlet occupies a structural chokepoint in healthcare AI.** Regulatory approval sits between every healthcare AI company and its market, and Scarlet says it holds unique designations — EU Notified Body #3022 for MDR, FDA Third Party, and MHRA Approved Body — making it the only SaMD/AIaMD specialist among 52 EU notified bodies, a dynamic also shaping growth at other [healthcare AI companies tracked for funding](/blog/healthleap-funding).
2. **Institutional capital is treating regulatory speed as a durable moat.** A $50 million Series B from Headline Growth suggests investors see Scarlet's regulatory arbitrage — faster approvals than incumbents — as a defensible business, not a temporary advantage.
3. **Faster certification compounds across the whole healthcare AI ecosystem.** As more AI-powered medical devices and software reach clinicians, companies that can get through regulatory review faster — the kind of problem [AI scribes and clinical tools](/blog/suki-funding) also face — gain real time-to-market advantage over competitors stuck in slower approval queues.
