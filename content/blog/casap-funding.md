**Casap raised $56 million in a Series B round**, announced on October 28, 2026 and led by **Base10 Partners**. The round lands as banks and fintechs hand more of their back-office fraud and dispute work to AI agents instead of human analysts.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals about AI's push into regulated financial back-offices.

## What is Casap?

Casap is an **agentic AI platform that automates payment disputes, chargebacks and first-party fraud detection**, and is increasingly moving into collections, customer complaints and claims management for financial institutions. Founded in **2023** by **Shanthi Shanmugam and Saisi Peter**, the company sells into a part of banking that's notoriously manual: sorting through disputed transactions, deciding which ones are legitimate fraud, and fighting chargebacks — work that's traditionally done by large teams of human analysts.

Casap counts **more than 100 financial institutions** — banks, credit unions, fintechs and payment processors — as customers.

## The raise: $56M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $56 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** October 28, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Base10 Partners

Casap's Series A was $25 million, closed in August 2025 and led by Emergence Capital — which returned to participate in this Series B roughly 14 months later. The company says revenue has grown 10x since that round.

## Who invested in Casap?

The Series B was led by **Base10 Partners**. Additional participants included:

- **Experian**
- **Synchrony**
- **Emergence Capital**
- **Lightspeed Venture Partners**
- **Primary Venture Partners**
- **SoFi**

The participant list is notable for how many are strategic financial players rather than pure venture funds — Experian, Synchrony and SoFi are all companies that operate directly in the credit, fraud and lending space Casap sells into.

## What Casap will do with the money

Casap said the funding will go toward **advancing its AI platform, expanding beyond fraud into collections and complaint management, developing claims handling, and targeting adjacent back-office operations** at banks and credit card companies. That roadmap tracks a familiar vertical-AI playbook: land in one painful, well-defined workflow, prove it works, then expand into neighboring workflows that share the same customer and the same underlying operational pattern.

## Why it matters

1. **AI agents are displacing humans in regulated back-offices, not just chat support.** Casap says it achieves a 96% chargeback win rate against a roughly 60% industry average, cut dispute resolution time from 90 days to 8, and automates 73% of cases while keeping human oversight — concrete operational numbers that are harder to dismiss as hype, and comparable to the production-grade claims coming out of [other enterprise AI infrastructure companies](/blog/baseten-funding).
2. **10x revenue growth since the Series A signals real product-market fit**, not just investor enthusiasm, in a sector — financial services compliance and fraud — where institutions are typically slow and risk-averse about adopting new technology.
3. **Vertical AI agents are proving they can unlock operational leverage in highly regulated industries**, a pattern showing up across [AI agent funding in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) as more startups find footholds in specific, high-friction enterprise workflows rather than trying to be general-purpose.
