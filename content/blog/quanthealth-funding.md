**QuantHealth raised $45 million in a Series B round**, announced August 6, 2026 and led by **Qumra Capital**, with **Pitango and Sanofi Ventures** participating. The round backs an AI platform that simulates clinical trials before they happen — a bet on cutting the enormous cost of late-stage drug failures.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why simulated clinical trials matter to pharma.

## What is QuantHealth?

QuantHealth builds an **AI platform that simulates clinical trials** to predict outcomes and improve drug development success rates before a trial ever begins. Rather than waiting for a real-world trial to reveal whether a drug will succeed or fail, the platform models how a trial is likely to play out — giving pharmaceutical companies a way to catch problems earlier and cheaper. The company's customers include **12 of the world's top 20 pharmaceutical companies**, a strong signal of enterprise validation for a healthcare AI startup at the Series B stage.

## The raise: $45M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $45 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** August 6, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Qumra Capital

QuantHealth hasn't disclosed a post-money valuation or details of its prior funding rounds, so the trajectory of its valuation over time isn't public.

## Who invested in QuantHealth?

The Series B was led by **Qumra Capital**. Additional participants included:

- **Pitango**
- **Sanofi Ventures**

The presence of Sanofi Ventures — the corporate venture arm of a major global pharmaceutical company — alongside financial investors like Qumra Capital and Pitango points to strategic, not just financial, interest in QuantHealth's technology from within the pharma industry itself.

## What QuantHealth will do with the money

QuantHealth said the funding will go toward **scaling adoption and R&D**. The company points to **90% predictive accuracy across more than 600 simulated trials** as evidence its models are ready for broader use — a track record that likely underpins its push to expand within its existing base of top-20 pharma customers and beyond.

## Why it matters

1. **AI is attacking one of pharma's most expensive problems.** Late-stage clinical trial failures can cost hundreds of millions of dollars and delay therapies for years, so a platform with a credible predictive track record has an unusually clear return-on-investment case to make to enterprise buyers.
2. **Deep-pocketed pharma incumbents are already customers, not just prospective ones.** Having 12 of the top 20 pharmaceutical companies as customers at the Series B stage is a level of enterprise traction that's uncommon this early, and it puts QuantHealth in a strong position relative to the broader wave of AI healthcare funding tracked across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **Strategic corporate investment signals durable demand.** Sanofi Ventures' participation suggests at least one major pharma company sees enough value in QuantHealth's simulations to back it directly, rather than simply licensing the technology as an arms-length customer.
