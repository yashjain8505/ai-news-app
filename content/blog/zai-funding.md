**Z.ai raised $5 billion in Hong Kong**, announced September 13, 2026, through a **share placement combined with $3 billion of zero-interest convertible bonds**, implying a post-money valuation of roughly **HK$60 billion or more**. The raise makes Z.ai one of the most aggressively capitalized AI companies in China, and it comes just two months after the company's previous multi-billion-dollar raise.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what it signals about China's AI buildout.

## What is Z.ai?

Z.ai develops and commercializes the **GLM (General Language Model)** family of open-weights large language models, operating both a **platform API business** and a **model-as-a-service** offering for more than **100,000 enterprise clients**, many running on Chinese-made accelerators. Founded in **2019** by **Tang Jie and Li Juanzi**, the company counts **Aurora Mobile Limited** among its enterprise customers.

In plain terms: Z.ai builds foundation models and sells access to them, much like OpenAI or Anthropic in the U.S., but with a specific focus on serving Chinese enterprises on domestic hardware — a positioning that matters as U.S. export controls push Chinese AI companies to adapt their infrastructure around homegrown chips.

## The raise: $5B share placement and convertible bonds

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $5 billion
- **Structure:** Share placement plus $3 billion in zero-interest convertible bonds
- **Valuation:** Implied post-money valuation of roughly HK$60 billion or more
- **Announced:** September 13, 2026

Z.ai went public in Hong Kong in **January 2026**, raising **$558 million at a $6.6 billion valuation**. It followed that with a **$4 billion share placement in July 2026**, and now this **$5 billion** raise in September — meaning Z.ai has completed two multi-billion-dollar capital raises within about two months of each other, on top of its IPO. That pace is unusual even among the best-funded AI companies globally, public or private. Earlier private rounds included a **$410 million raise in December 2024** at roughly a **$3 billion valuation**, and a **$342 million Series B3 in September 2023** at a **$1 billion valuation**.

## Who invested in Z.ai?

The raise drew participation from **Hong Kong investors**, who were **not individually disclosed** in connection with the share placement and convertible bond sale.

## What Z.ai will do with the money

Z.ai said it will direct **60% of the proceeds to R&D and computing infrastructure** for next-generation GLM models and fully self-training systems, **15% to expansion and M&A**, and the remaining **25% to capital structure optimization and working capital**.

The scale of that R&D and infrastructure commitment lines up with Z.ai's growth: its platform API business grew **292.6% in 2025**, reaching roughly **RMB 1.7 billion ($238 million) in annualized recurring revenue** by March 2026. Sustaining that growth curve requires continually scaling compute — particularly as the company adapts its stack around domestic accelerators rather than the Nvidia chips U.S. rivals rely on.

## Why it matters

Z.ai's raise is a significant marker in the global AI capital race:

1. **China's AI leaders are now tapping public markets at U.S.-style speed.** Z.ai became one of the first major Chinese foundation model companies to go public, and it's since raised two multi-billion-dollar rounds in as many months — a pace of capital formation that mirrors what [Positron AI](/blog/positron-ai-funding) and other U.S. infrastructure companies are doing, just funneled through Hong Kong's public markets instead of private venture rounds.
2. **Domestic-chip adaptation is expensive, and investors are pricing that in.** Z.ai's emphasis on Chinese-made accelerators reflects the broader reality that Chinese AI companies are racing to build competitive infrastructure without reliable access to the most advanced Nvidia hardware — a constraint that shows up directly in how much capital they need to raise.
3. **Revenue growth, not just model quality, is driving the valuation.** A 292.6% jump in platform API revenue is the kind of concrete commercial traction that's increasingly what separates well-funded AI labs from ones struggling to raise, in China as much as in [the biggest funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026) elsewhere.
