**Cohere raised C$7 billion in a Series E round at a C$35.7 billion valuation**, announced October 29, 2026 and led by **Schwarz Group**, with the **Government of Canada** co-investing directly. It's Canada's largest-ever private financing round, and the fact that a national government is a direct co-investor — not just a customer or policy backer — makes this one of the more unusual rounds of 2026.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what a government co-investment signals about the AI funding landscape.

## What is Cohere?

Cohere builds and deploys **large language models for enterprise and government use**, with a particular emphasis on **data sovereignty, on-premises deployment, and regulatory compliance**. Founded in **2019** by **Aidan Gomez, Nick Frosst and Ivan Zhang**, the company has positioned itself as an alternative to U.S.-hosted AI platforms for customers — especially governments — that need to keep their data and models within their own borders.

## The raise: C$7B Series E

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** C$7 billion
- **Round:** Series E
- **Valuation:** C$35.7 billion
- **Announced:** October 29, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Schwarz Group
- **Co-investor:** Government of Canada

The scale of the round alone — C$7 billion — makes it the largest private financing event in Canadian history. What sets it apart from other mega-rounds elsewhere in AI isn't just the size, but who's writing part of the check.

## Who invested in Cohere?

The round was led by **Schwarz Group**, with the **Government of Canada** co-investing directly as part of the same raise. No other participants were disclosed.

## What Cohere will do with the money

Cohere says the funding will go toward **expanding its model capabilities and capacity for enterprise and government adoption**, specifically in markets that require domestic, sovereign AI ownership rather than reliance on foreign-hosted models. That framing lines up with the company's existing pitch: governments and regulated industries that want AI capability without handing their data to a foreign cloud provider.

## Why it matters

1. **A direct government co-investment is a geopolitical signal, not just a financing detail.** As the U.S. and China compete to own frontier AI, this round shows an allied nation choosing to directly back a homegrown lab rather than depend entirely on foreign suppliers for a technology its government considers strategic.
2. **Sovereign AI is becoming its own investment thesis.** Cohere's focus on on-premises, compliant deployment puts it in similar territory to [Mistral AI](/blog/mistral-funding), a European lab built around the same pitch of offering an alternative to U.S.-controlled AI platforms — both are benefiting from a wave of capital that wants AI exposure without U.S. vendor lock-in.
3. **It raises the bar for how large an AI financing round outside the U.S. can get.** At C$35.7 billion, Cohere's valuation now sits alongside the broader surge in AI infrastructure funding tracked across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), even as independent infrastructure players like [Together AI](/blog/together-ai-funding) also raise at multi-billion-dollar valuations.
