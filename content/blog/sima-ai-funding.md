**SiMa.ai raised $150 million in a Series C round at a $1.45 billion valuation**, announced September 28, 2026 and led by **Fidelity Management & Research Company and Amplify**. The round lands as investors keep pouring money into "physical AI" — AI that has to run inside a robot, drone, or car rather than in a data center.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for on-device inference.

## What is SiMa.ai?

SiMa.ai, founded in **2018** by **Krishna Rangasayee**, builds **chips and software that let robots, drones, and cars run AI inference locally** rather than relying on cloud processing. That distinction matters for machines that need to react in real time — a drone avoiding an obstacle or a car reading a road sign can't afford the round-trip delay of sending sensor data to a remote server and waiting for an answer.

The company's customer list leans industrial and deep-tech rather than consumer: **Bosch, Emerson, Micron, Synopsys, and TRUMPF** are among the names using SiMa.ai's hardware and software to add on-device AI to their own products. That's a telling customer mix — these are companies that build physical equipment (industrial automation, semiconductor tools, factory machinery) rather than apps, which is exactly the kind of buyer that needs inference running inside a device rather than in a data center three states away.

## The raise: $150M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $150 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $1.45 billion
- **Announced:** September 28, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Fidelity Management & Research Company, Amplify

SiMa.ai's valuation trajectory has been steep. Its **Series B raised $85 million at a $960 million valuation** in July 2025 — so the Series C valuation is up roughly **50%** in just over a year, and total funding raised to date now stands at **$500 million**. That kind of jump, in a company that doesn't sell a consumer app or a chatbot, signals how much institutional capital is chasing the "edge AI" thesis specifically.

The round was led by **Fidelity Management & Research Company** and **Amplify**. Other participants included:

The mix is notable: alongside venture firms like Dell Technologies Capital and Alter Venture Partners, the round pulled in asset managers and public-market-adjacent investors — Fidelity, AllianceBernstein, Baron Capital, J.P. Morgan — plus a state pension-linked investor in the State of Michigan. That's the kind of investor base that typically shows up ahead of a public listing, not just another private round.

## What SiMa.ai will do with the money

SiMa.ai says the funding goes toward two things. First, **scaling Palette Neat**, the company's agentic software environment for Physical AI — the tooling layer that developers use to build and deploy on-device AI applications. Second, **funding next-generation hardware** slated to launch in **H1 2028**, which the company says will deliver **1,000 dense TOPS of compute**, spanning new machine learning IP, chiplets, and systems-on-chip.

Both of those are multi-year bets: software platforms take time to mature into developer defaults, and a chip roadmap stretching to 2028 means SiMa.ai is raising now to fund R&D years ahead of the product actually shipping. Targeting 1,000 dense TOPS of on-device compute, with its own machine learning IP, chiplets, and systems-on-chip, suggests the company is betting that future humanoid robots and autonomous systems will need to run substantially larger models locally — not just lightweight perception tasks — which is a much harder engineering problem than today's edge AI chips are built for.

## Why it matters

1. **Physical AI is being priced as a massive market, not a niche one.** SiMa.ai frames the opportunity around robots, drones, and autonomous vehicles as a $50 trillion addressable market — and investors backing a $1.45 billion valuation for an edge-inference chipmaker suggests that framing is landing with institutional capital, not just founders' pitch decks.
2. **On-device inference solves a problem cloud AI can't.** For humanoids, cars, and drones, latency and bandwidth aren't inconveniences — they're safety constraints. SiMa.ai's bet is that the chip layer for real-time, local AI becomes as essential as GPUs are for training, a theme that runs through other [AI chip funding](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026) this year.
3. **The round sits inside a broader wave of capital flowing into physical and robotics AI**, where [funding has accelerated sharply in 2026](/blog/physical-ai-robotics-funding-2026) as investors look past software-only AI plays toward companies building the compute that makes embodied AI possible.
