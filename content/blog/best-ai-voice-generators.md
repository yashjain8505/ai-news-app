**ElevenLabs is the best all-around AI voice generator for quality and voice cloning, but the right pick depends on what you're building: HeyGen for avatar video, Google Cloud TTS for enterprise scale, Murf AI for corporate training narration, and PlayHT for real-time voice agents.** Here's the actual pricing and what each tier gets you.

## The short answer

If you just need natural-sounding voiceover or voice cloning and don't have enterprise scale requirements, **ElevenLabs** is the default choice — its $5/month Starter plan (30K characters) is enough for casual use, and the $99/month Pro tier (500K characters) covers most content businesses. If you need a **finished video** with a lip-synced avatar rather than just audio, **HeyGen**'s Creator plan ($24-29/month) does that natively. If you're deploying voice at real scale inside an existing cloud stack, **Google Cloud TTS** charges per character with no subscription lock-in. **Murf AI** is worth a look specifically for corporate training and e-learning, where its pitch/speed controls matter more than raw voice realism. And if you're building **real-time voice agents**, **PlayHT**'s sub-300ms latency is the differentiator.

| Tool | Best for | Pricing | Key strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| ElevenLabs | General voiceover & voice cloning | $5/mo (30K chars) to $99/mo (500K chars) | Voice cloning included at every paid tier |
| HeyGen | Avatar video with voice | $24-29/mo (Creator) | Lip-synced AI avatars in 175+ languages |
| Google Cloud TTS | Enterprise scale | $4/1M chars (WaveNet) to $30/1M chars (Chirp 3 HD) | Enterprise SLA, pay-per-use |
| Murf AI | Corporate training/e-learning | $19/mo | 200+ voices with granular pitch/speed control |
| PlayHT | Real-time voice agents | $39/mo | 900+ voices, 142 languages, <300ms latency |

## ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs' pricing scales cleanly with usage: **$5/month** gets you 30,000 characters on the Starter plan, and **$99/month** gets you 500,000 characters on Pro, with voice cloning bundled in at every paid tier rather than gated behind an enterprise deal.

**Pros:** widely regarded as the highest voice quality in the category; voice cloning included, not an upsell; predictable per-character pricing.
**Cons:** heavier usage (long-form audiobooks, high-volume production) pushes you toward the higher tiers or enterprise pricing quickly.
**Who it's for:** anyone who wants the best general-purpose voice quality, from solo creators to content teams.

## HeyGen

HeyGen's Creator plan, at **$24-29/month**, is priced for a different job than the others on this list: it outputs a **finished video**, not just audio, with AI avatars lip-synced to the generated voice across 175+ languages.

**Pros:** skips the step of pairing separate voice and video tools; strong language coverage for localized video content.
**Cons:** if you only need audio (podcasts, IVR, audiobooks), you're paying for video capability you won't use.
**Who it's for:** teams making explainer videos, localized marketing content, or training videos who need a talking avatar, not just a voice.

## Google Cloud TTS

Google Cloud TTS is priced entirely differently from the rest of this list — per character, with no monthly subscription. **WaveNet** voices run $4 per million characters, **Neural2** runs $16 per million, and the newest **Chirp 3 HD** tier runs $30 per million characters, backed by an enterprise SLA.

**Pros:** cheapest option at low-to-moderate volume; scales with actual usage rather than a flat plan; enterprise SLA for teams that need it.
**Cons:** requires cloud setup and API integration rather than a simple consumer dashboard; per-character billing means costs can surprise you at high volume without monitoring.
**Who it's for:** engineering teams already on Google Cloud who want TTS as an API, not a standalone product.

## Murf AI

At **$19/month**, Murf AI offers 200+ voices with fine-grained pitch and speed controls — a feature set aimed less at cutting-edge realism and more at giving corporate training and e-learning creators precise control over pacing and tone.

**Pros:** affordable mid-tier pricing; granular voice controls suit scripted, instructional content well.
**Cons:** not the tool to reach for if photorealistic conversational voice quality is the priority — that's ElevenLabs' strength.
**Who it's for:** corporate L&D teams and e-learning producers who need consistent, controllable narration.

## PlayHT

PlayHT's **$39/month** plan includes 900+ voices across 142 languages, but its standout feature is **real-time voice agents with under 300ms latency** — built for use cases where the voice has to respond live, not just narrate a pre-written script.

**Pros:** largest voice and language library on this list; latency low enough for live conversational agents.
**Cons:** priced above Murf for a feature (real-time latency) that non-agent use cases won't need.
**Who it's for:** teams building voice agents, IVR systems, or any product where the AI voice needs to respond in real time.

## Which plan should you pick

Start from the output you need, not the voice quality alone. If you need **audio only** and want the best general quality, ElevenLabs' $5/month Starter plan is the cheapest reasonable entry point. If you need a **finished video** with an avatar, HeyGen is the only tool here that does that end-to-end. If you're an **engineering team** building at scale inside existing cloud infrastructure, Google Cloud TTS's per-character pricing avoids paying for a subscription tier you don't need. If your content is **scripted training material**, Murf's controls are worth the $19/month over ElevenLabs' raw quality. And if you're building something that talks back in **real time**, PlayHT's latency is the deciding factor, not price.
