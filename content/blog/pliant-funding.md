**Pliant raised $80 million in a Series C round**, announced in November 2026 and led by **Seaya Nova and Speedinvest**. The round opens up the company's B2B payments infrastructure directly to AI agents, betting that autonomous software will soon need to move money just as often as humans do.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why a payments infrastructure company is racing to plug into the Model Context Protocol.

## What is Pliant?

Pliant is a **B2B payments platform** offering modular infrastructure — card issuing, payment accounts and FX — built specifically so **AI agents can execute payments through the Model Context Protocol**, with permissions and audit trails baked in. Founded in **2020** by **Malte Rau and Fabian Terner**, the company has spent six years building the regulated plumbing that sits underneath corporate payments, and is now extending that plumbing to agents acting on a company's behalf.

Its customers include **Salabam Solutions, Doctari Group, Everydays, Sportissimi, Candis and Klippa**.

## The raise: $80M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $80 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** November 2026
- **Lead investors:** Seaya Nova, Speedinvest

Pliant's path to this round has been steady rather than explosive: a $28 million Series A in 2023, a €18 million (~$19 million) Series A Extension in April 2024, and a $40 million Series B in April 2025. Including this Series C, the company has now raised more than $240 million across seven rounds, and says ARR has surpassed $100 million.

## Who invested in Pliant?

The Series C was led by **Seaya Nova** and **Speedinvest**. Additional participants included:

- **Illuminate Financial**
- **Motive Partners**
- **Molten Ventures**

## What Pliant will do with the money

Pliant said the funding will support **expansion into the US and Canadian markets, entry into additional geographies, and acceleration of its AI roadmap** to connect agents directly to financial infrastructure. For a company whose whole business is being the licensed, auditable layer behind corporate payments, geographic expansion and agent-readiness are really the same bet: more markets, and more kinds of buyers — including software, not just people — moving money through its rails.

## Why it matters

1. **Someone has to be the regulated layer beneath agentic payments.** As AI agents start initiating real transactions, the licensing, permissioning and audit-trail requirements don't go away — they move underneath the agent. Pliant is positioning itself as that layer, a plumbing problem similar to the kind [other AI infrastructure companies](/blog/baseten-funding) are solving at different layers of the stack.
2. **ARR above $100 million at Series C shows this isn't speculative.** Seven rounds and $240 million+ raised over six years is a company that has been building steadily rather than riding a single hype cycle — the AI-agent angle is additive to an already-working payments business, not a pivot.
3. **It's part of a broader wave of infrastructure built for AI agents as customers**, a theme running through [agentic AI funding in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) as more capital flows toward companies that treat autonomous software as a first-class user of financial systems.
