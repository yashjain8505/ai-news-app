**Vesta raised $30 million in a Series B round**, announced on October 8, 2026 and led by **Conversion Capital**, with **Pennymac and New American Funding** co-leading. What makes this round unusual isn't the size — it's that three of Vesta's own mortgage-lender customers put their own money into the deal.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why mortgage lending — one of the most automation-resistant corners of finance — is starting to bend to AI agents.

## What is Vesta?

Vesta builds **AI agents that automate mortgage origination, underwriting and due diligence**, handling approximately **40% of tasks** on its platform while maintaining human oversight and regulatory compliance. Mortgage lending has long resisted automation because it's complex, heavily regulated, and carries real financial stakes if an agent gets something wrong.

Founded in **2020** by **Mike Yu and Devon Yang**, Vesta's customers include **Pennymac, New American Funding, NBKC Bank and FirstKey Mortgage** — all of which also back the company financially.

## The raise: $30M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $30 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** October 8, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Conversion Capital
- **Co-leads:** Pennymac, New American Funding

Vesta didn't disclose a valuation for this round. For context, the company previously raised a **$30 million Series A in January 2022**, led by Andreessen Horowitz with Zigg Capital, Conversion Capital and Bain Capital Ventures, following a seed round in November 2020. Both Andreessen Horowitz and Zigg Capital returned as participants in the Series B, alongside Conversion Capital, which stepped up from co-investor to lead.

## Who invested in Vesta?

The round was led by **Conversion Capital**, with **Pennymac** and **New American Funding** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **NBKC Bank**
- **Citi Ventures**
- **FirstKey Mortgage**
- **Navitas Capital**
- **Parker89**
- **Andreessen Horowitz**
- **Zigg Capital**

Pennymac, New American Funding, NBKC Bank and FirstKey Mortgage are all customers of Vesta's platform as well as investors in this round — a dual relationship that's uncommon outside of deeply embedded enterprise software bets.

## What Vesta will do with the money

Vesta plans to use the funding to **scale AI agent deployment across its lender networks, improve its product for complex underwriting scenarios, and expand international regulatory compliance** to support European operations under EU AI Act requirements.

The underlying momentum: Vesta says its customers originate **over $100 billion in loans annually** on its platform, and the company has grown revenue **12x year-over-year**.

## Why it matters

1. **Customers investing in their own vendor is a strong trust signal.** When Pennymac, New American Funding and NBKC Bank — lenders that actually use Vesta's agents in production — put their own capital into the round, it suggests they see the technology as core infrastructure, not an experiment, a pattern echoed across the broader [AI agents funding landscape in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
2. **Mortgage lending is one of the hardest domains for AI agents to crack, and Vesta appears to be cracking it.** The sector combines complexity, heavy regulation and high financial stakes — exactly the mix that has historically defeated automation attempts — yet Vesta's agents are already handling 40% of tasks with human oversight intact.
3. **Regulated finance is becoming a template for AI agent deployment, not a no-go zone.** Vesta's approach of maintaining human oversight and full audit trails while expanding to meet EU AI Act requirements for high-risk creditworthiness systems offers a model other regulated industries, including parts of [healthcare AI](/blog/general-medicine-funding), are likely to follow.
