**Vinci raised $250 million in a Series B round at a $1.5 billion valuation**, announced October 6, 2026 and led by **Advent, Temasek and Xora Innovation**, with **Eclipse, Khosla Ventures and Madrona** co-leading. It's one of the largest rounds yet for a company sitting squarely in the chip-design tooling layer — the unglamorous infrastructure that has to work before any AI chip can ship.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why physics simulation for chips is suddenly commanding billion-dollar valuations.

## What is Vinci?

Vinci builds **AI software that simulates physics for chip and hardware design**, predicting thermal, vibration, and electromagnetic behavior before a chip goes into manufacturing. Catching these problems in simulation rather than after fabrication saves chipmakers enormous amounts of time and money — a bad thermal profile discovered after tape-out can mean months of redesign and millions in wasted manufacturing runs.

The company was founded in **2024** by **Hardik Kabaria** and **Sarah Osentoski**, and has started with a specific focus: thermal simulation for AI chips, where heat dissipation is one of the hardest constraints on packing more compute into a given footprint. Vinci has said it plans to expand its simulation capabilities from thermal behavior into vibration and electromagnetics as well.

## The raise: $250M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $250 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $1.5 billion
- **Announced:** October 6, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Advent, Temasek, Xora Innovation
- **Co-leads:** Eclipse, Khosla Ventures, Madrona

Vinci's previous round was a **$46 million seed in December 2025**. Going from a $46 million seed to a $250 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation in under a year is an extraordinarily fast climb — roughly a 5.4x jump in round size in about ten months, for a company that is barely two years old.

## Who invested in Vinci?

The round was led by **Advent**, **Temasek**, and **Xora Innovation**, with **Eclipse**, **Khosla Ventures**, and **Madrona** co-leading. No additional participants beyond the lead and co-lead investors were disclosed for this round.

The mix is notable: Temasek is a sovereign wealth fund, Advent is a large private equity firm, and Khosla Ventures and Madrona bring deep venture roots in enterprise software and semiconductors. That combination of patient capital and specialist venture backing suggests investors expect Vinci's simulation tooling to be a long-term infrastructure bet rather than a quick flip.

## What Vinci will do with the money

Vinci hasn't disclosed a specific breakdown of how the $250 million will be spent. What is known is the company's product roadmap: it is starting with thermal simulation for AI chips and plans to extend into vibration and electromagnetic simulation next — each a distinct physics domain that requires its own modeling approach, and each a step toward Vinci becoming a full-stack simulation layer for chip design rather than a single-purpose tool.

## Why it matters

1. **Chip design tooling is becoming its own gold rush.** EDA and simulation software are bottleneck infrastructure — no one can ship a chip without running the simulations first — and Vinci's $1.5 billion valuation reflects how much value investors now place on anything that can accelerate chip design, a theme echoed across other hardware bets tracked in [AI chip startup funding in 2026](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026).
2. **The speed of the valuation jump is itself a signal.** A roughly 5x increase in round size within ten months, for a two-year-old company, shows how compressed the funding timeline has become for infrastructure that sits in the critical path of AI chip supply — demand to simply keep up with Nvidia and its competitors is accelerating how fast capital moves.
3. **Expanding beyond thermal simulation raises the stakes.** If Vinci can credibly extend from thermal into vibration and electromagnetic modeling, it starts to compete with incumbent EDA tools rather than just complementing them — a much larger addressable market, and likely the thesis behind the size of this round.
