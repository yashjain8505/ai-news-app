**Veridion raised $20 million in a Series A round led by Hoxton Ventures**, announced on September 19, 2026. The round follows an undisclosed seed and funds a company trying to solve a problem that has quietly frustrated sales, research and due-diligence teams for decades: corporate data that's out of date the moment it's published.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what the company plans to do with it.

## What is Veridion?

Veridion runs an **AI-powered business intelligence platform** that continuously crawls and structures public data into a real-time, machine-readable map of **640 million companies worldwide**. Instead of the periodic, manually-compiled datasets that traditional business-data providers sell, Veridion's pitch is that its map stays current because AI is doing the crawling and structuring continuously rather than on a quarterly refresh cycle.

No founders or named customers were disclosed alongside the round. The "machine-readable" framing is the key distinction from a traditional company directory: rather than a static profile a human reads, Veridion's data is structured so that other software — sales tools, risk models, sourcing platforms — can query and act on it directly.

## The raise: $20M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $20 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** September 19, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Hoxton Ventures

Veridion previously raised an undisclosed seed round before this Series A. No valuation was attached to either round, so there's no public trajectory to compare — but going from an undisclosed seed to a $20 million Series A with a single institutional lead is a standard, healthy progression for an early-stage data company that has shown enough traction to justify a bigger check. Series A rounds are typically the point where a company has to show it has paying customers and a repeatable way to acquire them, rather than just a working product.

## Who invested in Veridion?

The round was led by **Hoxton Ventures**. No co-leads or additional participants were disclosed.

## What Veridion will do with the money

Veridion says the funding will go toward **expanding its crawling infrastructure and AI enrichment to cover more sectors and geographies**. That's a direct scaling play: the value of a company database compounds with coverage, so pushing into more industries and more countries makes the existing 640-million-company map more useful to every customer already using it, not just new ones.

It's also a defensible moat, if it works. A dataset that's hard to replicate because it requires constant crawling, cleaning and structuring across hundreds of millions of entities is a harder thing for a competitor to catch up to than a product feature — which is likely part of why investors are willing to fund the unglamorous work of infrastructure expansion rather than a flashier front-end feature.

## Why it matters

1. **Business data is a large, sleepy market ripe for AI disruption.** Established providers like Dun & Bradstreet built their businesses on periodic, manually-verified datasets — a model AI-native crawling can undercut on both cost and freshness. Veridion's bet is that continuous, machine-readable coverage of 640 million companies, including the long tail of small businesses incumbents tend to miss, is worth paying for.
2. **Freshness is the actual product, not just the dataset size.** A lot of AI business-intelligence tools compete on breadth of coverage; Veridion is explicitly building around how often that coverage updates, which matters most for sales prospecting, supply-chain mapping and due diligence where stale data leads directly to bad decisions.
3. **It sits alongside a broader wave of AI-native research and intelligence platforms.** Companies like [AlphaSense](/blog/alphasense-funding) have shown investors will fund AI-driven research and data tools at scale — Veridion is making a similar bet at the earlier end of that market, and for more on where funding is concentrating in 2026, see Wortins' [funding tracker](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
