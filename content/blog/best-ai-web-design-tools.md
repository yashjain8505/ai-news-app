**Pick Framer if you want AI-assisted design with real code export, Webflow if you want an all-in-one builder with hosting included, and Figma if you want a pure design tool with AI plugins layered on top.** None of the three fully automates website building — they each speed up a different part of the process.

## The short answer

The right choice depends on what you're actually optimizing for. **Framer** ($12/month Pro) is built for designers and developers who want AI-assisted design composition that outputs real, usable code — it's the middle ground between design tool and dev tool. **Webflow** ($12-99/month) trades some of that AI-design flexibility for a genuinely all-in-one package: CMS, hosting, and code customization bundled together, so you're not stitching together a design tool and a separate host. **Figma** (free tier, paid $12+/month) is design-first — its AI capabilities come through a plugin ecosystem rather than being built into the core product, which makes it the most flexible but also the tool that requires the most additional setup to get AI-assisted output.

## Framer

Framer combines a visual web design and prototyping platform with AI-assisted design composition, and — its real differentiator — code export that developers can actually use rather than throwaway markup. The free tier gets you started, and Pro at $12/month adds the AI features along with team plan options above that.

**Pros:** AI design generation that produces real code, not just visual mockups; genuinely useful for a designer working alongside developers; competitively priced entry tier.
**Cons:** doesn't include hosting or CMS depth the way Webflow does, so you may still need a separate hosting/backend solution depending on the project.
**Who it's for:** designers transitioning toward code, and teams where design and development need to stay closely connected without a handoff gap.

## Webflow

Webflow ($12-99/month depending on tier) is the most complete package of the three: visual website builder, CMS integration, hosting, and code customization all in one product, with AI features layered into the workflow rather than being the entire pitch.

**Pros:** everything you need to launch a site lives in one product — no separate hosting or CMS to wire up; code customization gives more control than a typical no-code builder.
**Cons:** more setup overhead than Framer or Figma if what you want is quick AI-assisted design iteration rather than a full site build; pricing scales up faster at higher tiers ($99/month at the top end).
**Who it's for:** agencies and teams that want to go from design to a fully hosted, live website without assembling multiple tools.

## Figma

Figma (free tier, paid plans from $12+/month) remains design-first — its AI capabilities live in a plugin ecosystem that generates design variations and content rather than being a native, built-in feature the way Framer's AI design tools are.

**Pros:** the most mature, widely-used design tool of the three, so it fits existing design team workflows without a learning curve; free tier is genuinely usable for base design work.
**Cons:** AI features depend on third-party plugins rather than a unified native experience; doesn't export production code or host anything — it's a design tool, not a website builder.
**Who it's for:** design teams who want AI assistance bolted onto an existing, familiar design workflow rather than switching to a design-to-code or all-in-one platform.

## Other options worth knowing about

Two adjacent tools didn't make the head-to-head but are worth a mention if none of the three above quite fit. **Builder.io** is an AI website builder and headless CMS built for developers who want more automation in a code-first architecture. **Wix ADI** leans further into automation for non-technical users who want a website generated with minimal manual design work — more automation, less control, the opposite tradeoff from Figma.

## What "AI-powered" actually means for each tool

It's worth being precise about what the AI in each product actually does, since "AI web design tool" covers a wide range of capability. In Framer, AI assistance shows up in design composition — generating layout and component suggestions that then export as real, usable code, which is the closest of the three to an AI doing part of the actual build work. In Webflow, AI features sit alongside the visual builder as automation aids rather than being the primary interface — you're still building visually, with AI speeding up specific steps rather than generating whole pages unsupervised. In Figma, AI capability comes entirely through third-party plugins layered onto the core design tool, which means the quality and scope of "AI features" varies by which plugins your team adopts rather than being a single, consistent built-in experience.

That distinction should shape expectations: none of these tools will take a business description and output a finished, launch-ready site without human design and content decisions along the way. What they do is remove specific friction — Framer removes the design-to-code handoff, Webflow removes the build-to-hosting handoff, and Figma's plugins remove some of the blank-page problem in early design exploration. Picking the right tool means picking which friction you actually want removed.

## How to choose

If you're a designer who wants AI help composing layouts and you also need clean code output for developers to build on, **Framer** is the best fit, and its $12/month Pro tier is affordable enough to try without much commitment. If you want to go from design straight to a live, hosted website without juggling separate tools, **Webflow** does more of that end-to-end, at the cost of a steeper price ceiling ($99/month) and more setup. If your team already lives in Figma for design and just wants AI plugins for generating variations and content, there's little reason to switch — **Figma**'s free tier and existing familiarity make it the lowest-friction option, provided you don't need code export or hosting from the same tool. For teams building marketing sites, landing pages, or portfolios specifically, the AI-assistance gap between all three has narrowed enough in 2026 that picking based on hosting needs and code-output requirements matters more than picking based on AI features alone.
