**Freehand raised $75 million in a Series B round led by Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners**, announced August 10, 2026, bringing its total funding to roughly $100 million. The round arrives with a customer list that includes some of the largest companies in the world, which is the more interesting part of this story than the check size itself.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, the backers, and what Freehand is building.

## What is Freehand?

Freehand builds **AI agents for supply chain spend management and back-office operations**, automating the reconciliation and invoice processing work that large enterprises typically handle with armies of analysts and spreadsheets. The company was founded in **2021**.

Its customer roster is the clearest evidence of product-market fit: Freehand counts **Meta, Unilever, Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer, Dunkin and Cardinal Health** among its customers — a mix of tech, consumer goods, pharma and food-service brands that all share the same underlying problem of high-volume, error-prone spend data.

Supply chain spend management is a back-office category that rarely makes headlines, but it's one where the manual overhead is enormous: large enterprises route thousands of invoices and purchase orders through multiple systems, and reconciling them by hand is slow, expensive, and prone to the kind of small errors that compound at scale. Freehand's pitch is to put AI agents directly into that workflow rather than just building another dashboard for humans to review.

## The raise: $75M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $75 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** August 10, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Battery Ventures, NewRoad Capital Partners
- **Participants:** PSP Growth

Freehand's Series A brought its total funding to roughly $100 million before this round, meaning the company has now raised close to $175 million across its history. No valuation was disclosed for this round, but the identity of the backers — established growth investors like Battery Ventures rather than early-stage specialists — is itself a signal that Freehand has moved past the pilot stage.

## Who invested in Freehand?

**Battery Ventures** and **NewRoad Capital Partners** led the Series B, with additional participation from:

- **PSP Growth**

Two leads and a single additional participant is a relatively tight investor group for a $75 million round — a structure more common when a company already has strong inbound interest and doesn't need to syndicate the round across a large number of smaller checks to fill it out.

## What Freehand will do with the money

Freehand says the funding will go toward **product expansion and operational scaling** — building out its agent platform further and growing the team needed to support a customer base that already includes Fortune 500-scale operations. For a company selling into enterprises like Meta and Pfizer, that scaling is less about acquiring new logos and more about deepening how much of each customer's spend workflow the platform actually handles.

## Why it matters

1. **Supply chain is a natural fit for autonomous agents.** Spend management, reconciliation and invoice processing are exactly the kind of high-volume, rules-based, error-prone workflows that AI agents are proving they can handle reliably — it's the same pattern driving [AI agent funding across the market in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
2. **The customer list does the talking.** Meta, Unilever, Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer and Cardinal Health aren't logos companies collect for a press release — landing multiple Fortune 500 customers across different industries suggests Freehand's product works well enough to survive enterprise procurement and security review, not just a pilot.
3. **Blue-chip Series B backers signal durable traction.** Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners are growth-stage investors that typically require real revenue and retention data before committing — their involvement is a stronger signal of traction than the round size alone.
4. **Back-office automation is proving to be a quieter but sturdier AI category.** Unlike categories chasing frontier capability, spend management doesn't need a model breakthrough to work — it needs reliability at scale. That makes it a lower-risk, more defensible niche once a vendor like Freehand has real enterprise deployments to point to.
