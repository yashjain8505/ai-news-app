**River AI raised $1.1 billion in a combined Seed and Series A round at a $5 billion valuation**, announced August 11, 2026 and led by **General Catalyst and AMP PBC**. The round is one of the largest early-stage raises of 2026, arriving just two months after the company emerged from stealth.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, the backers, and what River AI plans to build.

## What is River AI?

River AI is an **AI infrastructure platform** that lets developers and enterprises **train, fine-tune, and deploy custom models** using LoRA fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on open-weight models. Founded in **2026** by **Igor Babuschkin**, the company emerged from stealth in June 2026 — meaning this funding round came together within roughly two months of its public debut.

River AI hasn't disclosed public customers yet, which fits its stage: this is a company being funded on the strength of its founding team and technical thesis rather than existing revenue. The underlying bet is that reinforcement learning on open-weight models — rather than just fine-tuning closed, proprietary ones — gives developers a way to build custom, specialized models they actually own and control, instead of remaining dependent on a handful of closed-model API providers.

## The raise: $1.1B Seed/Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $1.1 billion
- **Round:** combined Seed and Series A
- **Valuation:** $5 billion
- **Announced:** August 11, 2026
- **Lead investors:** General Catalyst, AMP PBC

This is River AI's first public funding round — the company had raised nothing publicly before this, having spent its short life in stealth since being founded earlier in 2026. Going straight from stealth to a $5 billion valuation in one combined seed-and-Series-A round is unusual even by 2026's AI funding standards, and reflects how much weight investors are putting on Babuschkin's background and the reinforcement-learning thesis itself.

Structuring the round as a combined Seed and Series A, rather than two separate raises, also suggests investors moved quickly and didn't need a priced seed round to first establish a baseline valuation — a sign of how much conviction General Catalyst and AMP PBC had going in.

## Who invested in River AI?

The round was led by **General Catalyst** and **AMP PBC**, with participation from:

- **NVIDIA**
- **AMD Ventures**
- **Y Combinator**
- **Temasek**

Having both NVIDIA and AMD Ventures in the same round is rare — the two chipmakers are direct competitors, and their joint participation suggests both see reinforcement-learning infrastructure as a category worth backing regardless of which hardware ultimately wins. Y Combinator and Temasek round out the syndicate, bringing an early-stage accelerator alongside a sovereign wealth fund — a combination that spans nearly the entire spectrum of investor types active in AI right now, from seed-stage specialists to state-backed growth capital.

## What River AI will do with the money

River AI says the funding will go toward **training infrastructure development, personalization products, and hardware development**, as the company expands full-stack — from training through models, the product layer, and hardware.

That "full-stack" ambition is notable for a company this young: rather than picking one layer of the AI stack, River AI is positioning itself to own training, deployment, and hardware simultaneously, which is a capital-intensive bet that only makes sense with this scale of funding behind it. Building "personalization products" on top of that infrastructure suggests River AI eventually wants to sell more than raw training capacity — it wants to ship products that put customized, fine-tuned models directly in front of end users.

## Why it matters

1. **Reinforcement learning is becoming the next frontier for agent capability.** As pre-training gains diminish, RL for fine-tuning and post-training is where labs are looking to squeeze out further performance — and River AI is betting enterprises want to own that process rather than rent it from closed-model providers.
2. **Chipmakers are placing early bets on the RL infrastructure race.** NVIDIA and AMD Ventures both participating in the same round, this early, signals that hardware vendors see reinforcement-learning infrastructure as a strategically important layer worth backing directly, not just selling GPUs into.
3. **The round fits a broader pattern of infrastructure players scaling fast.** River AI's stealth-to-$5B trajectory echoes how quickly [Baseten's valuation climbed](/blog/baseten-funding) earlier in 2026 — both show how compressed the fundraising timelines have become for AI infrastructure companies that can demonstrate technical credibility fast.

River AI's raise is a strong early signal that reinforcement learning — not just bigger pre-trained models — is where a meaningful share of 2026's AI infrastructure capital is heading next.
