**Enigma Robotics raised $71 million in a seed round**, announced on July 27, 2026 and led by **Index Ventures**, with **Ribbit Capital** co-leading. It's an unusually large seed check for a company founded just a year earlier, and it comes with backing not just from institutional VCs but from a roster of individual leaders at the biggest AI labs.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it signals about the race to control robots with AI.

## What is Enigma Robotics?

Enigma Robotics develops **proprietary AI robots and foundation models** aimed at making robot control as intuitive as adjusting a dial, rather than requiring specialized programming or interfaces. Founded in **2025** by **Jonathan Jacobi** and **Gal Niv**, the company is building toward interactive AI robotic systems for both research and enterprise use.

The company is also running a public-facing experiment called **robots.online**, where it says it has more than **100 robots deployed across Israel and California** for people to interact with directly — treating that interaction as a way to generate the data needed to train its foundation models.

## The raise: $71M Seed

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $71 million
- **Round:** Seed
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** July 27, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Index Ventures
- **Co-lead:** Ribbit Capital

This is Enigma's first disclosed funding round — there were no prior rounds. A $71 million seed is a significant amount of capital for a pre-revenue robotics company, reflecting both the capital intensity of building physical robots and the credibility lent by its early backers.

## Who invested in Enigma Robotics?

The seed round was led by **Index Ventures**, with **Ribbit Capital** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Conviction Partners**
- **Individual leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, xAI, Cognition and Wiz**

That last group is notable: rather than institutional funds, it's a set of individuals from the companies currently defining the state of the art in AI. Their personal participation functions as a signal of technical confidence in Enigma's approach to human-robot interaction, on top of the capital itself.

## What Enigma will do with the money

Enigma said the funding will go toward **research and development of its robotic hardware, AI foundation models, and large-scale human-robot interaction platforms** — in other words, both the physical machines and the software that controls them.

The robots.online deployment is central to that plan. By putting robots in front of the public at scale, Enigma is betting that real-world human interaction data is the fastest path to training foundation models general enough to control different kinds of robot hardware, rather than relying on proprietary control interfaces built for a single robot design.

## Why it matters

1. **Foundation models are starting to unbundle robotics from hardware.** Historically, controlling a robot required custom software built for that specific machine. If generalist AI models can control a range of robot architectures, it changes robotics from a hardware-first business into a software-and-data business — closer to how large language models reshaped software.
2. **Individual AI researchers are becoming a funding source, not just a talent pool.** The participation of leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, xAI, Cognition and Wiz in the same round shows how personal capital from the AI boom is now flowing directly into adjacent bets like robotics, alongside traditional VC.
3. **Public data-generation flywheels are an emerging robotics strategy.** Rather than relying solely on lab-controlled training data, Enigma's robots.online approach treats public interaction as a live data pipeline — a strategy that, if it works, could compound faster than closed-lab robotics research.
