**Flow Engineering raised $50 million in a Series B round at a $750 million valuation**, led by **Valor Equity Partners and Atreides Management** with **Sequoia Capital** co-leading. The raise pushes AI automation into one of the industries that has resisted it the longest: physical hardware design.

Here's the full breakdown of the round, who backed it, and what it signals about AI's reach into engineering.

## What is Flow Engineering?

Founded in **2023**, Flow Engineering builds an **AI engineering harness for hardware design**. In practice, that means letting AI agents automatically keep CAD drawings, product requirements, simulation results, and testing data in sync with each other inside a single connected graph — instead of engineers manually reconciling those documents by hand every time a design changes.

Hardware development is notoriously slow precisely because those pieces drift apart: a part gets redesigned, but the requirements doc, the simulation run, and the test plan don't automatically update to match. Flow Engineering's pitch is to close that loop with AI agents rather than spreadsheets and tribal knowledge. Its customer list already includes some of the most demanding hardware builders around: **Anduril, Rivian, Joby Aviation, General Motors PPU, RV Tech (the Rivian/VW joint venture), Stoke Space, Intuitive Machines, and Pacific Fusion**.

## The raise: $50M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $50 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $750 million
- **Lead investors:** Valor Equity Partners, Atreides Management
- **Co-lead:** Sequoia Capital

Sequoia Capital's role here isn't new — it also **led Flow Engineering's Series A in October 2025**, almost exactly a year before this round. Sequoia staying on as a co-lead through two consecutive rounds, rather than handing the baton entirely to new investors, is a reasonably strong signal that an early backer likes what it's seeing in the company's growth.

## Who invested in Flow Engineering?

The Series B was led by **Valor Equity Partners** and **Atreides Management**, with **Sequoia Capital** co-leading. Other participants included:

- Human Capital
- Evantic
- SV Angel
- Odyssey
- EQT

Valor Equity Partners has a track record of backing capital-intensive, physical-world companies (its portfolio includes names like SpaceX and Tesla), which lines up with Flow Engineering's focus on hardware rather than pure software.

## What Flow Engineering will do with the money

Flow Engineering says the funding goes toward **building out the leading AI harness for hardware engineering**, specifically improving review, branching, and evaluation capabilities within its platform — features that map directly to how software engineering teams already work with version control, just adapted for physical product design. The company also plans to **expand controls for complex programs** and **grow its engineering team**.

The emphasis on "complex programs" is worth noting given who's already a customer: defense contractor Anduril and aerospace companies like Joby Aviation and Intuitive Machines run some of the most tightly regulated, high-stakes hardware programs in the country. Serving that tier of customer typically demands more configurability and compliance tooling than a typical enterprise SaaS product.

## Why it matters

1. **Hardware is AI automation's next frontier, not its first.** Software engineering has had AI coding assistants for years; physical product design — CAD, simulation, testing — has largely been left out. Flow Engineering's growth suggests that gap is starting to close, echoing the broader push into [AI agents](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) handling increasingly complex, multi-step technical work.
2. **Marquee defense and aerospace customers are a strong signal.** Anduril, Rivian, and Joby Aviation don't typically adopt unproven tooling for programs where a design error is expensive or dangerous. Their usage suggests Flow Engineering's AI harness is already handling production workloads, not just pilots.
3. **A repeat lead investor across rounds is a vote of confidence.** Sequoia co-leading both the Series A and this Series B, rather than rolling off as new growth investors step in, suggests strong insider conviction in the company's trajectory — a pattern worth watching as more [AI funding rounds](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026) land in 2026.
