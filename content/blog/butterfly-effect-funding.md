**Butterfly Effect, the company behind the AI agent product Manus, raised more than $500 million at a valuation of roughly $4 billion**, announced October 8, 2026 and led by **Boyu Capital and IDG Capital**, with **Tencent, Sequoia China, ZhenFund and CATL** co-leading. It's the company's first funding round since a very public reversal: a roughly $2 billion acquisition by Meta that Chinese regulators forced to unwind just months earlier.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why it's as much a geopolitical story as a financial one.

## What is Manus?

Manus is a **Chinese agentic AI product** built by Butterfly Effect, designed to generate AI agents that automate tasks and workflows rather than simply answer questions. The company says it is generating more than **$100 million in annual recurring revenue**, putting it among the more commercially proven agentic AI products to come out of China. No details on the company's founding date, founders, or specific customers were disclosed.

## The raise: $500M+ at a ~$4B valuation

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** More than $500 million
- **Round:** Unspecified (follow-on financing)
- **Valuation:** Approximately $4 billion
- **Announced:** October 8, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Boyu Capital, IDG Capital
- **Co-leads:** Tencent, Sequoia China, ZhenFund, CATL

The context here matters more than usual. In December 2025, Meta had agreed to acquire Butterfly Effect for roughly **$2 billion**. By April 2026, Chinese regulators had unwound that deal, blocking the sale. This $500 million-plus round, raised entirely from Chinese and Asia-based investors, is the company's first financing since that reversal — and it values Butterfly Effect at roughly double what Meta had agreed to pay for the whole company just months before.

## Who invested in Manus?

The round was led by **Boyu Capital** and **IDG Capital**, with **Tencent**, **Sequoia China**, **ZhenFund**, and **CATL** co-leading. No additional participants beyond the leads and co-leads were disclosed.

The investor list is notably, almost entirely, Chinese or Asia-based — Tencent and CATL in particular are major Chinese corporates, not pure financial investors. That composition lines up with the regulatory backdrop: after Beijing blocked a sale to a U.S. buyer, the capital that stepped in to fill the gap came from inside the same ecosystem that blocked the original deal.

## What Manus will do with the money

Butterfly Effect has not disclosed a specific breakdown of how the funding will be used. What is clear is the business context it's being raised into: a company generating over $100 million in ARR from agentic AI, now independently capitalized after losing its planned acquirer, and needing to fund continued growth of its agent platform without the backing of a large U.S. tech parent.

## Why it matters

1. **This is a case study in AI as a geopolitical asset.** The forced unwinding of Meta's roughly $2 billion acquisition — reportedly due to Chinese regulatory pressure — shows that governments are now willing to intervene directly in cross-border AI deals, treating ownership of agentic AI technology as a matter of national interest rather than a routine M&A transaction.
2. **Capital filled the gap almost immediately.** Going from a blocked $2 billion acquisition to a $4 billion valuation funding round in roughly six months suggests domestic Chinese capital is both willing and able to backstop AI companies that lose access to Western acquirers — a dynamic worth watching alongside other [agentic AI funding rounds in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
3. **Agentic AI is proving it can generate real revenue, not just hype.** Manus's reported $100 million-plus ARR is a meaningful data point in a category — AI agents — that is still mostly unproven commercially outside a handful of companies, and it stands in contrast to how differently AI companies are being valued depending on which country's capital and regulators they answer to, a split also visible in how [Anthropic's IPO](/blog/anthropic-ipo) is being framed in Western markets.
