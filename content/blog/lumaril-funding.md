**Lumaril raised $66 million in a Series A round at a $400 million valuation**, announced in November 2026 and led by **Elad Gil**. The round is a bet that AI can finally crack one of the most stubbornly analog corners of the economy: the factory floor.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why investors are pricing a production-optimization startup at $400 million less than two years after its founding.

## What is Lumaril?

Lumaril builds an **AI platform that connects to factory control systems** to optimize industrial production, capturing operational knowledge from the floor and making real-time adjustments to throughput and yield. Founded in **2025** by **Mark Elliot and Ben Duffield**, the company is going after a problem that has historically resisted software: legacy control systems, physical constraints, and operational knowledge that lives in the heads of veteran plant workers rather than in any database.

Its customers include **Ultra-Poly and The Arthur Companies**.

## The raise: $66M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $66 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** $400 million
- **Announced:** November 2026
- **Lead investor:** Elad Gil

Lumaril's only prior round was a seed of roughly $12 million in January 2025, led by Lux Capital and General Catalyst — both of which returned for the Series A. Going from a seed-stage company to a $400 million valuation inside about two years is an unusually fast climb for a business selling into manufacturing, a sector where sales cycles and deployment timelines are typically long.

## Who invested in Lumaril?

The Series A was led by **Elad Gil**. Additional participants included:

- **Felicis**
- **Koch Disruptive Technologies**
- **Pax Ventures**
- **Lux Capital**
- **General Catalyst**

Lux Capital and General Catalyst backed both the seed and the Series A, while Koch Disruptive Technologies — the venture arm of one of the largest industrial conglomerates in the US — brings a strategic angle that's notable for a company selling directly into manufacturing operations.

## What Lumaril will do with the money

Lumaril said the funding will go toward **accelerating deployment across manufacturing environments** and expanding how it operationalizes the industrial knowledge it captures. The company also prices itself differently than a typical software vendor: it gets paid based on the production it adds for a customer, not on a software license — a model that only works if the product reliably delivers measurable gains on the factory floor.

## Why it matters

1. **Industrial AI has been a hard sell, and that's exactly the opening here.** Manufacturing's legacy systems and physical constraints have kept it behind software and consumer AI in automation — Lumaril's founders previously built Palantir Foundry, giving them direct experience wiring AI into messy enterprise data environments, a pedigree that echoes how [infrastructure-focused AI companies](/blog/baseten-funding) win in hard technical categories.
2. **Performance-based pricing is a real signal of confidence.** Charging for production gains rather than seats or licenses means Lumaril's revenue is directly tied to whether the product works — a structure investors don't back unless they believe the technology already delivers.
3. **A $400 million Series A suggests VCs see industrial AI as a durable, high-margin category**, not a one-off niche — consistent with the broader scramble for [AI infrastructure capital](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026) happening across adjacent sectors in 2026.
