**General Medicine raised $120 million in a Series B round**, announced October 6, 2026 and led by **Andreessen Horowitz**, with **Matrix, VXI Capital, Eli Lilly and Mercy Health** co-leading. No valuation was disclosed, but the lineup of co-investors is itself the headline: a top-tier VC alongside a major pharmaceutical company and a regional health system, backing a startup trying to make healthcare pricing as legible as an e-commerce checkout.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the push toward price-transparent healthcare.

## What is General Medicine?

General Medicine is an **online healthcare marketplace** that lets consumers search, compare, and purchase medications, lab tests, telehealth consultations, and procedures with transparent, upfront pricing. The pitch is straightforward: healthcare pricing in the U.S. is notoriously opaque, and General Medicine is applying the search-and-compare model consumers already use for flights or hotels to medical care.

The company was founded in **2023** by **TJ Parker, Elliot Cohen, and Ashwin Muralidharan**. Parker's track record is notable here — he previously co-founded PillPack, the online pharmacy Amazon acquired in 2018 for roughly $750 million. That exit gave Parker and his team direct experience building consumer-facing healthcare infrastructure at a company large enough to attract a hyperscaler acquisition, and General Medicine is effectively a second attempt at reshaping how people shop for care, this time across a far broader set of services.

## The raise: $120M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $120 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** Not disclosed
- **Announced:** October 6, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Andreessen Horowitz
- **Co-leads:** Matrix, VXI Capital, Eli Lilly, Mercy Health

General Medicine's fundraising history shows a company scaling quickly: a **$10.4 million seed round in August 2023**, followed by a **$32 million Series A in May 2025**, and now a **$120 million Series B** roughly 17 months later. That's a more than 3x jump in round size from Series A to Series B, and it brings the company's total disclosed funding to north of **$162 million** in just over three years.

## Who invested in General Medicine?

The Series B was led by **Andreessen Horowitz**, with **Matrix**, **VXI Capital**, **Eli Lilly**, and **Mercy Health** co-leading. Additional participation came from:

- **BoxGroup**

The presence of Eli Lilly, one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies, and Mercy Health, a multi-state hospital system, alongside traditional venture firms is unusual for a Series B. It signals that both a drugmaker and a health system see strategic value in a marketplace built around pricing transparency — rather than treating General Medicine purely as a financial bet.

## What General Medicine will do with the money

General Medicine says it will use the funding to **expand into additional care categories, enhance the infrastructure needed to standardize services across providers, and deepen its AI-enabled tools for helping consumers shop for clinically appropriate care**. Standardizing services is the harder of those three — comparing prices only works if the underlying service being priced is actually the same thing across different providers, which is rarely true in healthcare today.

1. **Healthcare transparency has investor-backed momentum again.** General Medicine is bringing e-commerce logic — search, compare, buy — to a sector where pricing has historically been deliberately hard to find, and a16z's lead signals renewed VC appetite for consumer-facing healthcare infrastructure, a theme that shows up elsewhere in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).

3. **Founder pedigree matters to the pitch.** TJ Parker already proved the "shop for healthcare like you shop online" model can produce a $750 million outcome at PillPack; investors are betting he can repeat that at a larger scale, and with AI-enabled tools layered on top to help consumers navigate what "clinically appropriate" even means, a problem also being tackled by [AI agent startups raising in 2026](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
