**Pick Ideogram if accurate text in your images matters — logos, posters, UX mockups with real words in them. Pick Leonardo.ai if you need higher resolution, more customization, or built-in image-to-video animation.** Both are capable general-purpose generators; the difference is in what they're optimized for.

## The short answer

Ideogram is the category leader for rendering legible, accurate text inside generated images — something most diffusion models still get wrong. If your work involves logos, packaging mockups, posters, or any image where words need to actually be readable and correctly spelled, Ideogram is the safer choice, and it also generates faster, which matters for quick iteration. Leonardo.ai trades that text specialty for broader capability: it supports higher max resolution (1376x768 at 16:9 versus Ideogram's 1280x720), includes background removal, and — the biggest functional gap between the two — has a built-in AI Video Generator that turns still images into animations, something Ideogram doesn't offer at all. Leonardo is also generally considered the stronger pick for game art and design assets that need more artistic customization.

## Ideogram

Ideogram's defining feature is text accuracy: it renders logos, typography, and in-image copy more reliably than most competitors, which makes it the go-to for anything where the words in the image matter as much as the visuals. The free tier gives roughly 10 credits/week (about 40 images/day on public generations), with Plus at $20/mo and Pro at $60/mo unlocking commercial rights and higher volume. It's also noticeably fast, which makes it a good fit for rapid mockup iteration.

**Pros:** best text rendering in the category by a clear margin; fast generation speeds; simple for quick, focused mockups.
**Cons:** lower max resolution (1280x720) than Leonardo; no video generation at all; paid tiers ($20-$60/mo) start higher than Leonardo's entry paid tier.
**Who it's for:** designers making logos, posters, packaging, or any image where legible in-image text is a hard requirement.

## Leonardo.ai

Leonardo.ai is the more full-featured of the two: its free tier (150 Fast Tokens/day) is more generous than Ideogram's weekly credit allowance, and its paid tiers — Essential ($12/mo), Premium ($30/mo), Ultimate ($60/mo, commercial rights) — undercut Ideogram at the entry level while adding capabilities Ideogram doesn't have, namely image-to-video animation and background removal. It also outputs at a higher max resolution (1376x768) and is generally regarded as stronger for game art and complex design assets.

**Pros:** more generous free tier; cheaper entry-level paid plan; built-in video generation and background removal; higher max resolution.
**Cons:** text rendering inside images is weaker than Ideogram's specialty; more features can mean more surface area to learn for someone who just wants a fast mockup.
**Who it's for:** teams that need customization depth, game/concept art, or want to animate generated images without a separate video tool.

## Speed and simplicity vs. depth

The two tools also diverge in how they feel to use day-to-day, which matters as much as the spec sheet if you're generating dozens of images a week. Ideogram is built for speed and focus: point it at a prompt, get a fast result, iterate quickly. That simplicity is a feature for quick mockups and social posts where you don't want to configure anything. Leonardo.ai asks a bit more of you in exchange for more control — more settings, more model options, and the extra step of learning where video generation and background removal live in the interface. Neither is objectively harder to use, but they're optimized for different working styles: Ideogram for volume and speed, Leonardo for depth and finishing.

## Budget considerations

On raw pricing, Leonardo.ai is the cheaper way in at every tier that matters for testing: its free plan (150 Fast Tokens/day) refreshes daily rather than weekly, so you can evaluate it more thoroughly before paying, and its first paid tier ($12/mo Essential) is well below Ideogram's first paid tier ($20/mo Plus). If budget is the primary constraint and text accuracy isn't a hard requirement, that gap alone may settle the decision. But if your output regularly needs correct in-image text, the cost comparison is somewhat beside the point — a logo with garbled lettering isn't usable at any price, so Ideogram's higher cost buys you output you can actually ship.

## How to choose

If any part of your use case involves words appearing correctly inside the image — a logo, a poster headline, packaging copy — Ideogram's text accuracy makes it the safer default even though its paid plans cost more. If you don't need in-image text and instead want resolution, customization, or the ability to turn a still image into a short animation, Leonardo.ai's broader feature set and cheaper entry tier ($12/mo Essential) make it the better value. Both offer usable free tiers, so the lowest-risk approach is to test each against your actual use case before committing to a paid plan — Leonardo's free tier is more generous for that kind of testing. Many teams that do both logo/text work and general creative work end up keeping a subscription to each, since neither fully replaces the other's specialty.
