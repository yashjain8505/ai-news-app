**Amazon is acquiring Decart AI for $7.5 billion**, a deal announced November 23, 2026, that hands Amazon a team of chip-optimization engineers at a moment when it's racing to make its own AI silicon a real alternative to Nvidia's.

Here's the full breakdown of the deal, who's behind it, and what it signals about the AI chip race.

## What is Decart AI?

Decart AI builds **chip optimization software** that lets AI models run efficiently on chips other than Nvidia's. Founded in **2023** by **Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf, and Moshe Shalev**, the Israeli startup's core pitch is that the hardest part of competing with Nvidia isn't necessarily building better silicon — it's writing the software layer that squeezes real-world performance out of whatever silicon you already have. Amazon plans to use that software to optimize its own **Trainium and Inferentia** processors.

## The deal: $7.5B acquisition by Amazon

The headline numbers:

- **Price:** $7.5 billion
- **Deal type:** Acquisition
- **Announced:** November 23, 2026
- **Acquirer:** Amazon
- **Founded:** 2023

Decart had raised roughly **$456 million** in prior funding across seed through Series C. Its most recent round — a **$300 million Series C** led by **Radical Ventures** in May 2026 — notably included a **$300 million strategic investment from Nvidia**, meaning the chipmaker Decart's software helps companies route around was, just months earlier, one of its own backers. Earlier rounds were led by **Sequoia Capital** and **Benchmark**. A $7.5 billion sale less than two years after founding, and roughly six months after that Series C, is an extraordinarily fast path from startup to nine-figure-plus exit.

## Decart AI's investors before the acquisition

Decart's cap table before the sale included:

- **Radical Ventures** — led the $300 million Series C
- **Nvidia** — strategic investor in the Series C
- **Sequoia Capital** — led early rounds
- **Benchmark** — led early rounds

## What happens next for Decart AI

Amazon said Decart's team and technology will be integrated into **Annapurna Labs**, the custom chip division responsible for Trainium and Inferentia, with the explicit goal of improving performance and cutting training and inference costs on Amazon's own processors. That framing matters: this isn't an acqui-hire for a new product line, it's Amazon buying a shortcut to better utilization of chips it has already spent years and billions designing.

## Why it matters

1. **Amazon is betting on software, not just silicon, to close the gap with Nvidia.** Rather than out-designing Nvidia's chips from scratch, Amazon is acquiring proven optimization software that can extract more performance from Trainium and Inferentia today — a faster and cheaper path than a multi-year hardware redesign.
2. **Nvidia backed a company that a rival later folded into a competing chip stack.** Nvidia's $300 million strategic check into Decart's Series C just months before the Amazon deal is a reminder of how entangled the AI infrastructure investor base has become, even among companies working on opposite sides of the same competitive question.
3. **The optimization layer is becoming as contested as the chips themselves.** Decart's exit lands in the same stretch as a wave of other chip-infrastructure raises — a sign that getting AI models to run efficiently on non-Nvidia hardware, whether through [inference platforms like Baseten](/blog/baseten-funding) or acquisitions like this one, is now viewed as a defensible, valuable layer of its own.
