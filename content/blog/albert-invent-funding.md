**Albert Invent raised $55 million in a Series B round at a $340 million valuation**, announced on October 13, 2026 and led by **Technology Crossover Ventures (TCV)**. The round is one of the clearer signals yet that domain-specific AI — not just general-purpose chatbots — is finding real, paying enterprise demand.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why a roster of Fortune 500 chemical companies is lining up behind it.

## What is Albert Invent?

Albert Invent builds **Valence AI**, a chemistry-native AI model trained on **15 million molecules and the complete chemical patent corpus**. The product is designed to speed up product development in materials science and chemical engineering — fields where formulating a new polymer, coating, or battery chemistry can take years of trial and error.

Founded in **2022** by **Nick Talken, Ken Kisner, Neelesh Vaikhary and Zack Kisner**, the company counts **BASF, Henkel, Clorox, Kenvue, AkzoNobel, Avient, Chemours, Nouryon, Solenis, Keystone Industries and Applied Molecules** among its customers — a list dominated by large, established industrial chemical companies rather than startups.

## The raise: $55M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $55 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $340 million
- **Announced:** October 13, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Technology Crossover Ventures (TCV)
- **Co-leads:** Delta-v Capital, Socium Ventures

For context, Albert Invent's previous round was a **$22.5 million Series A in December 2024**, led by Coatue, which followed a **$7.5 million seed round in June 2023**, led by Index Ventures. Including this Series B, the company has raised **$85 million** in total — Coatue and Index Ventures both returned as participants in this round after leading earlier ones.

## Who invested in Albert Invent?

The round was led by **Technology Crossover Ventures (TCV)**, with **Delta-v Capital** and **Socium Ventures** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **HighSage Ventures**
- **Coral Capital**
- **Legion VC**
- **Coatue**
- **J.P. Morgan Growth Equity**
- **Index Ventures**
- **Applied Ventures**

The presence of J.P. Morgan Growth Equity alongside more traditional venture names suggests growing institutional confidence in the durability of Albert Invent's enterprise contracts.

## What Albert Invent will do with the money

Albert Invent plans to use the funding to **scale Valence AI's model capabilities and expand its team** to serve the global chemicals market, where development timelines for new formulations and materials remain slow and expensive.

That's the core bottleneck Albert Invent is targeting: traditional chemical R&D cycles are measured in years, and even incremental improvements in formulation speed can translate into meaningful competitive advantage for its customers.

## Why it matters

1. **Domain-specific AI is outperforming general-purpose tools in narrow, technical fields.** Albert Invent says Valence AI can outperform general AI tools by 45x on chemistry tasks — a reminder that as the market matures, specialized models trained on proprietary, hard-to-replicate data may carve out durable advantages, much like the broader wave of [AI funding rounds tracked across 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026) increasingly reward vertical depth over breadth.
2. **Enterprise adoption is moving past pilots.** A customer list this dense with Fortune 500 chemical manufacturers — not just research labs — suggests chemical companies are putting real production workflows on the line, not just experimenting.
3. **Patent-aware AI is a genuine moat in regulated, IP-heavy industries.** Training on the complete chemical patent corpus lets Valence AI reason about prior art and constraints that a general model wouldn't know to check, which is exactly the kind of problem domain-specific [AI agents](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) are increasingly built to solve.
