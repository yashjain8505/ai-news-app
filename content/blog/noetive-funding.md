**Noetive raised $41 million in a seed round led by Eclipse, with Craft Ventures co-leading**, announced on September 19, 2026. The company, founded in 2026, emerged from stealth targeting one of the least glamorous but largest corners of the economy: the physical operations behind manufacturing, logistics, construction and energy.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why industrial AI is drawing this kind of seed check.

## What is Noetive?

Noetive builds an **AI platform for manufacturing, logistics, construction, energy and industrial operations**. Founded in **2026**, the company is going after what it describes as the unglamorous but vast physical economy — the factories, supply chains and infrastructure systems that power everything else, but that have gotten far less AI investment than consumer software or enterprise SaaS.

No specific founders or named customers were disclosed alongside the round. The industrial operations Noetive is targeting share a common trait: they generate enormous amounts of operational data — sensor readings, logistics schedules, equipment logs — that has historically been too messy and disconnected for software to act on directly. Applying AI to interpret and act on that data, rather than just dashboard it for a human to read, is the underlying bet.

## The raise: $41M seed

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $41 million
- **Round:** Seed
- **Valuation:** not disclosed
- **Announced:** September 19, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Eclipse
- **Co-lead:** Craft Ventures

No prior rounds were disclosed. A $41 million seed round — with two distinct lead investors rather than one — is a sizable bet for a company still at the earliest institutional funding stage, and it lands just as investor attention broadens from software-layer AI toward the physical operations that software alone can't automate. For comparison, most seed rounds close well under $10 million; a check this size at this stage typically reflects either an unusually experienced team, unusually strong early customer signal, or both.

## Who invested in Noetive?

The round was led by **Eclipse**, with **Craft Ventures** co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed.

## What Noetive will do with the money

Noetive says the funding will go toward **hiring, product development, and deploying its platform with design partners** across industrial verticals. The mention of existing design partners suggests the company already has real operators testing the platform on live industrial problems, rather than building in isolation before any market contact.

Design partnerships are especially important in industrial AI, where systems have to integrate with legacy equipment, existing operational workflows, and safety requirements that don't exist in consumer software. A platform that only works in a demo but breaks against a real factory floor's constraints isn't sellable, so early, hands-on deployment with operators is how a company like Noetive proves the product actually holds up.

## Why it matters

1. **Physical AI is pulling capital away from pure software plays.** Manufacturing floors, supply chains and energy infrastructure involve messy, high-stakes, real-world variables that are harder to model than a chat interface — and a $41 million seed suggests investors think the payoff for solving them is worth the extra difficulty. That's the same thesis behind rounds like [Walden Robotics' $300 million seed](/blog/walden-robotics-funding).
2. **Two co-leads on a seed round is a signal of investor conviction.** Eclipse and Craft Ventures both committing to lead, rather than one firm leading with the other participating, points to competitive interest in getting into the round early.
3. **The industrial AI category is still being defined.** Noetive spans manufacturing, logistics, construction and energy rather than picking one vertical, which is either a sign of platform ambition or a signal the company hasn't yet found its sharpest wedge — something later rounds, and disclosed customer names, will clarify.
