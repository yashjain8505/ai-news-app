**Prometheus raised $12 billion in a Series B round at a $41 billion valuation**, announced July 9, 2026 and led by **JPMorgan, BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, DST Global and Arch Venture Partners**. Barely a year after coming out of stealth, the company has become one of the most richly valued AI startups outside the large language model race — by aiming at physical engineering instead of software.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and why investors are betting so heavily on AI that designs hardware.

## What is Prometheus?

Prometheus is building what it calls **artificial general engineers** — AI systems designed to automate the design and manufacture of complex physical products, from jet engines to medical devices to drug compounds. Founded in **2024** by **Vik Bajaj**, the company is pursuing a different bet than most of the AI industry: instead of generating text, code or images, it wants AI to compress the slow, expensive cycle of engineering physical things.

In plain terms, designing a jet engine or a new drug molecule today involves years of iteration, simulation and physical testing. Prometheus is trying to build AI that can navigate that design space computationally, cutting down the time and cost between an idea and a manufactured product.

## The raise: $12B Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $12 billion
- **Round:** Series B
- **Valuation:** $41 billion
- **Announced:** July 9, 2026
- **Lead investors:** JPMorgan, BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, DST Global, Arch Venture Partners

Prometheus's Series A was $6.2 billion, led by Jeff Bezos in late 2025. Going from a $6.2 billion round to a $41 billion valuation in roughly seven months is an extraordinary climb for a company that is not yet two years old — and it puts Prometheus in the rarefied company of AI startups valued in the tens of billions before shipping a mass-market product.

## Who invested in Prometheus?

The Series B was led by **JPMorgan**, **BlackRock**, **Goldman Sachs**, **DST Global** and **Arch Venture Partners**. **Jeff Bezos** also participated, continuing his backing from the Series A.

The lineup is notable for how much of it comes from traditional finance rather than venture capital — JPMorgan, BlackRock and Goldman Sachs are not typical early-stage AI investors, and their presence signals that Prometheus is being underwritten more like an infrastructure or industrial bet than a software startup.

## What Prometheus will do with the money

Prometheus says the capital will go toward **scaling infrastructure, talent acquisition, and bringing AI-designed products toward commercialization**. Unlike a software company that can ship updates instantly, moving AI-designed hardware and drug compounds from computational design to physical manufacturing requires significant capital for compute, engineering talent and, eventually, production capacity.

## Why it matters

1. **The AI industry's next frontier may be physical, not linguistic.** While most frontier labs compete on language and coding models, Prometheus is targeting jet engines, medical devices and drug compounds — a much slower feedback loop, but one with potentially larger real-world payoffs if computational design actually works at scale. Software iteration cycles are measured in days; physical product cycles are measured in years, which makes Prometheus's bet a much longer and more capital-intensive one than a typical AI startup's.
2. **The valuation trajectory is among the steepest in AI.** A jump from $6.2 billion to $41 billion in about seven months puts Prometheus alongside the fastest-appreciating companies tracked in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), even though it has no consumer product to point to. That kind of appreciation, absent a shipped product, is a bet on the team and the underlying thesis more than on demonstrated commercial traction.
3. **Bezos' continued backing is a signal in itself.** Investors returning for a second round at a much higher price suggests conviction that AI-driven physical design is a durable category, not just a headline-grabbing pitch — a theme also playing out across [physical AI and robotics funding in 2026](/blog/physical-ai-robotics-funding-2026), where investors are increasingly willing to fund AI applied to hardware and manufacturing rather than only software.

Whether Prometheus can convert that capital into an actual manufactured product — a working jet engine component or an approved drug compound — is the open question. Engineering and pharmaceutical products face regulatory approval, physical testing, and manufacturing constraints that no amount of AI-driven design can shortcut entirely. The $12 billion Series B buys Prometheus time and talent, but the company's real test is still ahead: proving that AI-designed physical products can clear the same bars that traditionally engineered ones do.
