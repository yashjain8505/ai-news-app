**Pigment raised $250 million at a $2.3 billion valuation**, in a round reported October 20, 2026. The lead investor hasn't been specified, but the number itself is the headline: a European company built around enterprise financial planning is now valued well into unicorn-plus territory, competing in a category long dominated by Silicon Valley.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and what it says about enterprise AI.

## What is Pigment?

Pigment is an **AI-driven business planning and financial forecasting platform** that helps companies model scenarios, forecast finances, and automate the generation of slide decks and analyses. Founded in **2018**, the company sells into **mid-market and large enterprises**, where finance and operations teams rely on it to replace or augment spreadsheet-heavy planning processes.

## The raise: $250M Series D (implied)

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $250 million
- **Round:** Series D (implied)
- **Valuation:** $2.3 billion
- **Announced:** October 20, 2026
- **Lead investor:** not specified

Pigment has raised multiple earlier rounds, though its profitability trajectory has not been disclosed. What's notable here is less the round's mechanics and more its size relative to Pigment's category: enterprise planning software is not a flashy AI niche, yet it's commanding a multi-billion-dollar valuation.

## Who invested in Pigment?

The lead investor for this round has not been specified in available reporting, and no co-leads or additional participants have been disclosed.

## What Pigment will do with the money

Pigment says the funding will go toward **product expansion, sales acceleration, and geographic growth** — the standard playbook for a company trying to convert a strong product into a larger share of the enterprise planning market.

## Why it matters

1. **Enterprise finance data is a natural fit for AI.** Budgeting, forecasting, and reporting involve exactly the kind of structured, repetitive data work that AI automation delivers clear, measurable productivity gains on — a more boring but arguably more durable use case than consumer chatbots.
2. **A European company competing at this valuation is notable.** Pigment scaling to $2.3 billion from outside the usual Silicon Valley cluster, in a category tracked alongside other large rounds in [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), suggests deep domain knowledge of how enterprises actually run can compete with raw AI horsepower.
3. **It reflects continued investor appetite for vertical enterprise AI.** Rounds like this show capital still flowing into applied, workflow-specific AI products, not just general-purpose model makers.
