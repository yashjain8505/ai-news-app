**Emergent raised $130 million in a Series C round at a $1.5 billion valuation**, announced on July 15, 2026 and led by **Creaegis** and **Khosla Ventures**. The round makes Emergent a unicorn just over a year after launch — one of the fastest climbs to that status in the AI coding category.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI coding tools.

## What is Emergent?

Emergent is an **Indian AI coding platform** founded in **2025** by **Mukund Jha** and **Madhav Jha**. It generates **full-stack applications from plain-language descriptions**, letting users build working software without writing code directly.

In plain terms: a user describes what they want an application to do in ordinary language, and Emergent's platform handles the work of turning that description into a functioning, full-stack app — front end, back end and all — rather than just generating snippets of code that still need to be assembled by a developer.

In just over a year, the platform has reached **1.5 to 2 million monthly active users**, **more than 200,000 paying customers**, and has been used to build **more than 12 million apps**, according to the company.

## The raise: $130M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $130 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $1.5 billion
- **Announced:** July 15, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Creaegis, Khosla Ventures
- **Co-leads:** SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Lightspeed

Emergent's **Series B was $300 million in valuation in January 2026**. Crossing into unicorn territory at $1.5 billion just six months later, and roughly a year after the company launched, is among the fastest paths to a $1 billion-plus valuation in the AI coding category to date. That pace — from launch to unicorn in about 12 months — compresses a timeline that has historically taken AI startups two to three years, even in a funding environment where valuations have generally moved faster than in prior tech cycles.

## Who invested in Emergent?

The round was led by **Creaegis** and **Khosla Ventures**, with **SoftBank Vision Fund 2** and **Lightspeed** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Y Combinator**

## What Emergent will do with the money

Emergent said the funding will go toward **platform scaling** and **global expansion beyond India**, building on a customer base that already skews heavily international.

That international focus matters: the company says **70% of its revenue** comes from **North America and Europe**, so this raise is about deepening a global footprint it has already established rather than building one from scratch. With **1.5 to 2 million monthly active users** and **more than 12 million apps built** on the platform already, the funding is less about proving product-market fit and more about scaling infrastructure and go-to-market to match demand that already exists.

## Why it matters

Emergent's round is a notable data point for where AI coding investment is heading in 2026:

1. **Breakout AI companies no longer need to start in Silicon Valley.** Emergent's India-based founding, combined with the bulk of its revenue coming from North America and Europe, challenges the assumption that category-defining AI startups must originate in the US.
2. **The AI coding category is still minting unicorns at speed.** Hitting unicorn status on $120 million in annualized recurring revenue about a year after launch is among the fastest climbs in the category, and it puts Emergent in the same conversation as other fast-scaling AI coding platforms like [Lovable](/blog/lovable-funding).
3. **Capital is still chasing AI agent-adjacent tools aggressively.** Emergent's raise adds to a broader wave of 2026 funding into AI agents and coding assistants tracked in Wortins' [AI agents funding roundup](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026).
4. **Revenue, not just user counts, is driving the fastest valuations.** Reaching unicorn status on $120 million in annualized recurring revenue — rather than pre-revenue hype — suggests investors are increasingly demanding real monetization evidence even from fast-growing AI coding startups, not just usage growth.
