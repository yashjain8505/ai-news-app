**Instinct raised $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation**, announced August 26, 2026 and led by **Index Ventures and Benchmark**. The company is still in private beta, which makes the size of the round — and the valuation behind it — a striking bet on consumer AI agents that actually take action rather than just answer questions.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who's behind it, and why investors are moving this fast on an unreleased product.

## What is Instinct?

Instinct is an **AI life-management assistant** that helps users plan travel, buy groceries, book tickets and cancel subscriptions through text and voice interfaces. Founded in **2025** by **Noah Shinn**, the company is still in private beta — it hasn't launched a public product yet, but has reportedly spread by word-of-mouth among early users. No customer or usage numbers were disclosed.

## The raise: $350M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $350 million
- **Round:** Series B (total)
- **Valuation:** $2.5 billion
- **Announced:** August 26, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Index Ventures, Benchmark

Instinct's only prior fundraising was a **seed round with undisclosed terms**, meaning this Series B represents a significant, and rapid, step up in both capital raised and headline valuation for a company that hasn't yet moved out of private beta.

## Who invested in Instinct?

**Index Ventures** and **Benchmark** led the round:

- **Index Ventures**
- **Benchmark**

No co-leads or additional participants were named alongside the two lead investors.

## What Instinct will do with the money

Instinct said the funding will go toward **product expansion, model development, and market growth** to move the company beyond private beta and establish itself as a daily-use utility rather than a niche assistant.

## Why it matters

Instinct's raise is worth watching for a few reasons:

1. **Consumer AI agents are being priced as infrastructure, not novelties.** A $2.5 billion valuation for a still-in-beta assistant shows investors betting that agentic AI is moving from demo to daily dependency — see our [AI agents funding roundup](/blog/ai-agents-funding-2026) for how this compares to other bets in the category.
2. **The founder story is doing real work here.** Noah Shinn built Instinct's early traction through word-of-mouth alone, without a public launch — a growth pattern investors are rewarding heavily in this cycle.
3. **The hard part is still ahead.** An assistant that touches real calendars, real purchases and real subscriptions has to be reliably trustworthy at scale, and that trust is unproven outside of a private beta. Instinct's round is also a data point in the broader wave of outsized 2026 AI rounds tracked in our [biggest funding rounds roundup](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
