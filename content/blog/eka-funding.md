**Eka Robotics raised $150 million at a $1.45 billion valuation**, in a round reported October 20, 2026 and said to be led by **Google**. The money is going toward a problem that has quietly stumped robotics for decades: building an arm that can pick up things it has never seen before, without being painstakingly programmed for each one.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise and why generalized grasping is suddenly commanding billion-dollar valuations.

## What is Eka Robotics?

Eka Robotics builds an **AI-powered robotic arm** capable of grasping and manipulating a wide variety of unfamiliar objects smoothly, with dexterity that approaches human-level performance. Details on the company's founding date, founders, and named customers have not been disclosed.

In plain terms: most industrial robot arms are precise but brittle — they're tuned to repeat one exact motion on one exact object, and they break down the moment something is slightly different. Eka's arm is built to handle messy, varied, real-world objects without bespoke programming for each one.

## The raise: $150M Series B (implied)

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $150 million
- **Round:** Series B (implied)
- **Valuation:** $1.45 billion
- **Announced:** October 20, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Google (reported)

Eka has raised earlier rounds, though valuation details from those rounds have not been disclosed, making it hard to size up exactly how much the valuation has grown. What is clear is that a $1.45 billion price tag puts Eka among the more richly valued robotics startups of 2026.

## Who invested in Eka Robotics?

**Google** is reported to have led the round. No co-leads or additional participants have been disclosed, and the company's investor list has not been officially confirmed.

## What Eka Robotics will do with the money

The funding is earmarked for **scaling production and deployment** of the dexterous robotic arm — moving the technology from demonstrations and pilots toward real-world deployment at volume.

## Why it matters

1. **Generalized grasping is one of robotics' hardest unsolved problems.** Factory arms today are precise but inflexible; an arm that can reliably handle unfamiliar objects unlocks warehousing, fulfillment, and potentially home automation — markets that have been waiting on exactly this capability.
2. **Big Tech is placing direct bets on physical AI.** Google's reported involvement signals that the largest AI companies see robotics as a critical next frontier, not just a side project — consistent with the broader wave of capital tracked across [the biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **The valuation is a bet on commercialization, not just demos.** A $1.45 billion price tag implies investors believe this specific dexterity breakthrough will translate into deployable, revenue-generating robots rather than remaining a lab curiosity.
