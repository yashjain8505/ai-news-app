**Volantis raised $88 million in a Series A round**, announced October 3, 2026 and led by **Lachy Groom**, with **Abstract Ventures** co-leading. No valuation was disclosed, but the backing itself is notable: this is one of a small handful of startups betting that light, not electrons, is the way to break AI's next big bottleneck — memory bandwidth.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why it matters for the future of AI inference.

## What is Volantis?

Volantis builds **photonic interconnects using VCSEL lasers** to link AI chips with memory. The pitch is that moving data between a chip and its memory — not raw compute — is increasingly what limits how fast and how cheaply AI inference can run at scale. By replacing electrical connections with light-based ones, Volantis aims to widen that bottleneck.

The company was founded in **2022** by **Tapa Ghosh**, a Thiel Fellow and former Y Combinator participant, and **Roy Meade**, who previously led Micron's HBM (high-bandwidth memory) program. No customers were disclosed.

## The raise: $88M Series A

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $88 million
- **Round:** Series A
- **Valuation:** Not disclosed
- **Announced:** October 3, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Lachy Groom
- **Co-lead:** Abstract Ventures

Volantis previously raised a **$9 million seed round in June 2025**, led by **Alex Wang** of Scale AI and **Trevor Blackwell** of Y Combinator, with early backing from **Sam Altman**. Going from a $9 million seed to an $88 million Series A — roughly a 10x jump in check size — in just over a year signals strong investor conviction in the technical bet, even without a disclosed valuation to put a number on it.

## Who invested in Volantis?

The Series A was led by **Lachy Groom**, with **Abstract Ventures** co-leading. No additional participants were disclosed for this round, though the company's seed round carried backing from Alex Wang, Trevor Blackwell, and Sam Altman.

## What Volantis will do with the money

Volantis plans to use the funding to **build photonic memory production at scale, develop its first inference engines (which it expects to ship in 2027), and expand its engineering and supply chain teams**. That timeline — shipping product in 2027 — is an aggressive one for a hardware company working with a relatively novel interconnect technology.

## Why it matters

1. **Memory, not compute, is now the binding constraint on AI.** Volantis is one of only a few startups betting specifically on photonics — using light instead of electrical signals — to solve the data-transfer bottleneck between AI chips and memory, a problem that gets more expensive to ignore as inference workloads scale.
2. **The timing lines up with a bigger supply chain shift.** Volantis's 2027 shipping target coincides with new indium phosphide fab capacity coming online in Europe, and the company's choice of gallium arsenide is notable given how concentrated global indium phosphide production currently is — a geopolitical angle layered on top of a technical one.
3. **It's part of a deeper wave of hardware bets in AI's infrastructure stack.** Alongside other chip and interconnect startups tracked in [AI chip startup funding in 2026](/blog/ai-chip-startup-funding-2026) and the broader [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), Volantis represents the deeper, more technical end of where AI infrastructure capital is flowing this year.
