**Databricks raised $3 billion in a strategic round at a $188 billion valuation**, announced on July 17, 2026 and led by **Coatue**. The round arrives just five months after the company was last valued at $134 billion, one of the fastest valuation climbs of any private company this year.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for the enterprise AI market.

## What is Databricks?

Databricks is an **enterprise data platform** that combines data warehousing with AI governance and agent-building tools, giving companies a single place to store their data and build AI on top of it. Founded in **2013** by **Ali Ghodsi, Matei Zaharia, Reynold Xin and Michael Armbrust**, the company grew out of the team behind the open-source Apache Spark project at UC Berkeley.

In plain terms: most large companies have their data scattered across warehouses, lakes and siloed systems, and building reliable AI on top of that mess is hard. Databricks sells the layer that unifies the data and adds the governance and tooling needed to build AI agents on it safely. Its customers include **Shell, Bloomberg, Spotify and Microsoft**.

## The raise: $3B Strategic Round

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $3 billion
- **Round:** Strategic round
- **Valuation:** $188 billion
- **Announced:** July 17, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Coatue
- **Participants:** Lightspeed, NVIDIA

Databricks has raised money at an unusually steep pace since 2022. It closed a **$450 million Series D at a $40 billion valuation** in January 2022, then a **$1.2 billion Series E at a $43 billion valuation** in August 2023. From there, the valuation accelerated sharply — reaching **$134 billion by February 2026**, and now **$188 billion** just five months later. That $54 billion jump in five months is a scale of appreciation typically reserved for the very top tier of AI infrastructure companies.

## Who invested in Databricks?

The round was led by **Coatue**, with participation from:

- **Lightspeed**
- **NVIDIA**

NVIDIA's continued participation is notable — the chipmaker has been a recurring strategic investor in the AI infrastructure companies it depends on to drive demand for its hardware, and its stake in Databricks ties the two companies' growth stories together.

## What Databricks will do with the money

Databricks is directing the capital toward a set of AI-agent products it's been rolling out: the **Unity AI Gateway** for governing AI usage across an organization, **Genie**, an AI coworker for querying and reasoning over enterprise data, and **Lakebase**, a serverless Postgres offering built for AI agents that need transactional data access. The company is also putting money behind **go-to-market expansion** to get these products in front of more enterprise customers.

That combination — governance, an AI assistant, and agent-ready infrastructure — reflects Databricks' bet that the next phase of enterprise AI spending won't go to whoever has the best model, but to whoever can make AI safe and reliable to run against a company's actual, messy data.

## Why it matters

1. **Data infrastructure is becoming the AI moat.** Investors are increasingly betting on companies that embed AI into existing enterprise data systems rather than standalone model developers — a trend also visible across the [biggest AI funding rounds of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026), where infrastructure plays are outpacing pure model companies.
2. **The valuation curve keeps steepening.** Going from $43 billion in 2023 to $188 billion in mid-2026 shows how much capital is chasing companies that have already proven enterprise adoption at scale, rather than earlier-stage bets.
3. **Strategic investors are doubling down on infrastructure they depend on.** NVIDIA's participation echoes a broader pattern seen in [AI data center funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026), where chipmakers and cloud players are investing directly in the software and infrastructure companies that drive demand for their underlying hardware.

For anyone tracking where the largest sums in AI are landing in 2026, Databricks is a clear signal: the money is following companies that can turn a company's own data into a governed, agent-ready foundation for AI.
