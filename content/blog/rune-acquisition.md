**Salesforce has agreed to acquire Rune Technologies for $375 million**, announced October 29, 2026, in what's being described as Salesforce's most significant national-security acquisition to date. Rune's edge-native logistics software will be folded into Salesforce's Missionforce platform for defense and military customers.

Here's the full breakdown of the deal, what Rune does, and why Salesforce wants a battlefield logistics startup.

## What is Rune?

Rune builds **TyrOS**, an edge-native logistics platform for the U.S. military and allied forces. The software forecasts supply needs and optimizes resource allocation, and — critically — keeps working **offline**, letting military units make logistics decisions without a live network connection. Founded in **2019**, Rune counts the **U.S. Department of Defense** among its customers and had previously raised **$30 million from Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)** and other investors before this deal.

## The deal: $375M acquisition

The headline numbers:

- **Price:** $375 million
- **Acquirer:** Salesforce
- **Announced:** October 29, 2026
- **Prior funding:** $30 million raised from Andreessen Horowitz and others

The acquisition price is more than 12 times what Rune had raised in venture funding, a sizable multiple that reflects the premium acquirers are willing to pay for defense-tech companies that already have a foothold inside the Pentagon rather than building that access from scratch.

## Who's behind the deal?

The sole acquirer is **Salesforce**. No co-investors or additional parties were named in the acquisition.

## What happens to Rune

Rune is being integrated into **Salesforce Missionforce** as its tactical edge logistics solution, extending Missionforce's reach from software for defense back-office and planning functions down to frontline logistics decisions that have to work without connectivity.

## Why it matters

1. **AI and defense procurement keep converging.** This is Salesforce's biggest move yet into national security software, part of a broader trend of enterprise software companies buying into defense tech rather than building it in-house — a pattern visible across [the biggest AI acquisitions of 2026](/blog/biggest-ai-acquisitions-2026).
2. **Edge computing is the differentiator, not just AI.** Rune's value isn't a flashy model — it's the ability to forecast and optimize supply chains in environments with no connectivity, a capability that matters more on a battlefield than raw model quality.
3. **Defense tech M&A is picking up alongside defense tech funding.** Rune's exit follows a wave of venture dollars moving into [AI defense tech funding in 2026](/blog/ai-defense-tech-funding-2026) — acquisitions like this are the natural next step once that funding matures into real Pentagon deployments.
