**Neko Health raised $700 million in a Series C round at a $7 billion valuation**, announced on July 15, 2026 and led by **Lightspeed Venture Partners** and **O.G. Venture Partners**. The raise roughly quadruples the company's valuation from its last round, and it comes as consumer demand for AI-driven preventive health screening keeps climbing.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and what it means for AI in preventive medicine.

## What is Neko Health?

Neko Health is an **AI-powered preventive health company** founded by **Daniel Ek**, the Spotify co-founder. It runs **full-body scanning clinics** that offer **radiation-free assessments** combined with **AI-assisted analysis**, aiming to catch health issues earlier than traditional periodic checkups.

In plain terms: instead of visiting a doctor only when something feels wrong, or relying on standard annual checkups that screen for a narrow set of issues, patients visit a Neko Health clinic for a comprehensive scan that uses AI to flag anomalies across the body — the kind of broad, proactive screening that's traditionally been expensive or logistically difficult to offer at scale.

The company has already built significant consumer demand: **more than 100,000 completed scans** and **more than 350,000 people on its waitlist**, according to the company.

## The raise: $700M Series C

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $700 million
- **Round:** Series C
- **Valuation:** $7 billion
- **Announced:** July 15, 2026
- **Lead investors:** Lightspeed Venture Partners, O.G. Venture Partners
- **Co-leads:** Atomico, General Catalyst, Lakestar

Neko Health's **Series B was $1.7 billion in valuation in early 2025**. Reaching **$7 billion** in this Series C is roughly a **4x increase**, one of the steepest valuation climbs among AI-driven health companies this year. That kind of jump in roughly 18 months is more typical of AI infrastructure or model companies than of a healthcare startup that depends on physically building and staffing clinics — a business with real operational constraints that pure software companies don't face.

## Who invested in Neko Health?

The round was led by **Lightspeed Venture Partners** and **O.G. Venture Partners**, with **Atomico**, **General Catalyst** and **Lakestar** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **Mark Zuckerberg**
- **will.i.am**

Notable individual backers alongside the institutional investors underscore the level of high-profile interest the company has attracted beyond typical venture circles. Having Mark Zuckerberg as a participant is also a useful signal in itself — tech executives with the means to invest anywhere are choosing to put money behind a physical, clinic-based health company rather than another purely digital AI product.

## What Neko Health will do with the money

Neko Health said the funding will support **US expansion**, including opening clinics in **New York** and other cities, along with development of **next-generation hardware** for its scanning platform.

That expansion plan follows directly from demand: a waitlist of 350,000-plus people is far larger than the company's current clinic network can serve, and US expansion is the most direct way to close that gap. Unlike a software product that can scale by adding servers, Neko Health's growth is bottlenecked by physical clinics and scanning hardware — which is likely why a portion of this round is earmarked specifically for next-generation hardware rather than purely for marketing or headcount.

## Why it matters

Neko Health's round is a useful signal for where AI-driven healthcare investment is heading in 2026:

1. **AI plus consumer-friendly execution may finally make preventive screening work.** Preventive health screening has faced skepticism for decades over cost and unclear benefit, but Neko's waitlist and completed-scan numbers suggest the model is resonating with consumers now.
2. **A 4x valuation jump in about 18 months shows investor conviction, not just hype.** Going from $1.7 billion to $7 billion is the kind of jump usually reserved for AI infrastructure companies, not consumer health clinics — a sign investors see this as a category-defining bet, one of the larger 2026 rounds tracked in Wortins' [biggest AI funding rounds roundup](/blog/biggest-ai-funding-rounds-2026).
3. **Founder-led ventures from prior tech successes keep attracting outsized capital.** Daniel Ek's track record building Spotify appears to be giving investors confidence to back a capital-intensive, physical-clinic business model that would likely face more skepticism under a first-time founder.
