**Salience Labs raised $125 million in a Series B round**, announced on October 14, 2026 and led by **Bullhound Capital**, with **AMD Ventures and Intel Capital** co-leading. Two of the biggest chipmakers in AI backing the same optical photonics startup is not a coincidence — it's a signal about where the next data center bottleneck is.

Here's the full breakdown of the raise, who backed it, and why the GPU networking layer is suddenly getting so much capital.

## What is Salience Labs?

Salience Labs builds **silicon photonic optical circuit switches** for AI data center interconnects. The technology lets data centers connect thousands of GPUs directly over light, with sub-microsecond latency, instead of routing traffic through conventional electronic packet switches.

Founded in **2021**, the company was started by **Vaysh Kewada, Dr. Johannes Feldmann, Prof. Harish Bhaskaran and Prof. Wolfram Pernice** — a team with deep roots in Oxford photonics research. The pitch is straightforward: as AI training and inference clusters scale into the tens of thousands of GPUs, moving data between them fast enough, without burning excessive power, becomes its own engineering crisis.

## The raise: $125M Series B

The headline numbers:

- **Amount:** $125 million
- **Round:** Series B
- **Announced:** October 14, 2026
- **Lead investor:** Bullhound Capital
- **Co-leads:** AMD Ventures, Intel Capital

Salience Labs didn't disclose a valuation for this round. For context, it previously raised a **$30 million Series A in February 2025**, led by ICM HPQC Fund and Applied Ventures, and an **$11.5 million seed round in May 2022**, led by Cambridge Innovation Capital and Oxford Science Enterprises. With the Series B, the company has now raised **$166.5 million** in total — a roughly 4x jump in Series round size from A to B.

## Who invested in Salience Labs?

The round was led by **Bullhound Capital**, with **AMD Ventures** and **Intel Capital** co-leading. Additional participants included:

- **British Business Bank**
- **SBI Group**
- **Hudson River Trading**
- **Applied Ventures**
- **Oxford Science Enterprises**
- **Cambridge Innovation Capital**
- **Silicon Catalyst**

Oxford Science Enterprises, Cambridge Innovation Capital and Applied Ventures all returned from earlier rounds, while AMD Ventures and Intel Capital are new names — notable given the two firms are normally fierce competitors in the chip market, yet both are now financially exposed to the same networking startup.

## What Salience Labs will do with the money

Salience Labs plans to use the funding to **accelerate deployment of its optical circuit switching technology, scale volume manufacturing operations, expand its U.S. presence in Silicon Valley, and fund R&D on higher-density 128-port and 256-port switches**.

That roadmap tracks with where the market is headed: as GPU clusters grow, the switches connecting them need to support more ports without adding latency or power draw, which is the core engineering challenge Salience is racing to solve before competitors catch up.

## Why it matters

1. **Both AMD and Intel are now backing the same photonics startup.** When two rival chipmakers invest in the same interconnect vendor, it's a strong signal that the industry sees optical circuit switching as foundational infrastructure for AI data centers — not a niche bet, similar to how [Etched's custom AI chip](/blog/etched-funding) has drawn backing from investors betting on the next layer of AI hardware economics.
2. **The networking bottleneck is becoming as urgent as the compute bottleneck.** GPUs have gotten much faster, but moving data between thousands of them hasn't kept pace — Salience's pitch of eliminating optical-to-electrical-to-optical conversions directly targets that gap, with claimed power savings of 40-41% versus traditional packet switching.
3. **This threatens incumbent networking vendors.** If optical circuit switching scales as promised, it represents a structural threat to packet-switching vendors like Arista in a market Salience says could exceed $8 billion by 2030 — the kind of disruption that tends to attract more [AI data center infrastructure funding](/blog/ai-data-center-funding-2026) as the category matures.
